# Regression to the Mean Girls

The NFL opens on a Wednesday this year -- September 9, Seahawks hosting the Patriots in a Super Bowl LX rematch -- and on Wednesdays, we wear pink. Fitting, because August is when the league's narrative machine hits full production capacity. Every camp report is a coronation, every 7-on-7 clip is a franchise turning point, and every talking head is trying to make fetch happen.

So here's the season preview, Merrittocracy style: five storylines the discourse has already decided, and what the instruments actually say about each one. Spoiler: the answer is usually regression. The mean is undefeated.

## Clubber Lang Has the Belt Now

The Chiefs went 6-11. First losing season in Andy Reid's thirteen years, first January at home since 2014, nine straight division titles gone. The eulogies wrote themselves.

Here's what the eulogies skipped: the 2024 Chiefs went 10-0 in one-score games, an NFL record. The 2025 Chiefs started 0-5 in them and had dropped seven by mid-December. That's not a dynasty dying. That's a coin that landed heads ten straight times deciding to land tails. Then Patrick Mahomes tore his ACL and LCL in Week 15, which turned a variance problem into a funeral.

The rebound case: Mahomes was cleared for practice at the start of camp, seven months post-surgery, targeting Week 1 and promising to run less. He can afford to, because Kansas City signed Kenneth Walker III -- the reigning Super Bowl MVP, poached from the champs. They traded up to No. 6 for Mansoor Delane to replace the traded Trent McDuffie, and Travis Kelce came back at 36 after leading the team with 851 receiving yards during his own farewell tour.

The catch: the belt moved. Denver dragged New England to 10-7 in the AFC title game, and the Chargers landed the shot that eliminated KC. The division the Chiefs used to inherit by Thanksgiving is now something they have to take. I think they take it, but the one-score record regressing upward is doing more work in that sentence than anything Reid draws up.

## How Do You Like Them Apples?

Drake Maye is 23, played in a Super Bowl, and spent last season being anointed by everyone with a microphone and a Boston zip code. Some of it is earned. A lot of it is a schedule of outliers.

The Patriots became the first team in NFL history to win nine road games in a season. Historic outliers are, by definition, the thing least likely to happen again. Their AFC Championship win was a 10-7 rock fight that Christian Gonzalez and the defense dragged home, and the Super Bowl ended 29-13 with Maye throwing a fourth-quarter interception to Julian Love.

None of this makes Maye a fraud. The trajectory is real, the arm is real, Vrabel's roster is young and ascending. But "arrival" and "historically fortunate season" can both be true, and the market is only pricing one of them. When New England wins 11 instead of 13 and the sky starts falling on sports radio, remember which part was the outlier. It wasn't the quarterback.

## Wild Thing, You Make My Heart Sing

The Browns think Shedeur Sanders is the answer, and the league's content economy agrees so hard that a quarterback with an 18.8 Total QBR -- the worst in Cleveland franchise history among anyone with six starts, which for this franchise is genuinely saying something -- made the Pro Bowl Games. Franchise-worst QBR. Pro Bowl. No cleaner measurement exists of the gap between narrative production and football production.

The actual rookie file: seven starts, 3-4, 1,400 yards, seven touchdowns, ten picks. The 3-4 was the best record of any Browns starter, and the Titans game -- 393 total yards, four touchdowns -- is a real data point in his favor. The 31-3 Bears game with three interceptions is a real one in the other direction. He is the full Ricky Vaughn experience: electric, erratic, beloved in Cleveland before the control shows up.

Now Todd Monken runs the show, the Browns spent first-rounders on Spencer Fano and KC Concepcion to build the case around him, and his "competition" is Deshaun Watson, whose 33.1 QBR since arriving in Cleveland is the lowest among qualified passers. The question isn't whether Shedeur beats that bar. It's whether the year-two leap everyone has already scripted has any instruments behind it, or whether we're all just watching the wild thing and singing along.

## They Got Capone on Taxes

In April, our draft model liked Kenyon Sadiq at +0.169, one of the cleaner tight end profiles in the class. Then the Jets picked him and the projection flipped to -0.08. Omar Cooper Jr. came in at -0.11. Same culprit both times: New York's two-year player retention rate of 0.161, the feature that measures whether an organization keeps its investments long enough to cash them. The model didn't dock the players. It docked the address.

That was an April opinion. Now it's a falsifiable one. Geno Smith arrives via the Raiders to stabilize the position, Frank Reich is calling plays, and Aaron Glenn's second year is a referendum on a 15-year playoff drought, the longest in major American sports. If Sadiq and Cooper produce like their player-level profiles say they should, the organizational tax takes a public L and we will print it in this space. If they disappear into the same fog that swallowed a decade of Jets first-rounders, the tax was real. Either way, this is the out-of-sample test, and we don't get to grade our own homework quietly.

## Mr. Glass

The 49ers' six highest-paid players missed a combined 64 games last season, playoffs included. That's the entire analysis. No scheme note coming, no culture paragraph, no leadership-retreat quote.

Injury luck is the least sticky team stat in football, and San Francisco just lived through the tail of the distribution. When they're 8-3 in November, the discourse will call it a return to form and credit somebody's offseason speech. It will actually be the same force that sank the Chiefs' one-score record and inflated the Patriots' road record: variance, regressing, wearing whatever costume the story requires. Health isn't a narrative. It's a coin remembering it has two sides. Quietly, the best bounce-back bet in the conference.
