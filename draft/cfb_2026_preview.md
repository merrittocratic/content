# Everybody Brought a Quarterback

Talkin' season has a load-bearing sentence, and you've read it forty times by now: *they return their starting quarterback.* It gets deployed like a trump card, usually in bold, usually right before someone pencils a team into the playoff.

Here's the problem. Ohio State returns Julian Sayin. Notre Dame returns CJ Carr. Texas returns Arch Manning. Oregon returns Dante Moore. Oklahoma returns John Mateer. Miami didn't have one to return, so it purchased the best one available, which in 2026 counts as the same thing. When everybody has the edge, nobody has the edge. It's the ante now, not the raise.

The other stat everyone quotes is worse. Notre Dame leads the country in returning production at 72%, which sounds dominant until you learn that same 72% would have ranked 14th in 2024 and 7th last year. The Irish didn't get more experienced -- everyone else got less.

So the real question is what's standing next to the quarterback, and which side of the ball got hollowed out in April.

## The Buckeyes Mailed the Defense to the NFL

Ohio State is the preseason No. 1 and the offense is why. Sayin completed 77% of his throws for 3,610 yards and 32 touchdowns, the third-best single-season completion rate in FBS history. Jeremiah Smith is a good month from owning every receiving record the program has. Bo Jackson ran for 1,090 yards as a freshman. Four of five linemen return, and Ryan Day imported Arthur Smith from the Steelers to call it.

Now the other half. Seven Buckeye defenders got drafted, including Arvell Reese fifth, Sonny Styles seventh and Caleb Downs eleventh. That unit finished No. 1 in the country. Matt Patricia is the highest-paid assistant in the sport at a guaranteed $3.75 million, and he did this exact rebuild last year using blue-chippers already sitting in the room. This year he's doing it with transfers.

Quick note on Downs, since we spent April arguing the draft would punish him for playing safety: he went eleventh, higher than any safety in over a decade, and still eight picks behind a running back.

## Miami Bought the Band Back

The Hurricanes lost the title game to Indiana 27-21 after knocking off Ohio State and Ole Miss to get there. Then they lost Carson Beck, Francis Mauigoa and Rueben Bain Jr., and reloaded in about nine days.

Darian Mensah arrives from Duke with 3,973 yards, 34 touchdowns and six interceptions, and the distinction of having been *sued by his own university* to stop him leaving. Duke settled. Mensah brought his top receiver, Cooper Barkate. Damon Wilson II comes from Missouri with nine sacks to replace Bain. Mark Fletcher Jr. is back after 1,192 yards.

But the whole thing orbits Malachi Toney, who caught 109 passes for 1,211 yards as a true freshman who spent most of the season being seventeen. He led all of FBS in receptions, and he's the answer to "best receiver in college football" if you're willing to say something out loud that annoys Ohio State fans.

Circle November 7 in South Bend. Miami beat Notre Dame 27-24 in Week 1 last season, and that one result is the entire reason Miami played for a title and Notre Dame played in nothing.

## Notre Dame Has No Margin and Knows It

Carr threw for 2,741 yards with 24 touchdowns and six picks as a redshirt freshman and is now a real Heisman candidate. Jordan Faison is back, Leonard Moore might be the best cover corner in the sport, and 14 of the 21 defenders who played 200-plus snaps return.

What's gone is the safety valve. Jeremiyah Love went third overall, the highest running back drafted since Saquon Barkley, and Aneyas Williams inherits the job with roughly nothing on his résumé. Carr has never had to be the entire offense. Now he is.

The schedule is the real story: three ranked opponents, and 10-2 already got this program left out once. It opens September 6 at Lambeau against Wisconsin, because apparently the Irish enjoy playing meaningful games in stadiums they've never seen.

## Texas Fixed the Thing That Actually Broke Them

Texas opened last year as preseason No. 1 and finished 9-3 and home in December. The autopsy isn't complicated. The line entered 2025 with one returning starter, Texas averaged 3.3 yards a carry in SEC play, and Manning spent September running for his life. He completed 44% against UTEP. That's the version everyone remembers.

The version that matters came after. Over his final eight games Manning threw for 2,012 yards with 15 touchdowns and two interceptions. Same player, functioning line.

Three linemen return now, headlined by likely first-rounder Trevor Goosby, with Melvin Siani and Laurence Seymore imported to fill the rest. And Colin Simmons already has 21 sacks and 29.5 tackles for loss in two seasons, which is why the sharp play at your Labor Day cookout is arguing he's the first Longhorn off the board in 2027, not the guy with the famous last name.

Ohio State comes to Austin September 12 with GameDay on campus. The whole season, in Week 2.

## Oregon Simply Refused to Leave

Every Oregon story this offseason is a decision not made. Dante Moore was in the No. 1 overall conversation and came back. So did Matayo Uiagalelei, Teitum Tuioti and center Iapani Laloulu. The entire starting defensive front returns because none of them filed the paperwork.

Jordon Davison and Dierre Hill Jr. combined for 1,323 rushing yards as freshman backups and are now the feature guys. Evan Stewart returns from the patellar injury that erased his 2025. Kenyon Sadiq is off to the NFL, which we enjoyed thoroughly on draft night, with Jamari Johnson stepping in.

The risk is invisible on the depth chart. Oregon lost both coordinators to head coaching jobs, Will Stein to Kentucky and Tosh Lupoi to Cal. Continuity in the huddle is not continuity in the headset, and the last time we saw this roster it lost 56-22 to Indiana with a season on the line.

## The Darkhorse: Oklahoma Only Needs One Thing

Every darkhorse pitch requires a leap of faith. Oklahoma's is the shortest in the country.

The Sooners had an elite defense last year -- fourth in SP+, third against the run, 15.5 points allowed a game -- and it dragged them into the playoff. The offense finished 51st in SP+ and 92nd in total offense. That's a canyon between two units wearing the same jersey.

Mateer is why. He broke a bone in his throwing thumb in the SEC opener, had surgery, and came back to face Texas seventeen days later with a rebuilt grip. Oklahoma never gained 400 yards in a game again. He finished with 14 touchdown passes against 11 interceptions after leading the nation in total touchdowns the year before at Washington State.

Now he's healthy, he has a year in Ben Arbuckle's system, four linemen return, and Kip Lewis, Taylor Wein, David Stone and both Bowen brothers hold the defense together. The bet isn't that Venables builds something new. It's that a working thumb is worth three or four wins.

## What I'm Actually Watching

Two of these six will look like frauds by Halloween, and I'm not going to pretend I know which two.

The tells are specific and they arrive early. Whether Patricia can teach a transfer-heavy defense to be Ohio State's defense before Austin on September 12. Whether Manning behind a real line is the second-half player or the first-half one. Whether Oregon's scheme survives losing both of the people who wrote it.

Everybody brought a quarterback. That was the easy part. Now show me the other 21.
