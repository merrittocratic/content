# Don't Trust The Record

Three weeks in, and the records are talking louder than the tape. Buffalo and Kansas City are both 3-0, so naturally the AFC is a two-team race again. Houston and Dallas have one win between them and meet Sunday in a September "elimination game." And Tampa Bay is 0-3, so something must have gone horribly wrong. Let's check the receipts.

## I Am Inevitable

Neither 3-0 team is the AFC's best by net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed). Jacksonville is 2nd in the league, Kansas City 3rd, Las Vegas (also 3-0) 4th, Baltimore 7th and Buffalo 8th. And "inevitable" already took a hit last January, when the AFC title game was New England 10, Denver 7.

Still, half of this storyline holds up. KC is the real thing, and the defense is why: 16.7 points and 261 yards allowed per game, both 3rd in the league. The offense has reinvented itself around it. Mahomes is throwing just 5.8 yards downfield on average, down from 8.1 last year, and ranks dead last of 37 quarterbacks in throws into tight coverage. Kenneth Walker III does the heavy lifting, 1st in the league in rush yards over expected.

Buffalo is the shakier half. The offense is legit: 33.7 points per game (1st) and the league's best explosive-play rate. But the defense ranks 25th, gave up 31 to both Houston and Detroit, and the Bills needed to survive five turnovers to beat the Chargers. We gave Allen a 67% start chance in that one, and he scored 16.5. Honest miss. James Cook has been the hit, clearing his start bar both weeks we liked him.

Week 4 is KC's first real test: at Las Vegas, the league's best pass defense with a +5 turnover margin. One 3-0 record has to give. Our model has Mahomes at a 50% start chance against a 44% baseline, and likes Rashee Rice (model rank 8, Expert Consensus Ranking 15) more than Travis Kelce (model rank 9, ECR 3). Buffalo gets New England, whose run defense stuffs runs at the line less than anyone's. Cook sits at a 69% start chance.

KC looks like a contender built to last. Buffalo looks like a great offense living on borrowed defense.

## Houston, We Have A Problem

Houston's three losses came by 21 points combined, and since Buffalo shredded it in the opener, the defense has been elite.

The offense is the problem, and it isn't new: 24th in EPA per play after finishing 26th last year. The run game is the worst part, stuffed at or behind the line on 27.3% of carries, the league's worst rate. And here's what surprised me: C.J. Stroud isn't the issue. He's completing passes above expectation with zero interceptions. Without Nico Collins, his receivers just aren't getting open. Xavier Hutchinson has 19.4% of the targets and ranks 93rd of 105 in separation.

Dallas is the mirror image. Its offense turns 59.3% of drives into points, best in the league, and CeeDee Lamb has cleared his start bar all three weeks. The defense is dead last in EPA per play and gives up 148 rushing yards a game.

So the "season on the line" framing is half right. Since 2002, 0 of 68 teams that started 0-4 made the playoffs. Teams at 1-3 made it 13.5% of the time, roughly 1 in 7. Houston is the only team to make the playoffs from 0-3 in that span, and it did it twice. Nobody has climbed out of 0-4.

This is the get-right spot. The most-stuffed run game in the league faces the worst run defense. If Houston can't run here, it can't run anywhere. Our model has Stroud at a 41% start chance against a 30% baseline (model rank 7, ECR 17), one of its biggest disagreements with the market. Full disclosure: we were higher than ECR on Stroud in Weeks 2 and 3, and he missed both times.

## Close, But No Cigar

First, a correction to the premise. Tampa Bay's losses were 27-33, 19-23 and 16-23. Three one-score games, 17 points total. The defense is 5th in EPA per play. That's not "horribly wrong."

The offense, though? Every bit as bad as advertised. Four touchdowns on 37 drives, and 27% of drives end in a turnover. That's why a defense 6th in yards allowed is 23rd in points allowed: the offense keeps handing opponents short fields. Baker Mayfield is last of 27 in EPA per dropback, and all three games were bad.

That's the part of this storyline I think holds up, because it's been building for a year. Tampa's offense ranked 5th in EPA per play in 2024, slipped to 19th in 2025, and sits at -0.20 per play now.

Week 4 might make it worse. Mayfield hasn't practiced, Jalon Daniels took the final dropbacks in Week 3, and our news layer cut Mayfield's start chance from 32% to 9% on reports of a thumb injury. The one break: Green Bay can't run either (32nd in yards per carry), which puts the game on Jordan Love, who ranks 35th of 37 in completing passes above expectation. Ugly football incoming.

---

Two-and-a-half storylines survive the receipts, and the half that doesn't is the interesting one. Tampa Bay's problem is real. It's just not where the record says it is.
