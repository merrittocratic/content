# You Know Nothing, Jon Snow

The Bears have been the Monsters of the Midway for decades. This year, both Norths are making a run at the title. The NFC North and AFC North are a combined 29-8 against everybody else, and they're the only two divisions in the league without a losing team. Monsters of the North! But, are they really? A record tells you who you beat. It doesn't tell you who you played, and nobody has played an easier slate than the Norths.

## Who Are Those Guys?

Start with the case for the monsters. The NFC North is 15-3 outside the division and the AFC North is 14-5. Third place is the NFC West at .556, and nobody else is even above .500.

Now for the turn: who were those wins against? The average non-division opponent for both Norths ranks about 20th in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed). For every other division, that average opponent ranks between 14th and 16th. Against teams in the top 12 in net EPA, the two Norths are a combined 1-4. And by average net EPA, the best division in football isn't either North. It's the NFC West, with teams ranked 1st, 6th and 7th (and 28th).

Hardest divisions to win? Absolutely, especially the AFC North, where all four teams are within a game of each other: Cleveland and Pittsburgh at 5-2, Cincinnati at 4-2 and Baltimore at 4-3. Best divisions? Not so much. And once you get inside them, a few of these records start to look a little fishy.

## Luck Be A Lady Tonight

Three teams have records that are writing checks their play can't cash.

Minnesota is 6-0, and the defense is the real deal. The Vikings are 1st in EPA per play allowed and 1st in sack rate (9.9%), and opponents score touchdowns on just 23% of their red-zone trips, the lowest rate in the league. The offense, on the other hand, is along for the ride. It's 30th in EPA per play and 32nd in success rate, and Kyler Murray's 85.8 passer rating ranks 22nd of 32 qualifiers.

Here's the part that should sting in Minnesota. The Vikings went with Murray over J.J. McCarthy, and McCarthy landed with the Giants. In Weeks 6 and 7, he averaged 10.1 yards per attempt with a 95.2 passer rating and +10.6 passing EPA. Murray is at 6.5 yards per attempt and -8.4 passing EPA for the season. It's two games, but right now the quarterback Minnesota moved on from is running an offense better than the one it kept.

Minnesota is 4-0 in one-score games, and its strength of schedule ranks 30th: five of its six opponents rank 12th or worse in net EPA. Great defense, soft schedule and a little luck.

Cleveland's record is the fishiest of the bunch. The Browns are 5-2 with a -13 point differential, and they rank 18th in net EPA. They're 5-1 in one-score games, three of them by exactly three points. Deshaun Watson's CPOE of -4.5 ranks 31st of 32 qualifiers. A defense that ranks 8th in EPA allowed keeps the Browns in games. Close-game luck is doing the rest, and luck has a way of running out.

Detroit is 4-2 but ranks just 21st in net EPA. Last week I said a Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the best-looking shootout on paper in a generation, and nothing has changed. Jared Goff leads all qualifiers with a 112.4 passer rating, and the offense ranks 3rd in EPA per play. The defense ranks 31st in EPA allowed and gives up a league-worst 4.8 completions of 20-plus yards a game. Three of the Lions' four wins came by one score. That's an elite offense covering for a bottom-two defense, without much margin to spare.

## Nobody Puts Baby In A Corner

Then there are the records selling teams short.

Cincinnati is the best team in either North, and it's sitting third in its own division. The Bengals are 4-2 but rank 3rd in the NFL in net EPA, up from 28th last year, and their +41 point differential leads the division by 26. Joe Burrow is cooking! He has 15 touchdowns, three interceptions and a 110.0 passer rating, 2nd among qualifiers, with eight touchdowns and no picks in the last three weeks. If you're picking a monster out of this group, pick the one in third place.

Chicago is 5-2 and 8th in net EPA, and both losses came inside the division: 9-3 at Minnesota, and 31-7 to Green Bay in a game Caleb Williams missed. Williams ranks 6th in passer rating (106.5) and 3rd in CPOE (+5.8).

The other three are closer to what their records say. Pittsburgh (5-2, 16th in net EPA) wins with the run game and a defense that ranks 6th in EPA allowed, despite Aaron Rodgers' passing. Green Bay (4-3, 12th in net EPA) has the league's worst run game and the most takeaways. And Baltimore (4-3, 10th in net EPA) is two different teams: 3-1 with Lamar Jackson, 1-2 with Tyler Huntley.

## Let's Get Ready To Rumble!

Week 8 brings the first real test for the 6-0 Vikings. Minnesota visits Detroit, the market expects the Lions by 3, and by EPA it's best on best and worst on worst: the league's top defense against its 3rd-best offense, and its 30th-ranked offense against its 31st-ranked defense. Our model is fading Goff hard, with a 29% chance of a startable week, down from 49% in his games so far, while the market has him at QB12. If Murray and the Vikings can't score on a defense this bad, the 6-0 has a ceiling.

---

The Norths are deep, not dominant. Two of the division leaders, Minnesota and Cleveland, are built on soft schedules and close wins, and the best team in either division is a 4-2 Bengals team sitting in third place. Records tell you who won. Check who they played.

*Method: game results, point differentials, EPA, success rate, CPOE, passer rating and strength of schedule via nflverse/nflreadr. Net EPA is offensive EPA per play minus defensive EPA allowed per play; ranks are out of 32 teams, and passer rating and CPOE ranks are among 32 qualifying quarterbacks (100+ attempts). Week 8 spreads are the market's expectations, not ours. Start chances from Boxscore Prophet's Monday run, compared against the market's rankings; no injury report was out at run time.
