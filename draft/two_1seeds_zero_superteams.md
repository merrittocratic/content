# Two 1-Seeds, Zero Superteams
## Research Scaffold — Conference Finals 2026

---

## The Angle

The 2026 NBA Conference Finals are a natural experiment nobody scheduled. The East and West 1-seeds — Detroit Pistons and Oklahoma City Thunder — both built through the draft. Neither assembled a superteam. Neither traded away their future for a window. The media spent most of the last decade arguing that path was a fantasy. The bracket disagrees.

This piece uses the Conference Finals setup (and Game 1 reactions) to check the narrative: is the "build it right" model vindicated? Or are we pattern-matching too early?

---

## Core Narrative Spine

- **OKC:** Drafted SGA, surrounded him with Holmgren, Williams, Dort. Patience incarnate. Now a 1-seed with home court in the WCF against the team everyone expected to win the West.
- **Detroit:** Drafted Cade, added Duren, stayed the course through early chaos. Came back from 3-1 in Round 1, just survived a Game 7 against Cleveland. Now face a Knicks team that has been resting for two weeks.
- **The contrast:** The opposing narrative — Knicks (traded for pieces, fired their coach mid-rebuild) and Spurs (acquired Fox, landed Wemby via tank, added Harper in the same draft) — aren't pure superteams either. This is a more nuanced story than "build vs. buy."

**Central question:** Is the 2026 bracket a vindication of the draft-and-develop model, a coincidence, or something more specific about *this* generation of players?
