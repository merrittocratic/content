# Are We Being Konned?

---

ESPN's final Rookie of the Year straw poll came in 80-20. Cooper Flagg got the 20.

Let that land for a second. The consensus #1 pick. The biggest preseason ROY favorite in the history of the betting market. The first rookie since Michael Jordan to lead his team in points, rebounds, assists, AND steals. Twenty votes out of a hundred.

Everyone else went Kon Knueppel. I get the case. I also think we're being Konned.

It's Merrittocracy time!

## The Konn case, at face value

It's real. Knueppel led the entire NBA — not just rookies, the whole league — in three-pointers made with 273. Rookie record. First rookie ever to lead the NBA in triples, period. He shot 43% from deep on volume we haven't seen from a first-year player since Steph Curry. He played 81 games. He helped drag Charlotte from 19 wins to 44.

Durable, efficient, winning. The résumé voters reward.

And yet.

## The per-game problem

| | Cooper Flagg | Kon Knueppel | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Games | 70 | 81 | Knueppel |
| Total points | 1,470 | 1,499 | Knueppel (by 29) |
| Points / game | 21.0 | 18.5 | **Flagg** |
| Rebounds / game | 6.7 | 5.3 | **Flagg** |
| Assists / game | 4.5 | 3.4 | **Flagg** |
| Steals / game | 1.2 | 0.7 | **Flagg** |
| Blocks / game | 0.9 | 0.2 | **Flagg** |
| FG% | 46.8 | 47.5 | Push |
| 3P% | 29.5 | 43.0 | Knueppel |
| 3PM (total) | ~70 | 273 (NBA leader) | Knueppel |
| Team wins | 26 | 44 | Knueppel |

Stare at the total points row for a minute. Knueppel played ELEVEN more games than Flagg and logged 207 more minutes, and ended up with a twenty-nine point edge. One decent scoring night covers his entire volume advantage.

Flagg leads every production category that isn't "made threes." He became the youngest player in NBA history to score 50. Then he did it again four days later. That isn't a counting stat. That's an event.

## Context isn't a counter-argument

The usual pro-Konn defense is contextual. He plays for a real team, Flagg plays for a roster in freefall, team success matters.

Fine. But context cuts both ways. Flagg carried a 26-win Mavericks team that lost Luka last winter and never replaced him. His usage was elevated because it had to be. He drew the top defensive assignment every night. Knueppel played off LaMelo Ball in an offense that asked him to spot up, move the ball, and take what came to him.

The nearest parallel is Wembanyama vs. Holmgren in 2024. Holmgren played 82 games on a 57-win Thunder team. Wemby played 71 on a 22-win Spurs team. Wemby swept all 99 first-place votes. The per-game alpha on the losing team won unanimously.

Same shape of race. Different answer. What changed?

## Where this goes in Year 2

Real question, and somehow the one nobody is asking: what are these guys going to be next year?

Knueppel is a 20-year-old who just shot 43% from three on league-leading volume in a real NBA offense. Legitimate calling card. Also roughly what he is. I won't be surprised if Knueppel settles in as an 18 ppg player for the next decade — a very good complementary piece, the third-best guy on a contender, the kind of sharpshooter who plays fifteen years and makes real money doing it. Great career. Not a franchise-tilter.

Flagg is a 19-year-old who just put up 21/7/5 on a team with no floor spacing and no primary creator. Put shooting around him. Drop in a secondary engine so defenses can't load up on him every possession. A leap to 25-27 ppg isn't optimistic — it's the median outcome. The ceiling on a 6-9 forward who defends, passes, and can drop 51 against playoff-caliber defense is the ceiling of the league itself.

One guy has likely hit his level. The other is just starting to find his.

## Who do you want with the ball?

One more question, and it's the one Rookie of the Year is actually supposed to measure. It's late, it's tied, you need a bucket. Flagg or Knueppel — who do you want?

Nobody's honest answer is Knueppel. Not the voters, not the gamblers, not Charles Lee.

Which is why Charlotte's last two games went the way they did. Tuesday in the play-in against Miami, Knueppel went 2-for-12 from the field, 0-for-6 from three, and watched crunch time from the bench while LaMelo dragged them to overtime. Friday in Orlando — 3-for-10, 1-for-6 from deep, 11 points in a 121-90 elimination that ended Charlotte's season and extended the longest active playoff drought in the NBA.

ROY ballots were locked before tip-off on Tuesday. None of it counts against the vote. But the voters who went 80-20 for Konn were telling themselves a story about who this kid is when the lights are brightest, and the last two nights pressure-tested that story in live, unedited video.

It didn't hold.

The alpha of this year's class is Flagg. The per-game numbers say so. The degree-of-difficulty says so. The ceiling says so. And when the lights came on, the bench said so.

Don't be Konned.
