# All Roads Lead Through Oxford

Three weeks into the season, and somehow every storyline worth checking runs back through the same 60 miles of north Mississippi. Trinidad Chambliss is making the Heisman case for Ole Miss. Lincoln Kienholz's one game against a ranked team happened to be against Ole Miss. And the coach who built the current Ole Miss roster left it for LSU, whose fans are now watching him get out-gained by the guy he recruited. Let's check the receipts on all three.

## Different Zip Code, Same Stat Line

We flagged the Chambliss-Mensah gap a few weeks back, and three games later the production is still basically a wash. Darian Mensah ranks 6th nationally in our production index (79 attempts, 873 yards, 11 touchdowns, zero picks), Chambliss ranks 7th (110 attempts, 924 yards, 7 touchdowns, 2 picks). If you're grading the stat sheet in a vacuum, Mensah's line is marginally cleaner.

But nobody grades a stat sheet in a vacuum, and this is where the case actually lives. Chambliss beat a then-#7 LSU team 32-24 and knocked off Louisville 41-38. Mensah's three wins — Stanford (45-6), Florida A&M (77-7, SWAC), Wake Forest (33-20) — have a combined zero currently-ranked opponents. The honest version of this argument isn't "Chambliss is outplaying Mensah." It's "these two guys are producing at the same rate, and one of them is doing it against a schedule the other hasn't sniffed." That's a real Heisman argument. It's just a different one than the box score alone would suggest.

## Patience, Grasshopper

Now for the one that actually surprised us when we pulled the numbers. Lincoln Kienholz backed up Julian Sayin at Ohio State as recently as 2025 — 11 pass attempts, total, all season — before transferring to Louisville. That backstory is exactly why "no Buckeye fans have heard of the next Joe Burrow" writes itself as a headline. Problem is, the data doesn't back the "not better than Sayin" half of it.

Kienholz ranks 5th nationally in our production index: 816 passing yards, 6 touchdowns, zero interceptions, plus 153 rushing yards and 4 rushing scores — a legitimate dual-threat line. Sayin ranks 34th: 839 passing yards, 6 touchdowns, 1 interception, and negative net rushing once you count sack yardage. In the one game where Kienholz has actually faced a currently-ranked opponent — that Louisville-Ole Miss game from the section above — Louisville had the better per-play EPA (Expected Points Added, or how many points a play was worth relative to an average play in that situation) at 0.161 to Ole Miss's 0.056, despite losing 38-41. Both quarterbacks carry exactly one loss to a ranked team: Sayin to #1 Texas, Kienholz to #8 Ole Miss.

Where we'd actually push back is the Burrow comp itself, not the Sayin comparison. Our own pre-season Heisman research had Burrow ranked 35th of 67 P4 quarterbacks the year before his breakout, and that breakout still took a full second year on campus. Kienholz is three games into year one at a new school. So: he doesn't need to be the next Joe Burrow to be the better story than Sayin right now. He's out-producing him with zero turnovers and was the more efficient offense in the one available head-to-head data point. The Burrow question is just too early to ask either way.

## Wrong Turn At Oxford

Which brings us to the guy who isn't walking off a bus in Oxford anymore. On the raw box score, Ole Miss's 32-24 win over LSU wasn't a turnover-luck special — Ole Miss ran the more efficient offense, 0.219 EPA per play to LSU's 0.121. The passing splits were close (0.270 to 0.237), but the run game is where the game actually got won: 0.256 to 0.118.

Zoom out and the trend line makes the case on its own, independent of one September Saturday:

| Year | Ole Miss (Kiffin → Golding) | LSU (Kelly → Kiffin) |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 8-4 | 9-4 |
| 2023 | 10-2 | 9-3 |
| 2024 | 9-3 | 8-4 |
| 2025 | 11-1 (Kiffin's last year) | 7-5 (Kelly's last year) |
| 2026 (3 games) | 3-0 (Golding) | 2-1 (Kiffin) |

Kiffin left a program on a four-year upward climb, at the best record of that stretch, for one coming off its worst record of the stretch. That's the strongest of the three arguments in this piece, on both the game tape and the multi-year trend.

Two things keep it from being a clean verdict, though. First, Ole Miss's Sept. 19 quarterback was Chambliss — a Kiffin recruit who played for him in 2025. Golding's fast start is running on a roster his predecessor built, which is worth remembering before anyone hands him full credit. Second, and more inconvenient for the thesis: season-long SP+ (an opponent-adjusted efficiency rating, not tied to any single game) still has LSU rated 8th nationally at 24.1 overall, with Ole Miss at 20th and 15.2. By the full-season number, LSU is still rated the better team. The single game and the season rating are pointing in opposite directions, and we're not going to pretend only one of them is real.

---

Three storylines, three different verdicts: one narrative holds up with an asterisk, one gets flipped by its own data, and one earns the take but not a clean sweep. That's usually how it goes when you actually check.
