# Defense Wins Championships. Right?

Two storylines are running in opposite directions this week. The NFC East can't beat anybody, and people are asking whether a division winner could finish under .500. Detroit and Buffalo can't stop anybody, and people are asking whether a Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the highest-scoring ever. Both premises mostly hold up. Both need a correction.

## Misery Loves Company

The last two weeks were ugly. Outside the division, the NFC East went 0-6 in Weeks 5 and 6 and was outscored by 44. Its only other Week 5 game was Giants 27, Commanders 27. In Week 6, all four teams lost: Dallas 23-13 to Green Bay, the Giants 34-27 to New Orleans, Philadelphia 25-14 to Carolina and Washington 23-17 at San Francisco.

How rare is that? Since 2002, only eight divisions have gone winless outside the division across Weeks 5 and 6. By point differential, this one ranks eighth of the eight. The 2011 AFC South (0-7) and the 2020 NFC East (0-4) were worse. An all-four-lose week isn't historic either. It has happened 51 times since 2002, about once every 18 weeks, and already three times this season.

The season record is bad, not historic. The NFC East is 5-11 outside the division, a 31% win rate, and has been outscored by 83. That's 25th-worst of 200 division-seasons since 2002. The 2020 NFC East won 14% of those games through Week 6, and its winner finished 7-9. This year's leader is the Giants at 3-2-1.

Team by team, by net EPA (expected points added, how much a play adds or costs compared with an average play in the same down, distance and field position):

- **Philadelphia** is 31st of 32 in net EPA. Its offense is 30th in EPA per play and scores 17.7 points a game, and Jalen Hurts is out with a blood clot.
- **Dallas** is 25th in net EPA, with the league's 32nd-ranked defense by EPA.
- **Washington** is 24th in net EPA and has allowed 28.7 points a game, 29th in the league.
- **The Giants** are 22nd in net EPA, the least bad of the four.

Now the good news, if you can call it that. The division plays itself. Week 7 is Dallas at Philadelphia, Week 8 is Philadelphia-Washington and Week 9 is Giants-Philadelphia, so the NFC East is guaranteed a win in each of the next three weeks. The rest of the schedule is soft too. By their remaining opponents' net EPA, Dallas has the 30th-hardest schedule of 32, Philadelphia the 29th, the Giants the 27th and Washington the 23rd. And history says divisions bounce back. The 10 worst divisions through Week 6 since 2002 won 46% of their non-division games the rest of the way, ranging from 32% to 58%. None stayed this bad.

So expect improvement. But a division winner at or below .500 is still in play.

Monday night, Dallas visits Philadelphia as a 4.5-point favorite with a total of 44.5, which puts the Cowboys at about 24.5 points and the Eagles at about 20. It's the league's worst defense by EPA against its 30th-ranked offense by EPA per play, without Hurts. Our model likes Tanner McKee more than consensus does: a 28% chance of a startable week (QB16, against an Expert Consensus Ranking of QB20). It also likes George Pickens against the Eagles: a 51% chance (WR10, against a consensus WR18). Dak Prescott is a touch below the market at 35% (QB10, against a consensus QB8).

## Ludicrous Speed

The premise holds. Buffalo and Detroit both score 32.8 points a game, 1st and 2nd in the league, and rank 2nd and 3rd in EPA per play. They also can't defend: 30th and 31st in EPA allowed, and 28th and 30th in points allowed at 28.5 and 29.6 a game. Detroit's games average 62.4 total points, the most in the league, and Buffalo's average 61.3, 2nd.

They've already played. Buffalo won 41-31 in Week 2, a 72-point game. The Super Bowl record is 75, from Super Bowl XXIX (49ers 49, Chargers 26). The highest since 2002 is 74, from Super Bowl LII (Eagles 41, Patriots 33).

On paper, nothing since 2002 comes close. Average the two teams' game totals and you get about 62. The highest figure for any Super Bowl pair since 2002 is 52.6, Tampa Bay and Kansas City after the 2020 season.

Here's the catch: Super Bowls between the highest-scoring teams have stayed low. The five pairs with the highest averages since 2002 all finished under them, at 40, 16, 51, 38 and 62 points. The record games came from less obvious pairs. Super Bowl LII was expected around 47, and Super Bowl LVII (Chiefs-Eagles, 73 points) around 50. This isn't a general pull toward low scores, either. Across all 24 Super Bowls since 2002, the result averaged 3 points above the two teams' regular-season averages. The pull shows up in the high-powered matchups.

And 76 points is rare in any setting. About 2% of regular-season games since 2002 have reached it, 127 of 6,223. Neither team has done it this season. Their high is the 72-point meeting.

So a Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the most explosive matchup on paper since at least 2002. Breaking the record would still take a top-2% game, in exactly the setting where high-powered matchups have most often fallen short. The real variable is whether either defense improves by January. Both are bottom-three by EPA allowed right now.

Buffalo has a bye in Week 7. Detroit hosts Green Bay Sunday afternoon with a market total of 53.5. Our model agrees with consensus on the Lions' stars: Jahmyr Gibbs is RB1 with a 71% chance of a startable week, Amon-Ra St. Brown is WR2 (consensus WR4) at 60%, and Jared Goff is QB4, right where consensus has him, at 46%. The disagreement is Jameson Williams. Our model gives him a 29% chance (WR41, against a consensus WR30), so the shootout doesn't reach him on our board.

---

The NFC East's slump is real but recent, and the schedule is about to bail it out. A Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the best-looking shootout on paper in a generation, which, historically, is the best reason to bet the under.
