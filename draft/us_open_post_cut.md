# Across the Bag

The comfortable read on Wyndham Clark is that he's caught fire with the putter and Shinnecock is handing him an opening. That's not wrong. It's also not the story.

Clark leads the field at -7 through two rounds on the strength of two independent vectors: approach play at +4.84 SG (#5 in the field) and putting at +4.67 SG (#7 in the field). Round 1 was putter-driven -- +3.98 with the flat stick, +2.84 on approach, +9.31 total. Round 2 looked like the correction that usually ends these kinds of heaters: the putter cooled to +0.69, the approach held at +2.00, and he posted +3.25 anyway. That's not a hot putter running out of gas. That's a player who can beat you a different way once you take the first way away.

Going into Thursday, the model had him 19th by predicted SG and 15th in win probability at 1.6% -- a legitimate preseason bet, not an afterthought, but he's outperforming even that reasonably bullish profile. Win probability after two rounds: 34.2%, more than three times the next closest player. The model's R3 projection has him 21st in the field by expected strokes gained. That's regression to the mean working as designed. The lead is already banked. The math works regardless.

## The Challengers

Schauffele had the most impressive Friday in the field. His R2 of +6.25 SG moved him to 11.0% win probability, and the model has him fifth in projected R3 SG at +0.928. The pre-tournament case for Schauffele was straightforward: elite talent, elite prior, due for a clean week. He's finally having one.

Fitzpatrick was one of the opening seven in the preview and the model's seventh-ranked player going in at +1.39 predicted SG -- a stronger pre-tournament case than Clark. Through 36 holes he's at +8.56 total SG and T2. But the profile is the direct contrast to Clark's entire story. Approach (+4.46, #6) and around the green (+2.79, #4) are carrying him. Off-the-tee is -1.35, 129th in the field. He's not winning across the bag -- he's surviving a bad driver on the strength of his irons and short game. A 2022 US Open champion doing it without his driver is a more fragile position than the leaderboard suggests. At Shinnecock, the rough collects eventually. He knows what the last day looks like. The question is whether the driver lets him see it.

Morikawa is the sleeper the preview built a case for -- Shinnecock is an iron-play course and his approach game is the best in this field. Round 1 was modest at +0.31 SG. Round 2 was +7.25 SG. The argument just needed a round to find itself.

Scheffler remains the model's #1 pick by projected R3 SG at +1.843 and 6.6% to win. He'd need Clark to stall and the best 18 holes he's played all year. Scottie Scheffler things are always possible.
