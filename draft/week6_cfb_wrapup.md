# There Can Be Only One. Right?

Texas was #1 in the AP poll on Saturday morning. By Saturday night it had lost to Oklahoma, and it looked a lot more like the team that trailed Ohio State 23-3 in September than the one that came back. The Big Ten's heavyweights are leaking oil while UCLA, of all teams, has crashed the party. And the defending champs have won 22 straight and sit 7th. So, who's the best team in the country? Ask five rating systems and you get five different answers. Let's check the receipts.

## Will The Real Texas Please Stand Up?

Oklahoma 27, Texas 24. Texas led 17-7 at halftime and lost on a 47-yard field goal with three seconds left. Sounds like the same collapse Ohio State suffered in Austin in Week 2. It isn't.

Stat Nerd refresher: EPA (expected points added) is how many points a play added or cost compared with an average play in the same situation. Ohio State's offense went from +0.187 EPA per play in the first half of that game to -0.395 in the second, and Texas scored 21 straight in the fourth. That's a collapse. Texas never played well enough to collapse. Its offense was below zero in the first half too (-0.060), and Oklahoma's only first-half touchdown came on a blocked punt. The 17-7 lead was a mirage.

After halftime it got worse (-0.266), and Arch Manning threw two interceptions that decided it. Peyton Bowen took the first one 42 yards to the house for a 24-17 Oklahoma lead. In the preview I named Peyton and Eli Bowen among the guys holding the Sooner defense together, and that's what holding it together looks like. Texas tied it, missed a 53-yard field goal with two minutes left, and then Courtland Guillory picked off Manning with 24 seconds to go to set up Tate Sandell's game-winner.

Back in August, I said Manning's 2025 season turned around once his offensive line started functioning. This time the line wasn't the problem: 97 line yards (rushing yards credited to the offensive line) to Oklahoma's 48. Obvious passing downs were, where Texas succeeded just 23.8% of the time. And the Ohio State game is starting to look like the exception:

| Opponent | Texas offense EPA/play | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Ohio State | +0.178 | 24-23 |
| UTSA | -0.175 | 30-6 |
| Tennessee | -0.224 | 20-17 |
| Oklahoma | -0.165 | 24-27 |

Three straight FBS games below zero. Texas ranks 89th of 138 in offensive EPA and 102nd running the ball. In fairness, FPI rates its schedule the 2nd-hardest so far, and EPA doesn't adjust for opponents. The ratings that do have Texas 4th (FPI) and 3rd (SRS), with the 2nd-best roster by the 247 talent composite. The talent is there. The offense hasn't shown up since mid-September.

## Honey, I Shrunk The Big Ten

The Big Ten is down, and it's not just a feeling. Through Week 6 of each season, by net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed):

| Big Ten through Week 6 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best team's net EPA rank | 1st (Ohio State) | 2nd (Oregon) | 5th (Indiana) |
| Teams in the FBS top 10 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| Teams in the FBS top 25 | 7 | 7 | 4 |
| Non-conference record vs. Power 4 | 6-8 | 5-6 | 4-10 |

Ohio State has slid from 1st to 5th to 11th, and the defense is why. In the preview I said Matt Patricia was rebuilding the nation's #1 defense with transfers. That unit was 4th in defensive EPA in 2024. It's 42nd now, and 102nd against the run. Oregon has gone 25th, 2nd, 32nd. I also wrote that continuity in the huddle is not continuity in the headset, after the Ducks lost both coordinators. The offense ranks 76th, and against UCLA it converted 1 of 21 passing downs.

Which brings us to the dark horse. UCLA's net EPA rank the last three years: 128th, 129th, and now 12th, the biggest turnaround in the data. It's built on defense, 5th in defensive EPA and 3rd against the run. The offense is the catch: 129th passing. FPI (35th) and Elo (37th) are skeptical, and FPI rates the schedule the 113th-hardest.

So can UCLA knock off Ohio State or Indiana? Not in the regular season, because it doesn't play either one. What's left: Wisconsin (29th in net EPA), Michigan (31st), USC (50th), and four teams ranked 89th or worse. That's a path to the Big Ten title game, not a gauntlet. If UCLA is going to make Columbus or Bloomington nervous, it'll have to do it in Indianapolis.

## There Can Be Only One

Who's #1? Net EPA says Georgia. FPI says Ohio State. Elo says Notre Dame. SRS says Alabama. The AP said Texas, and Texas lost. Five systems, five different teams.

That's because nobody is pulling away. This year's top five by net EPA (Georgia, LSU, Notre Dame, BYU, Indiana) are separated by just 0.10 EPA per play. The fairest way to compare across years is how far #1 sits above the FBS average in standard deviations, which accounts for how spread out the whole field is. Ohio State was 2.90 at this point in 2024 and Texas Tech was 2.96 last year. Georgia is 2.55.

Georgia does have the best resume: 1st in net EPA, 1st in talent, 2nd in FPI, with wins over Alabama (10th) and Oklahoma (44th). But the 20-17 win over Alabama wasn't convincing. Alabama had the better success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule), 44% to 41%, and ran right through Georgia at +0.52 EPA per carry. Georgia won through the air, +0.57 EPA per dropback to -0.31, and needed a 23-yard field goal after missing from 39 earlier in the fourth.

Notre Dame (3rd in net EPA, 1st in Elo) and Miami (9th, 5th in FPI) look elite on paper and haven't beaten anybody. Notre Dame's best opponent is Wisconsin, 29th; everyone else is 90th or worse. Miami's is Wake Forest, 15th; everyone else is 98th or worse, plus FCS Florida A&M. Last week I said we had no idea how good Miami was. We still don't, and its pass defense ranks 103rd.

Then there's Indiana, and here's where it gets weird. The Hoosiers have won 22 straight, 16-0 last year with the national title plus 6-0 this year, and have drifted from 4th to 7th in the AP. Since 2000, every other power-conference team on a 20-plus game streak sat in the top 5 of every poll along the way, most of them 1st or 2nd. The only teams on streaks that long ranked outside the top 5 were Group of 5 programs, UCF and Boise State. Indiana is the first power-conference team to do it, and it's the defending champ.

So the voters are disrespecting Indiana? Not so fast, my friend. Net EPA has the Hoosiers 5th, FPI 7th, SRS 9th. Only Elo, which rewards winning, has them 2nd. The roster ranks 38th in talent, FPI rates the schedule 108th-hardest, and against Nebraska (7th in net EPA), the best team it has played, Indiana lost the success-rate battle 32% to 40% and won by three. The voters aren't ignoring the streak. They're watching how Indiana wins.

---

Texas was never as good as its comeback made it look. The Big Ten's hottest team doesn't play its two best teams. Georgia has the best resume, but Alabama outplayed it per snap, and Indiana is ranked right where its numbers say it should be. There can be only one, but this year nobody has earned it yet.
