# We've Seen This Movie Before. Both Endings.

---

If the Oklahoma City Thunder get past the San Antonio Spurs, the NBA Finals
will feature a matchup we've already run twice this season. We know what both
teams look like against each other. We know where the edges are. The regular
season left receipts, and the receipts tell a more complicated story than
"defending champs will handle the team that hasn't been here since 1999."

OKC went 2-0 against New York. Both games came down to the final possession.
Both ended with the Knicks on the wrong side of a one-score result. And both
of them contain clues about how this Finals could go.

## What the Box Scores Actually Show

**March 4, MSG. OKC 103, NYK 100.**

Chet Holmgren walked into Madison Square Garden and dropped 28 points and eight
rebounds, including a career-high-tying six 3-pointers. He had 14 points in the
first quarter alone. The Knicks had no answer, and Oklahoma City built a 44-31
lead. Then the Knicks went on a 40-9 run in the third quarter, Jalen Brunson
banked in a 3-pointer to put New York up 80-77, and you could feel MSG tipping.

Then Holmgren hit two 3-pointers in the final 46 seconds of the third to put
it back to 50-40 at half... wait, let me re-read that. He had 14 in the first
quarter and two more late in the third. The man was everywhere. SGA added 26.
Karl-Anthony Towns had 17 and 17 but fouled out in the fourth quarter. Brunson
had 15 assists and 5-for-18 shooting. OKC held on for three.

**March 29, OKC. Thunder 111, NYK 100.**

Brunson bounced back with 32 points on 13-for-22 shooting. KAT added 15 and 18
rebounds. New York cut the deficit to one in the fourth quarter and never took
the lead. SGA was 5-for-15 in the first three quarters and then went 3-for-3
in the fourth with 10 points, icing a game the Thunder had no business making
close. He made 13 of 16 free throws. The streak reached 135. Of course it did.

Two games. Both within a possession. OKC won them both. Here's what that means
for June.

## The Chet Variable

The conventional pre-Finals read on Holmgren is grim: he averaged 19.0 points
and 9.1 rebounds in the first seven playoff games, then spent two games watching
Wembanyama put him on a poster in the Western Conference Finals, dropping to
10.5 points and seven boards on 7-for-17 shooting. The narrative writes itself.
Chet had a hard time.

The regular-season box scores complicate the narrative. Against the Knicks
specifically, Holmgren averaged 22 points and 8.5 rebounds across two games,
and he did it in two different roles: dominant stretch big against the zone
(Game 1) and complementary piece when Shai asserted control (Game 2). Karl-
Anthony Towns does not protect the rim the way Wembanyama does. Towns plays
out at the perimeter, and Holmgren torches that coverage from the elbow, the
dunker spot, and the 3-point line. This is a better matchup for Chet than San
Antonio was, and that's not a small point.

The question the postseason has raised isn't whether Chet is good. He is. It's
whether he has a ceiling problem against elite interior opposition. Against
the Knicks, that problem doesn't apply the same way. Watch how quickly
Holmgren's role expands the moment the defense stops being Wembanyama.

## KAT's Foul Trouble is the Hidden Story

The regular-season matchups keep raising this: KAT fouled out of Game 1 in the
fourth quarter with the game on the line. OKC held on. That's not an anomaly;
that's Oklahoma City's defense doing something intentional. The Thunder switch
aggressively and hunt mismatches, and Towns' tendency to reach on switches
invites contact in ways that accumulate. In the playoffs, New York has been
9-0 when KAT dishes out five or more assists. The flip side is that he's also
averaging 3.8 fouls per game this postseason.

A team with SGA and Holmgren has the personnel to run Towns into pick-and-roll
situations that force him to choose between gambling for the steal and fouling.
If OKC can get Towns into foul trouble in a Finals game, New York's offense
fundamentally changes: Brunson stops receiving clean pin-down actions, Bridges
loses his corner-3 cadence, and the half-court spacing that's made New York's
offense one of the best in playoff basketball compresses.

The KAT assist narrative is real and it's earned. So is the foul number.

## Shai in the Fourth Quarter

In Game 2, Gilgeous-Alexander was 5-for-15 through three quarters. Not good. He
made all three of his fourth-quarter attempts, hit his free throws, and ended
with 30 points. The Knicks cut it to one and never got the ball back.

This is the defining trait of a two-time MVP: the ability to absorb a bad three
quarters and still be the best player in the building when it matters. Brunson
is a legitimate superstar and he will give OKC problems. But Brunson has never
done what SGA does in close games with this much at stake. The Knicks guard is
elite. The Thunder guard is in conversation with the five or six best players on
earth. The gap narrows, but it doesn't close.

## The Injury Asterisk

This whole preview assumes a healthy OKC roster, and right now that assumption
is doing a lot of work. Both Jalen Williams and Ajay Mitchell missed Game 4 of
the Western Conference Finals with injuries. Williams, who scored 22 points on
7-for-11 shooting in the March 29 regular-season matchup, is OKC's pressure
release valve: the player Brunson can't completely ignore while chasing SGA.
Without Williams, Oklahoma City becomes a two-man show with a deep bench, which
is a different proposition against a team that's been 11-0 when it hits its
stride.

Mitchell's absence matters in a different way. New York's Mikal Bridges was
averaging 15.6 points on 67% shooting and 44% from three coming out of his
mid-playoffs slump. Bridges against a healthy, long OKC perimeter rotation is a
different proposition than Bridges against a shorthanded one.

The injury report for Game 1 on June 3 will tell you more about this series
than anything written between now and then.

## The Honest Preview

The data from the regular season says two things. First, OKC is the better team
in a neutral-site seven-game series. They went 2-0 against the Knicks in games
that went to the wire, both times making the winning play in the fourth quarter.
When Shai is Shai and the roster is intact, this team has the pieces to disrupt
everything New York does well.

Second, the margins in both regular-season games were so thin that the Knicks
had ample chances to win either one. Brunson's 15 assists in Madison Square
Garden weren't enough because he shot 28% and KAT fouled out. The Knicks cut
the lead to one in Oklahoma City and couldn't execute on the final possession.
These are correctable problems for a team that has now won 11 straight playoff
games, including every close-out game on the road.

New York is peaking at the right time. Oklahoma City might be banged up at the
wrong one.

If everyone's healthy in June, bet the defending champs. If the injury report
looks anything like it did in Game 4 of the West Finals, that calculus shifts.
For the first time in 27 years, MSG is going to host an NBA Finals game.
Whatever happens after tip, that part's already true.
