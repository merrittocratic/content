## What Is On It

Four positions, every one with its own model, because a tight end is
not a wide receiver with smaller usage and a quarterback is not a
running back who throws. Different job, different model.

Every player gets two numbers and no decimals:

- **Start**: the chance he gives you a week you would have started him
  for. Fifteen PPR points for backs and receivers, twenty for
  quarterbacks, twelve for tight ends.
- **Boom**: the chance he wins you the matchup outright. Twenty,
  twenty-five, seventeen.

Alongside the main board: a streamer view for the waiver-wire crowd,
and the rookie tracker, which will be mostly empty this week and
extremely interesting by October.

## How To Read It Without Getting It Backwards

Two rules, and they are the same two rules every single week.

**Forty percent is not a bench.** Look at any flex-zone board and you
will find twenty guys, all of them somebody's starter, and not one of
them above 45. That is not the model being scared. That is what the
flex spot has always been. You are not looking for a player over 50,
you are looking for the best available number in the group you
actually have.

**Compare inside the tier, not against 100.** The useful information
is that your guy is a 43 and the man on your bench is a 31, or that
the rookie your leaguemate just grabbed is a 45 and the veteran you
drafted in round three is a 43. The gap is the product. The absolute
number is just honest.

## What Happens The Rest Of The Week

Today's board assumes everybody plays, because on Tuesday nobody has
filed an injury report yet.

That changes as the week goes. As practice reports land, the board
re-scores -- and the version that matters for your lineup, Saturday's,
knows who is out and who is carrying the extra work because of it. That
is not a cosmetic update. When the back ahead of your guy gets ruled
out, that is worth about four touches that the season-long averages
have not noticed yet, and it is the single biggest thing this model
learned to do all summer.

Two things come out of that Saturday number, starting next week:

**Start 'Em, Sit 'Em: The Movers**, every Saturday, is the actionable
half. It does not lead with the top of the board -- everybody's column
leads with the top of the board, and you did not need us to tell you
to start your studs. It leads with the players whose own chance moved
the most since we last published a number on them, in either
direction, with the matchup or the injury report that explains it.

**On the Record**, every Tuesday, is the accountable half. Before we
tell you anything new, we tell you what happened to what we told you
last week: the calibration table, the misses by name, the longshots
that hit. Both halves, every week, all season.

If something breaks late that moves a player meaningfully, it goes out
on X.

## House Rules, Restated On Day One

The model is **frozen** for the season. What is on the board today is
what gets graded in Week 18. No quiet formula adjustments after a bad
Sunday.

We are **on the record**. If we tell you 60 percent, it needs to
happen about 60 percent of the time, or the number is vibes with a
percent sign on it. A bad week gets the same table as a good one --
starting next Tuesday, every Tuesday, no exceptions.

We **publish the nulls**. Three things we spent the offseason on came
back empty and got written up anyway.

And the **code is public**, because "trust me" is not a methodology.
