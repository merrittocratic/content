# Your Draft Board Is Somebody Else's Problem

## STATUS: DRAFT -- lede-in post 3 of 5, target Tue 2026-09-01

It is the Tuesday after Labor Day. Half of you drafted this weekend,
the other half are drafting Thursday night with a beer and a
spreadsheet, and every fantasy outlet on earth is currently running
its highest-traffic content of the calendar year.

So this is a genuinely stupid week for me to tell you that our model
cannot help you draft.

But it cannot, and I would rather say it now than have you find out
in October and feel lied to.

## The Long Con of the August Projection

Boxscore Prophet is a weekly model. It reads what is actually
knowable on the Friday before a game: how your guy has been used
lately, who he is playing, who is ahead of him on the depth chart and
whether that guy practiced, and what the betting market thinks the
game will look like.

Now look at that list and ask which of it exists for Week 6 right
now. None of it. There is no Week 6 usage. There is no Week 6 injury
report. There is a Week 6 line, and it is not real yet.

When somebody hands you a full-season projection in August -- 1,180
yards, 8.4 touchdowns, 244.6 points -- they are not doing the thing
I just described. They are doing a blend of last year, average draft
position, and vibes, and then putting a decimal on it so it looks
weighed instead of guessed. It is a legitimate job. Some people do it
well. It is not our job, and I am not going to fake it for one week
of traffic.

Draft however you want. Take the guy your gut likes at 3.07. We will
be here Tuesday.

## Round Eight Is Where Your Season Actually Lives

Here is the part where I stop being useless.

The picks you agonize over are the picks you are least likely to get
wrong. Rounds one through three are, for the most part, a list of
players who are good, on offenses that are good, who will get the
ball. You do not need a model to start Saquon, and you did not need
one to draft him either.

Your season gets decided four hours later, in the rounds where
everybody left on the board is a coin flip, and then it gets decided
again every Wednesday on the waiver wire from October to December.

Here is what that zone looks like through our model. This is running
backs 15 through 35 from our dress rehearsal on last season's Week
15 -- deliberately not the top of the board:

![RBs 15-35, 2025 Week 15](img/teaser_board_rb_w15.png)

Derrick Henry -- a name you would start on reputation alone, a name
that went in the third round of somebody's draft -- comes in at 43
percent, behind two rookies. Kyren Williams and Kenneth Walker, both
penciled in as starters in most leagues, are one-in-three. Bucky
Irving shares a row with Kimani Vidal, who was on waivers a month
earlier.

And nobody in that entire stretch clears 45 percent.

That last one is the whole point. Twenty-one players, all of them
somebody's starter, and not one of them is more likely than not to
give you a startable week. That is not the model hedging. That is
what your flex spot has always been, described accurately for once.

The zone where reputation is least reliable is exactly the zone where
a weekly probability is worth the most. It is also, conveniently, the
zone your draft cannot solve, no matter how many hours you put into
it this weekend.

## The Kids Are Alright, or They Got Lucky Twice

One more thing that will matter to you in about six weeks.

We track all 146 rookies from this class, and the tracker is built
around one split: opportunity versus efficiency. A rookie who just
posted 22 points on nine touches and a long touchdown, and a rookie
who posted 22 points on nineteen touches and a growing snap share,
look identical in your league's chat. They are not remotely the same
player going forward, and one of them is about to be dropped by a guy
who thinks he sold high.

Every October there is a rookie your leaguemate panics over. The
tracker exists to tell you whether the usage is real.

Chances, not decimals. Starting when the games start.
