# Not My Problem

---

Today is the single busiest draft-traffic day of the fantasy calendar. Every rankings site, every podcast, every guy in your league has a board.  Not us.

That's not a tease for something bigger coming later this week. A draft board is not on the menu at all because Boxscore Prophet is an in-season, week-to-week lineup tool. It tells you, given everything that's already happened, what's likely to happen this Sunday. Drafting asks a completely different question: who's going to hold a role for seventeen games before a single snap occurs.  Our model doesn't really have an opinion on that, on purpose. Not because we're being humble, but because it structurally can't answer it, and we'd rather tell you that on the day it's most inconvenient to say than bury it in a footnote later.

## I Can't Even

Here's what "can't" actually means, with receipts.

Every rookie running back's volume features: carry share, target share, snap share are built from his own rolling average of recent games. At Week 1 of a rookie season, that average doesn't exist. It's blank by construction. The only pre-season signal left is draft round, and draft round is identical for every back in the same tier of the same class. The model doesn't have a role feature to categorize a back as a future "bell-cow" or future a "committee" back. It finds out the same way you do: by watching him play.

How did we get here?  We checked every first-round rookie back with enough sample to grade, 13 backs, 182 player-weeks, and on average the model undersold this group by 10.6 percentage points. The problem is, the average hides the real story, and the real story is not about draft slot as all 13 of these guys went in round one. Split the population and it's two groups moving in opposite directions, plus a handful who landed in between. Seven backs got a genuine bell-cow role and beat their number by 13-36pp: Fournette, Elliott, Barkley, Harris, Bijan Robinson, McCaffrey, and Gibbs. Three walked into a committee with the same draft capital and missed their number by 11-27pp the other way: Edwards-Helaire, Jeanty, Penny. Same round, same blank slate, opposite outcomes, and nothing available before Week 1 tells you which one you're getting.

Jeremiyah Love is the live question right now. Third overall to Arizona, first back off the board, the highest a running back has gone since Barkley at #2 in 2018, $53M guaranteed. The Cardinals' first depth chart of the summer had him as RB2, behind Tyler Allgeier.  He has since moved to RB1, but with James Conner still in the room, that could lead to a touchdown vulture situation. Most analysts expect Love to end up the workload leader, but a Barkley outcome and an Edwards-Helaire outcome are both still live for him.  No model, ours included, can price that in before real snaps occur. We'll leave the guessing to the fantasy draft sites. It was never going to be our problem to pretend to solve.

## What Would You Say You Do Here?

Once the rolling features wake up, and a guy has played two or three real games, this stops being a guessing exercise and becomes exactly the kind of evidence-based call our model is built for. Not the locks at the top of your roster. We care more about *The Flex Zone*: the volatile, boundary-tier start/sit calls that decide your Sunday and that nobody else is pricing seriously in August. We showed you that zone in the season preview, a 2025 Week 15 board where Derrick Henry sat at 43% probability of having a *startable* week behind two rookies.  No player in the entire flex tier cleared 45% and that's not the model hedging.  That's what the flex zone  actually is, and you might have two players in that zone.  So, knowing who has a higher probability to score in the starter range, or even better, who has the higher probability of having a boom week could be the difference between winning your league or shaving your eyebrows.  It's a harder, more useful problem than a preseason ranking that's already stale by Week 3.

We trade pre-season certainty for in-season honesty. That's not a smaller product, it's a different one, and it starts grading itself in public next week.  Today we're telling you what we don't do. Friday, we show you exactly how we'll grade ourselves once there's a game where we can check our work. The actual Week 1 board goes live next Tuesday. The scoring starts the week after that, in public, same as always.
