# Unscripted

Are you seeing it in your social media feeds and on network season preview show?  Leaked camp footage that makes you think, this is the year!  August is peak narrative season. Every joint-practice rep becomes the proof of a legend or a loser, and regardless of last year's record, every team feels like they got a shot to make it to SoFi in February. 

Five storylines are already making the rounds and most don't survive contact with a spreadsheet.

## If You Ain't First, You're Last

In 2024, the Chiefs went 11-0 in one-score games, best in the league. In 2025, they went 1-9 in the same bucket, worst in the league. Same coach, same quarterback mostly. The gap between a No. 1 seed and a lost season was much thinner than the Chiefs 6-11 record demonstrated.

The 16-13 Week 15 loss to the Chargers cost Mahomes his ACL and LCL, while ending any chance of backing into the playoffs or finishing over .500 for the year. Just 7 months after the injury, he's back at camp, aiming for Week 1. Kansas City sent three picks to Cleveland to move up to No. 6 for cornerback Mansoor Delane, replacing the traded Trent McDuffie. Kelce decided he needed one more fairy-tale ending after marrying the princess in a castle at MSG.  If getting a top CB in the draft and running it back with two Hall of Famers isn't enough, let's add the reigning Super Bowl MVP, in Kenneth Walker III, into the mix.   

Just as it was clear that repeating 11-0 in one-score games was unlikely, so is repeating 1-9.  Regression says some of the losing was noise. The problem, it doesn't say the division got easier. Denver went 14-3 last year, and ended Kansas City's nine-year AFC West streak outright.  For now, the Chargers appear to have their O-line intact and are poised to make a run and finally win a playoff game with Justin Herbert. The Chiefs can and probably will win more coin flips this year. Whether that's enough to top a division that no longer runs through Arrowhead by default is an entirely different question.

## Patriot Games

Drake Maye directed the largest single-season turnaround in modern NFL history, 4-13 in 2024 to 14-3 in 2025, tied with the 1999 Colts and 2008 Dolphins. New England went a perfect 9-0 in true road games, capped by a 10-7 win at Denver in the AFC Championship, and became the first team ever to beat three top-five defenses in one postseason. None of that is noise.

What that record doesn't capture was how historically easy last year's schedule was.  It started off "labeled" as easy before a single game was played, with just 4 returning playoff opponents and a combined .429 winning percentage.  Then it became "actually" easy as the season progressed, with their opponents finishing with a combined .391 winning percentage, the easiest since 1999.  So, did Drake Maye "make a real leap", or was last year a mirage.  

When the going got tough in the playoffs last year, the numbers did as well.  207 YPG passing, 58% completions, 6TDs vs. 4 INTs, and 7 Fumbles with 4 of them lost.  It also has to be noted that 235 of his total 828 playoff passing yards came in "garbage time" in the Super Bowl.  In spite of the stats, his team won 3 of those 4 playoff games, so that's worth noting.  *HOWEVA*, Super Bowl hangovers for the losing team are real, the schedule gets much tougher this year, and I think Drake Maye as a top-5 QB talk needs to simmer for a bit.

## The Highlights? ...Well there aren't any. So, to Hell with 'em!

Pro Bowl Qaurterback, Shedeur Sanders finished his rookie year with an 18.8 QBR, the worst mark in Browns history among quarterbacks with at least six starts. Yes, you read that first part right, the worst statistical season for a franchise not known for great quarterback play, garnered Pro Bowl roster spot after several opt outs. **Legendary**

The seven-start file: 1,400 yards, seven touchdowns, ten interceptions, a 3-4 record that was still the best mark of any Browns starter that year, ahead of Flacco (1-3) and Gabriel (1-5). The 393-yard, four-touchdown outing against Tennessee is a real signal. The three-interception collapse against Chicago the following week is one as well, just in the other direction.

New head coach Todd Monken now runs a quarterback competition against Deshaun Watson, whose 33.1 QBR since arriving in Cleveland is the league's lowest among qualified passers. Sanders might win the job by default alone, kind of like his Pro Bowl roster spot. 

## The J-E-T-S Tax

This is not a new initiative by Mayor Mandami.  This is about talent development.  In April, our draft model liked tight end Kenyon Sadiq's player-level profile at +0.169, one of the cleanest marks in the class. Once the Jets drafted him, the team-adjusted projection dropped to -0.08. Wide receiver Omar Cooper Jr. came in at -0.11. Same culprit both times: the Jets' two-year retention rate of 0.161, the feature that tracks whether an organization keeps what it drafts long enough to develop it. Notice how Sam Darnold is no longer seeing ghosts.  The model didn't downgrade the players. It downgraded the address.

That was an April opinion and it will have a January verdict. Geno Smith arrived via trade from the Raiders to steady the position, and Aaron Glenn's second year doubles as a referendum on a 15-year playoff drought, the longest active streak in major American pro sports. If Sadiq and Cooper produce like their profiles project, the organizational tax takes a public loss, and we'll print it here. If they vanish into the fog that's swallowed a decade of Jets first-rounders, the tax held. Either way, in the predictive modeling world it's known as the out-of-sample test, and nobody grades their own homework quietly.

## Mr. Glass

The 49ers went 12-5, upset the defending champion Eagles in the Wild Card round, then got run off the field 41-6 by the Seahawks in the Divisional round, their second-worst playoff loss ever. Over The Cap tracked more than $95 million in cap value on injured reserve at various points, the most of any team in football. Nick Bosa and Fred Warner combined to miss 25 games. Brock Purdy missed all of six and most of eight games. George Kittle missed six. Most would call their success gritty, or in HR speak, they showed resilience.  I call it survival, and it's why every Shanahan acolyte calls him one of the best coaches in the league.  

Health is the least sticky team-level stat in football. Though with the 49ers, there seems to be an exception to that rule.  There is an advanced stat called adjusted games lost that tracks injuries by player importance and partial availability. The 49ers have been in the bottom-third in the league in adjusted games losts since 2013.  So, the real question isn't whether Shanahan's roster has the toughness the January columns credited it with. It's how good this team looks with Bosa, Warner, Purdy and Kittle on the field together for close to a full season, because that barely happened in 2025 and the result was still a road win over the defending champs.
