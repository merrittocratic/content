# Everything Must Go

The deadline gave us 18 in-season deals, and the teams that spent the most did not always buy the most. Jacksonville paid a 2027 first-round pick and two second-rounders. Chicago paid two firsts. San Francisco paid a fourth and a sixth for two starters. The price of a trade and the size of the hole it fills are two different numbers, and this year they barely correlate. So I went through the major deals to see who patched a leak, who bought a name, and who is left holding the bag.

## Go Big Or Go Home

The Jaguars are 6-2 and went all in, sending a 2027 first, a 2027 second, a 2029 second and two later picks for defensive tackles Jordan Davis and Davon Godchaux and safety Jessie Bates III. The record is real, but the defense behind it is odd. Jacksonville is 3rd in EPA per play allowed (points added or lost per play versus an average play), yet 21st in yards per play allowed (5.22). The gap is takeaways: 14 of them (3rd) and a +8 turnover margin (2nd). Philadelphia and Indianapolis have the 29th and 30th offenses by EPA per play, and the one top-10 offense Jacksonville faced, Houston, scored 34.

Here is the part that makes the trade make sense. In Weeks 1-6 the Jaguars allowed 72.7 rushing yards a game at 3.33 a carry. In Weeks 8-9, after DaVon Hamilton went down, that jumped to 141.5 at 4.88. Davis and Godchaux are the patch. Bates plugs the other hole: on throws 20-plus yards downfield, Jacksonville allows a 46% completion rate, 27th in EPA. Atlanta allowed 31% with Bates, 7th.

My problem is the price tag. The first-rounder went for Davis, who played 35-65% of Philadelphia's defensive snaps. That is a lot to pay for a part-time nose tackle. The test is at Tennessee (1-7), where Jacksonville is a 10.5-point favorite. If Tony Pollard runs for 4.5 or more a carry, the hole isn't patched yet.

## Pay No Attention To The Man Behind The Curtain

Everyone will talk about Keenan Allen, but the 49ers' real move was DeForest Buckner. San Francisco is 7-1 and 1st in EPA per play, success rate (the share of plays that keep the offense on schedule) and red-zone touchdown rate (85%). It did that with Mike Evans out since Week 6 and Deebo Samuel out since Week 5, so a receiver was a reasonable shopping item.

Allen, though, is a chain-mover, not a difference-maker. With the Colts he averaged 43.9 yards a game and 6.05 yards per target, 59th of 66 receivers with 30-plus targets. His separation (distance to the nearest defender at the catch) ranks 100th of 117. He also has a 69% catch rate and 20 first downs. Brock Purdy is at 8.39 yards per attempt, and Allen's quarterback in Indianapolis, Daniel Jones, was at 5.71. A sixth-round price fits that.

Buckner for a fourth is the one that matters. The 49ers were already 1st in yards per carry allowed (3.44), but their sack rate is 5.2%, 27th. Buckner had 4 sacks and 8 QB hits in seven Colts games. This week he faces Dallas, which takes sacks on only 4.0% of dropbacks (5th-best), in a game with the highest total on the board (55.5).

## Hello? Is Anybody There?

Kansas City picked up Josh Downs for a fourth and a fifth. The Chiefs' receiver room behind Rashee Rice is thin: Xavier Worthy is at 5.91 yards per target, 60th of 66. Downs was at 6.21, 56th of 66, so the bet is that he was a quarterback problem. His separation (2.97 yards, 55th of 117) is mid-pack and well ahead of Allen's. Jones's completion rate above expectation was -2.0, 24th of 36. It's a fair price for that bet.

The Colts are the ones who paid for it. At 2-7 they sold Allen, Downs, Buckner and Quenton Nelson for a third, two fourths, two fifths and a sixth. They were already last in the league in passing EPA, but Allen and Downs were about 38% of their targets. In the two games since Nelson and Buckner left, offensive EPA per play went from -0.06 to -0.33 and rushing yards allowed from 126 a game to 182. The run defense was already poor, so the sale made a bad unit worse. It didn't break a good one. Jonathan Taylor, who stayed, ran for 69 yards on 21 carries with no touchdowns in those two games. Miami has the league's 32nd-ranked defense by EPA per play. If Taylor can't run there, "stranded" is the right word.

## Buyer Beware

Two other deals tell the same story. Minnesota getting Kirk Cousins for a third is the biggest upgrade in the market. The Vikings' quarterbacks were Kyler Murray at -0.06 EPA per dropback (hamstring, not practicing) and Carson Wentz at -0.27. Cousins in Las Vegas was at +0.17, and he was sacked on just 3.3% of dropbacks. Minnesota's offensive line allows a 9.3% sack rate, 27th, so that is the one caution. The model never gave him better than a 34% start chance, and he cleared the 20-point line in five of nine games. That is a miss I'm not hiding.

At the other end is Houston, which spent a second-round pick on Marvin Harrison Jr. In three games he has 11 targets, 6 catches and 60 yards, on 19%, 21% and 46% of snaps. The Texans were already 3rd in ANY/A (yards per dropback after sack yardage, with a touchdown bonus and an interception penalty) without him.

The highest price went to Chicago for Jeffery Simmons: two firsts and a fourth for 2 sacks and 3 tackles for loss in two games. The Bears are 7-2 with a -7 turnover margin, 29th. The record is ahead of the profile again.

The pattern is the same as the one from the Vikings last week. Price tells you what a team wanted, not what it needed. Check the hole, then check the receipt.
