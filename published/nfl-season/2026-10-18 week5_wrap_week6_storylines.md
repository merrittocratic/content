# It's Not Me, It's You

The classic breakup line is being turned upside down this week. Marvin Harrison Jr. might have just James Harden-ed himself out of Houson, minus the fat suit. C.J. Stroud and Dak Prescott are playing way above their team's current records.  And then there was Jayden Daniels.  "*We* have to communicate better, and that *includes* me".  Another way of phrasing that, "It's not me, it's you Dan Quinn.  

## Raising Arizona

Questions have to be asked when consensus can't miss talents of multiple drafts keep landing in your lap, and they underperform.  Marvin Harrison Jr. wasn't benched in Arizona. He was ignored. He played 74% of the offensive snaps this year, but drew just 13 targets in four games, an 8.3% target share, down from 22.6% as a rookie. That was a choice by new head coach Mike LaFleur, not a sign Harrison couldn't get on the field.

The fair knock is separation. As a rookie, Harrison averaged 2.05 yards between himself and the nearest defender when the ball arrived, 95th of 96 qualifying receivers. But he also got bad balls. FTN's charting graded 68% of his targets as catchable in 2024 and 65% in 2025, against a league average rate of about 74%. When he did get good looks in 2025, he ranked 13th of 78 receivers in EPA per target. That's not a bust.

Harrison's exit spawned a theory: with him gone, defenses will double Trey McBride. While nobody publishes double coverage stats, FTN charts contested targets, where a defender was in position to contest the catch. Arizona's contested rate jumped from 18.9% to 30.4% in Week 5. The catch: the opponent was Detroit, which forces contested catches more than all but two defenses.  Still the 30.4% last Sunday was above Detroit's average of 22%, and what was particularly telling was the jump in contested catches for Trey McBride.  His season average was 26% and it jumped to 40% vs. the Lions.

Back in September I said Jeremiyah Love could turn out to be Saquon Barkley or Clyde Edwards-Helaire, and nothing before Week 1 could tell you which. Five games in, he's closer to Helaire. Unlike Harrison Jr., Love has played in under half the team's offensive snaps in four of five games. He's clearly in a committee role, but is isn't all about usage. Love averages 3.66 yards per carry (24th of 35 backs) and is 0.39 yards per carry below expected (29th of 42), a stat that already accounts for his blockers. He isn't facing stacked boxes either. Of the eight top-10 backs drafted since 2015, his first five games rank last in scrimmage yards per game (56.2) and yards per carry.  LaFluer also just came out and said we need Love to block more.  Keep in mind, this a 215 pound back who runs a 4.36 40, and they want him to block more?

Sunday at the Rams, the market has Arizona at about 19.5 points. Our model gives Love a 25% chance of a startable week (RB24, against an Expert Consensus Ranking of RB19). Harrison is set to debut for Houston at WR77 on our board, 25 spots below consensus.

## Deserve Has Got Nothin' To Do With It

Houston is 1-4, and in Week 3, I said Stroud wasn't Houston's problem.  He is 3rd in the league in completion percentage over expected (CPOE, accuracy adjusted for how hard each throw is), and the Texans have scored 110 points, while allowing 112.  This would normally produce about 2.5 wins, and they only have one. It's the biggest shortfall in the league. They've lost three one-score games, without a single win in that scenario.  Stroud does have to own part of it. His EPA per play falls from 0.164 in the first three quarters to 0.031 in the fourth quarter and overtime.  Houston is also 22nd in red-zone touchdown rate, and most of that is due to sacks and a poor run game.  The most telling stat is that Houston averages 8.8 yards to go on third down in the Red Zone.

The case for a team's record underperforming it's QB play is even cleaner in Dallas. Prescott is 7th in EPA per play and he's at his best late, 0.447 per play in the fourth quarter and overtime. The offense is 4th in third-down rate and 3rd in red-zone touchdown rate. In the same Week 3 article I called Dallas the exact opposite of Houston, and it still is: the defense is dead last in EPA per play, and the Cowboys are 2-3 with three one-score losses of their own.

Sunday in London, Jacksonville's run defense is 1st in yards per carry allowed, but its pass defense is 23rd in yards per attempt allowed. Houston's run game is awful anyway, so this game will be on Stroud's arm. Our model gives him a 24% chance of a startable week (QB19, against a consensus QB15).  Dallas visits Green Bay as a 1.5-point underdog, and the Packers' pass defense is 3rd in EPA per play, the best Prescott has faced. Our model gives him a 44% chance of a startable week (QB8, against a consensus QB6).

## I Can't Unsee That

It was all going so well.  Jayden Daniels' efficiency has come back this year: 0.137 EPA per play, up from -0.018 in 2025.  During this week 5 matchup with the Giants, he was even exceeding his efficiency per play average on the way to 32.3 Fantasy points.  But, it has not been all sunshine and double rainbows in 2026, as his accuracy is down with a CPOE of -0.2, 24th of 30 qualifying quarterbacks.  He's also holding the ball longer, 2.97 seconds before each throw (10th-longest of 37 qualifying QBs). 

This brings us to the overtime clock management against the Giants. Tied 27-27, no Washington timeouts. At 0:55, Rachaad White ran up the middle for 2 yards, in bounds, to the Giants' 24. The next snap came did not come until 0:12 showed on the clock. That's 43 seconds between snaps, the longest of 63 comparable situations since 2024, where the median was 16 seconds elapsing. Daniels was sacked on that snap, and then proceeded to sit on the ground as 3 more seconds elapsed, and eventually time expired and the game ended in a tie.  One slammed headset and two press conferences later, both QB and Coach are saying the right things, but it's clear there is a lack of communication and urgency in the building.  I don't think this is survivable for either.  Quinn will be gone by season's end, and no incoming coach is going to be able to trust Daniels' game situation awareness moving forward.

Monday night at undefeated San Francisco, the 49ers allow 6.25 yards per pass attempt, 2nd-fewest in the league. A quarterback taking 2.97 seconds to throw is the pressure point. 

---

Harrison was ignored more than he failed, and Love may be headed down the same path. Stroud's record is unlucky, but he's got to own some of it. And Daniels lost the clock, probably the locker room, and his coach's job.
