# On This Team We Fight For That Inch

Can you smell it?  Football is in the air!  Training camps are open, and there is an overwhelming sense of optimism around the league.  I mean, the Browns even think that Shedeur is the answer at quarterback right now!  Bijan just got paid and Jahmyr is gently tapping his fingers together like Monty Burns.  Eeeexcellent!  Speaking of Gibbs, every fantasy site will hand you the same thing this September: a decimal point. "Jahmyr Gibbs is projected to score 17.3 points this week." The reality is nobody knows whether Gibbs will score 8 or 28 on Any Given Sunday, and the decimal point is there to make a guess look like a measurement.

So, The Boxscore Prophet, our new model launching Week 1, doesn't do decimals. It answers the two questions you're actually asking when setting you lineup on Sunday morning: what's the chance this guy gives me a week worthy of a start, and what's the chance he goes full league-winner?

## I'll Gladly Pay You Tuesday for a Fantasty Winner Today

Every Tuesday, a fresh board for all four skill positions: RBs, WRs, QBs, and tight ends, who all get their own model because a tight end is not a wide receiver with smaller usage.  Different job, different model.

Here's what a board looks like, from our dress rehearsal on 2025's Week 15 for flex-level starters:


Two numbers per player. The full bar is the chance of a startable week; the dark bar is the chance of a boom. Now read what a decimal can't tell you. Derrick Henry, a name you'd start on reputation alone, comes in at 43% behind rookies TreVeyon Henderson and Quinshon Judkins. Kyren Williams and Kenneth Walker, penciled-in starters in most leagues, are one-in-three this week. Bucky Irving shares a row with Kimani Vidal, a month-ago waiver pickup.  And nobody in this entire stretch clears 45%, and that's not the model hedging, that's what the flex zone actually is. The board doesn't care what the back of the jersey says.



 When our model says 60%, it needs to happen about 60% of the time, or the number is just vibes with a percent sign.  So before we let it near your lineup, we made it prove itself across a decade of NFL seasons, week by week, using only what was knowable the Friday before kickoff. No peeking at Sunday. It passed and actually did a little better than Tommy Boy's D+ without going to college for 7 years.
 
 And you won't have to take my word for it, because it keeps taking the test in public: every week we grade the previous board against the actual box scores and post the scorecard, flattering or not. The model is frozen for the season, and there are no quiet mid-year tweaks, no "we've adjusted the formula" after a rough Sunday. What ships in Week 1 is what gets graded in Week 18, just based on what the model thinks of the incoming data.
