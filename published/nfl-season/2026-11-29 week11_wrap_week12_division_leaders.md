# Here's the deal, I'm the best there is.

So who are the best division leaders in the NFL? I ran the math on the ties first, then profile the top six plus the Bears and Rams, because while those two teams don't lead anything right now, they grade out way ahead of some of the teams that do.

## It's Not Personal, Sonny. It's Strictly Business.

Kansas City and Denver are both 8-2, split their two games, and are 3-1 in the division. Talk about splitting hairs!  To figure out the division leader, the next tiebreaker is record in common games, which requires a four-game minimum. The Chiefs and Broncos have both played the Chargers, Raiders, Cardinals and Seahawks, which makes exactly four.  Denver is 4-0 and Kansas City is 3-1, with the difference being a 20-3 loss to Seattle in Week 7. That Chiefs' loss to the Seahawks is the whole division lead, and the tiebreaker ends there.

It's an odd way to settle it, because Kansas City has outscored opponents by 108 points and Denver by only 26, but point differential is the last tiebreaker, not the first. For the record, if both teams had been 4-0 vs. common opponents, the Chiefs would have won the next tiebreaker on conference record (6-1 to 4-1). The Broncos have had the harder road. Their opponents have a combined win percentage of .564, 7th-toughest in the league, against Kansas City's .388, the league's easiest. The tie lasts until one of them loses. Kansas City plays at Buffalo Thursday and Denver is at Pittsburgh on Friday as a 3-point favorite. Given that Aaron Rodgers was just released, and Mike McCarthy may currently be napping, Denver may be able to maintain their edge for at least one more week.

The Bears and Lions are a different kind of tie. They're both 8-2 and haven't played, so head-to-head gets skipped. Detroit wins on division record, 2-0 vs. 0-2, and Chicago's only two losses of the year are to Minnesota and Green Bay. A Thanksgiving Day matchup will settle the question temporarily. They play again in Week 17, so the head-to-head isn't really settled until then.

So here's how I'd rank them: the six best teams that lead a division, plus a couple that could based on talent.  And, yes I'm saying the Chiefs are leading the division because I'm writing the article, and "you will listen to every d@mn word I have to say..." 

## I'm The King Of The World!

**San Francisco 49ers (9-1).** Of the eight, this is the only team without a hole I could find. The 49ers are 1st in SRS (+10.5), 1st in net EPA, and 4-0 against teams with winning records. The offense is 1st in yards per play (6.57), third-down conversion rate (52.3%) and red-zone touchdown rate (85.7%). Brock Purdy has 23 touchdowns and 5 interceptions and is +5.7 in CPOE (completion percentage over expected given throw difficulty), 4th of 37 qualifying quarterbacks. The smallest hole is the pass rush, which is last in the league in sack rate (4.4%) even after the deadline trade for DeForest Buckner. Nick Bosa is still working his way back into form following an injury, so I would expect the pass rush to round into form soon. The 49ers host 6-4 Seattle off a bye. The Seahawks are 1st in yards per play allowed (3.88), and San Francisco's worst offensive game of the year was the 32-29 win in Seattle in Week 5.

**Jacksonville Jaguars (7-3).** The Jaguars have the best point differential in the league (+12.0 per game), the 2nd-best SRS (+9.8) and the 3rd-best net EPA. They've lost to Denver, Houston and the Giants, and the Giants loss is the strange one: Jacksonville outgained New York 355-243 and lost 23-18, with Trevor Lawrence going 25-of-47 and throwing 2 interceptions. The offense is lopsided, too, because it's 7th in EPA per play while the run game is 21st in yards per carry (4.15). Tennessee is up next, last in passing yards allowed and in explosive passes allowed, with Jacksonville a 14.5-point favorite. The real test will be the following Sunday, when the Jaguars travel to Chicago.

**Buffalo Bills (9-1).** There are two Buffalo defenses. The Bills allowed 28.5 points per game over their first six and just 9.25 over the last four, against Baltimore, Minnesota, the Jets and Miami. The offense runs the ball better than anyone but one team (2nd in rushing yards per game at 149, and 1st in runs of 10 or more yards), and Josh Allen has 11 rushing touchdowns, 9 interceptions and a -1.3 CPOE (22nd of 37). They're 3-0 against winning teams, 4th in point differential (+10.5 per game) and 3rd in SRS (+9.2), but only 6th in net EPA. Thursday night is where the new defense meets a top-5 offense by EPA per play. Buffalo is a 3-point favorite against the Chiefs.

## You're Killing Me, Smalls

**Kansas City Chiefs (8-2).** The numbers say Kansas City is the 2nd-best team in the NFL by net EPA and 4th by SRS (+7.9), with a +10.8 point differential per game (3rd). I'm not sure the numbers travel. Seven of the Chiefs' eight wins are over teams that are 4-7 or worse, and the eighth was Denver in Week 1. They're 1-2 against teams with winning records. The defense does something unusual (it allows touchdowns on 13.3% of drives and 37% of red-zone trips, both the best marks in the league), but the offense has an average throw depth of 6.3 yards for Patrick Mahomes, 35th of 37 qualifying quarterbacks. This is a team that went 6-11 last year and is now 2nd in net EPA, a leap that has come against the easiest schedule in the league. Thursday in Buffalo is their first game against a winning team since Week 8.

**Cincinnati Bengals (7-3).** The Bengals are Joe Burrow, end of story. He leads the league in completion rate (74.2%), has 29 touchdowns and is +6.5 in CPOE (3rd), and that's why Cincinnati is 5th in net EPA with the 2nd-best offense by EPA per play. The rest of the roster is a problem. The run game is 30th at 80 yards per game, and the run defense is 31st in yards per carry allowed (5.20). Washington found that out: the Commanders outrushed Cincinnati 150-32 and outgained them 428-313, and still needed a field goal with 4 seconds left to win 22-20 after going 2-for-10 on third down. The Bengals have the 2nd-easiest schedule in the league (opponents' combined win rate of .391, right behind Kansas City's .388) and they are 1-2 against winning teams.  They shouldn't be tested this week, when New Orleans visits as a 10-point underdog. The Saints are 28th in rushing yards allowed, which is the first chance in a while for the 30th-ranked run game to show up.

## Life Is Like a Box of Chocolates. You Never Know What You're Gonna Get

**Detroit Lions (8-2).** Detroit has won six straight, and their efficiency has followed. Net EPA per play is +0.187 over the last four games, compared with +0.012 before that. The offense is 4th in EPA per play and has the second-fewest giveaways in the league (7), which is how a defense that's 27th in EPA per play, and 26th against the pass, stays at 8-2. The Lions are 2-1 against winning teams, 6th in point differential (+6.8) and 6th in SRS (+5.9), and their defense has only 9 takeaways (27th). It makes Thursday a fair fight. The Bears are 2.5-point favorites in Detroit in a game with a total of 55.5, the highest on the Week 12 board.

**Chicago Bears (8-2).** On paper, Chicago and Detroit are the same team. The Bears are 7th in net EPA (+0.068) and the Lions are 8th (+0.066), a gap of two thousandths of a point per play. Chicago is just built backwards: the defense is 7th in EPA per play and 3rd in sack rate (8.5% of dropbacks), while the offense is 10th. Caleb Williams has started seven games and the team is 6-1 in them, and he's +6.2 in CPOE (4th among quarterbacks with 100 or more attempts). In the three games he missed, Case Keenum (-0.06 EPA per dropback) and Tyson Bagent (-0.07) played and the team went 2-1. Both of Chicago's losses came in the division: a 9-3 home loss to Minnesota in Week 2, where Williams threw 26 passes before Bagent finished the game, and a 31-7 loss at Green Bay in Week 5 with Keenum and Bagent splitting the snaps. That's why the Bears are 8-0 outside the division and 0-2 inside it. The risk is the 19 giveaways, among the six most in the league. 

**Los Angeles Rams (6-4).** The best team not leading or tied for leading anything. The Rams are 4th in net EPA (+0.154), with the 8th-ranked offense and the 4th-ranked defense by EPA per play, and 9th in SRS (+4.8). The defense is the strength, tied for 2nd in rushing EPA allowed, 4th in passing EPA allowed and 4th in yards per play allowed (4.67). Three of the four losses came by four or fewer points (30-26 at Denver, 37-34 to Buffalo and 18-16 to Arizona), and the fourth was the Week 1 loss to San Francisco, 27-7. They beat Arizona 34-6 four weeks after losing to them. They're 1-3 against winning teams, though, and Matthew Stafford (372 attempts, 19 touchdowns and 10 interceptions) is average, +0.4 in CPOE (18th of 34 qualifiers). The gap to San Francisco is three games, and the remaining chance to close it is Week 14 at San Francisco. First comes Green Bay, a 2.5-point underdog in Los Angeles on Wednesday night.

## Fasten Your Seatbelts, It's Going To Be A Bumpy Ride

The top six hold up: San Francisco, Jacksonville, Buffalo, Kansas City, Cincinnati and Detroit, in roughly that order. Then there's a cliff. The Giants (6-3-1) and Panthers (5-5) lead their divisions, but neither is a top-12 team by SRS or net EPA, and both rank behind Chicago, Los Angeles, Denver and Seattle. If I were building an NFL bracket today, those four would jump the Giants and the Panthers in a heartbeat.

Two storylines to watch, and both come down to Thursday. Kansas City at Buffalo tests both the schedule and the turnaround all at once: if the Chiefs win, the soft schedule stops being the whole story, and if they lose, Denver can take a full game lead on Friday against a spiraling Steeler team. They would also already have the tiebreaker in hand.  And Chicago at Detroit takes the NFC North's tie off the board. Whoever wins is 9-2, with the loser's only consolation prize being the rematch in Week 17.

---

*Method: game results, point differentials, EPA, CPOE and schedule data via nflverse/nflreadr. EPA is computed on pass and run plays and is not opponent-adjusted; net EPA is offensive EPA per play minus defensive EPA per play allowed. SRS is a point-margin rating adjusted for opponent strength, solved iteratively from game results through Week 11. Division tiebreakers follow the NFL's two-team procedure (head-to-head, division record, common games with a four-game minimum, conference record); strength of schedule is opponents' combined win rate. Ranks are out of 32 teams, and quarterback ranks are among qualifying passers. Week 12 spreads are the market's expectations, not ours. Data is through Week 11. Full writeup at [github.com/merrittocratic/boxscore-prophet](https://github.com/merrittocratic/boxscore-prophet).*
