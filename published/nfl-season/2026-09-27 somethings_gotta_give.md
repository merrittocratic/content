# Something's Gotta Give

Two weeks into the season and the storylines have already hardened. The Raiders are the quiet 2-0 team nobody's talking about.  The Saints might actually be *for real*.  And the Texans and Chargers are 0-2 with all four losses coming at home. Does any of it survive Week 3? The schedule makers helped us out: the Raiders and Saints play each other.

## Something's Gotta Give

Raiders at Saints is the rare game where both teams' best trait runs straight into the other team's worst one.

The Raiders' defense is #1 in the league in EPA per play allowed. Stat Nerd refresher: EPA is how many points a play adds or costs compared with an average play in the same down, distance, and field position. Opponents have had 23 drives and scored 3 touchdowns.  The catch is who they did it against: Miami's offense ranks 27th and the Chargers' ranks 30th.

The Saints' offense has jumped from 27th last season to 13th, and it earned that ranking against real competition. They took Detroit (9th in offensive EPA) to overtime, then beat Baltimore on the road as 8.5-point underdogs. Tyler Shough went 27-of-34 with a +14.1 CPOE in Baltimore, and Chris Olave has 268 yards on a 28% target share.

So one of these units has been tested and the other hasn't. That's the matchup to watch Sunday.

Flip it around and it gets even better. The Saints' defense allows a 53% success rate, 2nd-worst in the league, and ranks 27th against the run. Success rate is the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule. That should be a get-right spot for Ashton Jeanty, who's running for 3.4 yards per carry with a 32% success rate and has salvaged his fantasy line (43.0 PPR points) as a receiver. If Jeanty can't find daylight against *this* front, the run game is a real problem.

The market has the Saints as 3.5-point favorites in their home opener. Our model has already picked a side in this matchup: Olave was a high call both weeks and boomed both times, and Shough was a low call both weeks and cleared his start line both times. The model buys the receiver but not the guy throwing him the ball, and so far the quarterback is winning that argument. **[TK: Week 3 board numbers for Olave, Shough, Jeanty, Cousins]**

## Houston, We Have a Problem

"Rocky start" fits Houston's Week 2 a lot better than Week 1. Losing 36-31 to a Buffalo team with the league's #1 offense is not a red flag, and the Texans' offense was genuinely fine in that game. Week 2 was the problem: 6 points against Cincinnati, -0.25 EPA per play, and C.J. Stroud dropping back 57 times without a touchdown. Nico Collins didn't play, and reports this week say he's unlikely to face the Colts with his Grade 1 hamstring strain.

This is where the preview part gets interesting. The Colts are also 0-2, and their pass defense has been a sieve: a league-high 9.8 yards per attempt allowed and the most passing yards in the league. To be fair, they've faced Lamar Jackson and Patrick Mahomes. If Houston's passing game can't get right against this secondary, even without Collins, that's when you worry.

The run game is a different story. Houston ranks 28th in rushing EPA, and David Montgomery is averaging 2.7 yards per carry. I wouldn't expect that to fix itself.

Our model's best call of Week 2 came from this offense. With Collins out, the live news layer flagged extra targets for Dalton Schultz, the model ranked him 3rd against a market rank of 15th, and he boomed for 26.0 PPR. One wrinkle: Schultz was among the Texans who sat out Wednesday's practice. **[TK: Schultz status + Week 3 board number]** 

## Out of the Frying Pan

The Chargers lost 26-14 to Arizona as 8.5-point favorites and 26-14 to the Raiders as 6.5-point favorites. Two identical losses, both at home, both as big favorites, and neither opponent ranks higher than 20th in offensive EPA. The offense is 30th in EPA per play and dead last in rushing EPA. Justin Herbert's CPOE ranks 27th of 31 qualifiers.

Now they head to Buffalo, which is 2-0 with that #1 offense and coming off a Thursday game, so the Bills are the rested team. Both of the Chargers' top tight ends, David Njoku and Charlie Kolar, just landed on IR. The stakes: since 1990, teams that start 0-3 have made the playoffs 2.9% of the time.

If there's a crack of light for LA, it's the Bills' defense, which is allowing 31 points a game. But a Chargers offense that couldn't score on Arizona or Las Vegas isn't built to win a shootout, so I'd expect this narrative to persist.

This one's on us. We bought the Mike McDaniel hype in Week 1 and kept buying in Week 2. Herbert scored 13.3 at a 58% start chance, then 7.9 at 44%. We ranked Ladd McConkey and Tre Harris roughly 30 spots above the market, and both finished in the 6s. **[TK: did the Week 3 board finally cool on this offense?]**

---

Three games, four narratives, and they don't all get tested equally.  The Saints and Raiders settle something Sunday, one way or the other.  The Texans get a matchup that should tell us whether Week 2 was a blip.  The Chargers get the worst possible place to prove Week 2 was one.
