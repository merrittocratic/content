# Htrae - Bizzarro World

Two weeks in and we already have some **REAL** surprises. The scriptwriters are working overtime this year.  Earth spelled backwards is the only reference I could think of to explain that the Raiders are  2-0!  Somehow nobody's talking about it.  The Saints might also be *for real*.  What is happening??  The Texans and Chargers, perennial pre-season darlings, are both 0-2 with all four losses coming at home. 

What survives past Week 3?  At least we get an assist from the schedulers: the Raiders and Saints actually play each other this week.  Of course in Bizarro world, that game will probably be a 28-27 win for the Saints and we'll be no closer to ruling the Raiders or the Saints out as contenders.

## Something's Gotta Give

Raiders at Saints is the rare game where both teams' best trait runs straight into the other team's worst one.

The Raiders' defense is #1 in the league in EPA per play allowed. Stat Nerd refresher: EPA is the points a play adds or costs versus an average play in the same situation. Opponents have had 24 drives against them and scored just 3 touchdowns.  The catch, they did it against the Dolphins and the Chargers.  Offensive ranks 27th and 30th, respectively.  Not exactly the 80s 49ers, but they can only play the teams on their schedule.

The Saints' offense has jumped from 27th last season to 11th, and it earned that ranking against real competition. They took Detroit (8th in offensive EPA) to overtime, then beat Baltimore on the road as 8.5-point underdogs. Tyler Shough went 27-of-34 with a +14.1 completion percentage over expected (CPOE) in Baltimore, and Chris Olave has 268 yards on a 28% target share.

One of these units has been tested and the other hasn't.

Flip it around and it gets even better. The Saints' defense allows a 52% success rate, 2nd-worst in the league, and ranks 27th against the run. Success rate is the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule. This should be a get-right spot for Ashton Jeanty, who's running for just 3.4 yards per carry on a 32% success rate.

The market has the Saints by 3 in their home opener. Our model spent two weeks doubting Shough, giving him a 30% start chance in Week 1 and 23% in Week 2, and he cleared his 20-point start line both times. This week it finally came around. Shough is QB7 on our board against a consensus QB12, and his 38% start chance is his highest of the season. On Olave, the model and consensus agree at WR5. The model sees the Jeanty get-right spot too: his 59% start chance is 11 points above his own average. The biggest disagreement in this game is Alvin Kamara, RB25 for us and RB56 for consensus.  This comes after our news layer flagged him reclaiming the lead back role.

## Houston, We Have a Problem

"Rocky start" fits Houston's Week 2 a lot better than Week 1. Losing 36-31 to the league's #1 offense in Buffalo is not a red flag, and the Texans' offense was fine that day. Week 2 is where it gets ugly: 6 points against Cincinnati, -0.24 EPA per play. C.J. Stroud dropped back 57 times and threw for 357 yards and no touchdowns. Nico Collins didn't play in week 2, and reports this week say he's unlikely to face the Colts with his Grade 1 hamstring strain.

The Colts are also 0-2, and their pass defense has been a sieve: the most yards per attempt and passing yards allowed in the league.  To be fair, they've faced Lamar and Mahomes in those two games. If Houston's passing game can't one in the end zone aginst this secondary as 2.5-point road favorites, even without Collins, then it really is time to panic.

The run game is the real story for the Texans for all the wrong reasons. Houston ranks 26th in rushing EPA, and David Montgomery is averaging just 2.7 yards per carry.  This is not likely to fix itself.  

Our model's best call from Week 2 still came from this offense. With Collins out, our live news layer flagged extra targets for Dalton Schultz, the model ranked him TE4 against a consensus TE12, and he boomed for 26.0 PPR. This week the model is cooler than the market, at TE11 against a consensus TE6, and Schultz was among the Texans who sat out Wednesday's practice. Keep an eye on that one.

## No One Circles The Wagons Like The.... Chargers?

The Chargers lost 26-14 to Arizona as 8.5-point favorites and 26-14 to the Raiders as 6.5-point favorites. Neither opponent ranks higher than 20th in offensive EPA. The Chargers offense is 30th in EPA per play and dead last in rushing EPA. Justin Herbert's CPOE ranks 27th of 29 qualifying quarterbacks.  In a word, it's **BAD**!

Now they head to Buffalo, which is 2-0 with that #1 offense and 10 days of rest. Both of the Chargers' top tight ends, David Njoku and Charlie Kolar, just landed on IR. The market has LA as 7-point underdogs. Since 1990, 0-3 teams have made the playoffs only 2.9% of the time.  Even Colin Cowherd might have to sell his Chargers and Herbert stocks after this weekend.

If there's an ounce of hope for LA, it's the Bills' defense, which is allowing 31 points a game. But a Chargers offense that couldn't score on Arizona or Las Vegas isn't built to win a shootout, so I'd expect this narrative to persist.

Gotta own this one. The boxscore-prophet model had Herbert as QB1 in Week 1, three spots above consensus, and he scored 13.3. It still gave him a 44% start chance in Week 2, and he scored 7.9. In Week 2 we also ranked Ladd McConkey and Tre Harris about 23 spots above the market, and both finished in the 6s. The board has finally cooled. Herbert's start chance is down to 36%, his lowest of the season. McConkey has flipped from well above consensus to WR54 against a consensus WR18, and Omarion Hampton now sits below consensus too.

---

Three games, four narratives. The Saints and Raiders settle something Sunday, maybe.  The Texans get a matchup that should tell us whether Week 2 was a blip and the Chargers get the worst possible place to prove Week 2 was the anomaly.

*Method: pre-week model start probabilities and ranks vs. consensus rankings compared against actual PPR outcomes (start line: 15 for RB/WR, 20 for QB, 12 for TE).
