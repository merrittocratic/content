# The Leasts Of The East

When I pick out storylines to write about, I pick ones that interest me and not necessarily ones that are always front of mind in the mainstream sports media.  The NFC East is probably the most "covered" conference in all of football.  Most of that is due to the Cowboys, but the Eagles and Giants can suck the oxygen out of any room as well.  Right now the NFC East can't beat anybody, and it's worth talking about because the division winner could finish under .500. On the other end of the spectrum is the Super Bowl that America and the world needs to see.  It's been on my wish list for the last 4 years, and if Detroit and Buffalo meet in the Super Bowl this year, it will probably be the highest scoring game in Super Bowl history.  Both teams have high octane offenses, and defenses that can't stop anybody!  It's time America, we need this!

## Yes, But They Were All Bad

The last two weeks have been ugly for the NFC East. Outside the division, the NFC East went 0-6 in Weeks 5 and 6 and was outscored by 44 points. The one game that wasn't a loss for the division, was the time management debacle between the Giants and Commanders that ended in an OT tie.  In Week 6, all four teams lost: Dallas 23-13 to Green Bay, the Giants 34-27 to New Orleans, Philadelphia 25-14 to Carolina and Washington 23-17 at San Francisco.

So, how rare is this? Since 2002, only eight divisions have gone winless in inter-division play across Weeks 5 and 6.  And yes, I had to verify that I used inter correctly here.  By point differential, this one ranks eighth of the eight. The 2011 AFC South (0-7) and the 2020 NFC East (0-4) were worse. All four teams losing in a week has happened 51 times since 2002, about once every 18 weeks.  The NFC East is 5-11 outside the division, a 31% win rate, and has been outscored by 83 points. That's 25th-worst of 200 division-seasons since 2002, so they have room to get worse.  The 2020 NFC East won 14% of their games through Week 6, and the division winner finished 7-9. Right now, the Giants are the lead dogs at 3-2-1, and they are about to start their third quarterback of the season.  

Team by team, by net EPA:

- **Philadelphia** is 31st of 32 in net EPA. Its offense is 30th in EPA per play and scores just 17.7 points a game, and Jalen Hurts is out with a blood clot.
- **Dallas** is 25th in net EPA, with the league's 32nd-ranked defense by EPA.
- **Washington** is 24th in net EPA and has allowed 28.7 points a game, 29th in the league.
- **The Giants** are 22nd in net EPA, the least bad of the four.

Now the good news, if you can call it that. All of these bad teams get to play each other, so there are bound to be a few wins here. Week 7 is Dallas at Philadelphia, Week 8 is Philadelphia-Washington and Week 9 is Giants-Philadelphia, so these can't all end in ties, can they? The rest of the schedule is soft too. By their remaining opponents' net EPA, Dallas has the 30th-hardest schedule of 32, Philadelphia 29th, the Giants 27th and Washington 23rd. History is also on their side. The 10 worst divisions through Week 6 since 2002 on average won 46% of their non-division games the rest of the way, so none stayed this bad.

Monday night, Dallas visits Philadelphia as a 4.5-point favorite with a total of 44.5, which puts the Cowboys at about 24.5 points and the Eagles at about 20. It's the league's worst defense by EPA against the 30th-ranked offense by EPA per play, without Hurts. Our model likes Tanner McKee more than consensus does: a 28% chance of a startable week (QB16, against an Expert Consensus Ranking of QB20). It also likes George Pickens against the Eagles: a 51% chance (WR10, against a consensus WR18). Dak Prescott is a touch below the market at 35% (QB10, against a consensus QB8).

##  Aw, Buckle This! Ludicrous Speed, Go!

Buffalo and Detroit both score 32.8 points a game, 1st and 2nd in the league. But, neither can defend: 30th and 31st in EPA allowed, while allowing close to 29 points per game each. Detroit's games average 62.4 total points, the most in the league, and Buffalo's average 61.3, 2nd.  We've seen this matchup once already and the atmosphere for the opening of the new HighMark Stadium was electric. Buffalo won 41-31 in Week 2, a 72-point game. The Super Bowl record total is 75, from Super Bowl XXIX (49ers 49, Chargers 26). 

On paper, nothing since 2002 comes close. Average the two teams' game totals and you get about 62. The highest figure for any Super Bowl pair since 2002 is 52.6, Tampa Bay and Kansas City after the 2020 season.  Here's the catch: Super Bowls between the highest-scoring teams often don't produce high scoring games. Of the five pairs with the highest averages since 2002 all but one would have hit on the under, at 40, 16, 51, 38 and **62** points. 

So a Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the most explosive matchup on paper since at least 2002. But, breaking the record would still take a top-2% game for both offenses, in exactly the setting where high-powered matchups have most often fallen short.  Not saying either team should be favored to make the Super Bowl, but a guy can dream, right?  

---

The NFC East's slump is real, but the upcoming schedule just may be able to bail it out. A Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the best-looking shootout on paper in a generation, which, historically, is the best reason to bet the under.
