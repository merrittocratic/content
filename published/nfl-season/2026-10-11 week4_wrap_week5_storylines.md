# That's Offensive!

Defense wins championships! That poster hangs in every high school weight room in America.  But, does it really?  Four weeks into 2026, Jacksonville looks like real contender, Atlanta went from a 34-3 home beatdown to hanging 45 on the Saints, and head coaches who came up calling offensive plays are 21-6 against the ones who came up calling defenses.  

## Duuuuval!

The Jaguars are 3-1 and 2nd in the league in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed).  Only the 49ers rank higher. The offense is the real deal. It finished 14th in EPA per play last year and sits 4th now, scoring on 51% of its drives (3rd) and turning 86% of its red zone trips into touchdowns (2nd). Trevor Lawrence is the biggest single change. His completion percentage over expected (CPOE) went from 30th of 42 qualifying quarterbacks last year to 5th of 35 this year.  He is slinging it! The only loss was a 20-13 grinder in Denver, and two of their three wins were by 20-plus points.

The defense is where you have to start asking questions. "Do ya feel lucky, punk?  Well, do ya?" The run defense is elite, allowing 64 rushing yards a game (2nd). The pass defense is not. It gives up 8.5 yards per attempt (30th) and 292 passing yards a game (31st). What's holding it together is a league-leading six interceptions.  Strip out the turnover plays and the defense drops from 7th in EPA to 21st.  A shaky foundation for a Super Bowl contender.  

Sunday brings the Eagles, 2-2 but 31st in net EPA, and the market expects Jacksonville by 7. Philly has four sacks all season (28th), so Lawrence should have all day to throw. Jalen Hurts ranks 28th of 35 in CPOE, and his offense has just ten completions of 20-plus yards (22nd) all season, so this isn't the passing attack that exposes a leaky secondary.

Contender? The offense says yes. The defense says ask again when the interceptions stop falling from the sky.

## We're contenders now!

Atlanta opened 0-2, capped by that 34-3 loss to Carolina with five turnovers. Going into Green Bay last Thursday, there were numerous analysts calling them the worst team in the league.  Then they put 35 on Packers and 45 on New Orleans. What changed? The guy under center. Cooper Rush started Weeks 1 and 2 and posted a CPOE of -21 in the opener, and he and Jack Strand combined for five interceptions in those first two games. Michael Penix Jr., still working back from last year's torn ACL, didn't take a snap until Week 3. Offensive EPA per play went -0.32, -0.47, then +0.33 and +0.42. In Week 4, Penix ranked 2nd among 32 qualifying quarterbacks in EPA per dropback.

Bijan Robinson took off right alongside him: 194 rushing yards against the Packers, 145 against the Saints, and 1.49 rush yards over expected per carry, 2nd of 51 qualifying backs. The defense was never the problem. It's 1st in the league in both rushing yards and yards per carry allowed, and 4th in EPA even after you strip out the turnovers.

Now for the turn. Those two wins came against the 26th and 30th ranked teams in net EPA. Baltimore should be a real test, if Lamar plays. The Ravens are 3rd in offensive EPA and Lamar leads the league at 10.0 yards per attempt, though they've built those numbers against four defenses averaging 28th in EPA. He got hurt on a sack on the last snap before halftime against Tennessee, and multiple reports give him only an outside chance to play Sunday. Tyler Huntley was efficient in relief, 8 of 9, but Baltimore went scoreless in the second half and a 24-7 lead turned into a 24-18 nail-biter. Either way, the matchup to watch is Bijan against a Ravens run defense that's 22nd in EPA allowed but 11th in yards per carry. The market expects Atlanta by 3.5, on a short week after Monday night.

## A Beautiful Mind

Do offensive minds really win more? I sorted every head coach from 2021 to 2025 by which side of the ball they coached before becoming head coaches (the dates were chosen to avoid capturing COVID noise).  The standings say yes. Offensive-path coaches win 53% compared to 47% for defensive coaches.  Teams with offensive-path coaches made the playoffs 49% of the time against 35%, and won four straight Super Bowls from 2021 to 2024: McVay, Reid twice, and Sirianni.

Here's the part that should bother a GM. When you hire a defensive head coach, you're presumably buying a better defense. Not happening! Since 2021, teams with defensive-minded head coaches have finished with a top-10 defense 29% of the time, while teams with offensive-minded head coaches did it 33% of the time. Their average defensive rank is identical, 16.4. Meanwhile the offensive guys deliver what they were hired for: a top-10 offense 44% of the time, against just 15% for the defensive guys. And it isn't because they inherited better teams. First-year hires on both sides took over teams with .340 win percentages the year prior. Last season's Super Bowl was Mike Macdonald's Seahawks over Mike Vrabel's Patriots, so defense can still win a title. 

Which brings us back to 2026, where the offensive coaches are 21-6 head-to-head and beating the market's expectation by almost three points a game. That's the biggest gap in six seasons, but five years of history say expect it to shrink. Liam Coen came up on the offensive side and his offense is Jacksonville's engine. Kevin Stefanski came up calling plays, and it shows. Michael Penix Jr. ranked 22nd in EPA per dropback last season under Raheem Morris; in two games with Stefanski, he's been one of the most efficient passers in the league. Kirk Cousins tells the same story from across the country: 26th in his last year under Morris, 10th now in Las Vegas under Klint Kubiak. Small samples and soft opponents, but two quarterbacks Morris couldn't get going are both finding it with offensive minds running the show.  This year there are 9 of 32 head coaches who came up on defense, four of them at 0-4

---

Jacksonville's offense is real, but its defense is living on interceptions. Atlanta found its quarterback and its running game, but hasn't beaten a good team yet. And offensive-minded coaches are winnning more, period.

*Method: team efficiency, drive outcomes, turnover-adjusted EPA and opponent strength via nflverse/nflreadr; CPOE (40-attempt minimum) and rush yards over expected via NFL Next Gen Stats; Week 5 spreads are the market's expectations, not ours.
