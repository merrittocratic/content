# Offense Sells Tickets...

...and defense wins championships. That poster hangs in every high school weight room in America. Four weeks into 2026, Jacksonville is selling tickets, Atlanta went from a 34-3 home beatdown to hanging 45 on the Saints, and head coaches who came up calling offensive plays are 23-9 against the ones who came up calling defenses. Let's see which of these survive a spreadsheet.

## Do You Feel Lucky, Duval?

The Jaguars are 3-1 and 2nd in the league in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed).  Only the 49ers rank higher. The offense is the real deal. It finished 14th in EPA per play last year and sits 4th now, scoring on 51% of its drives (3rd) and turning 86% of its red zone trips into touchdowns (2nd). Trevor Lawrence is the biggest single change. His completion percentage over expected (CPOE) went from 30th of 42 qualifying quarterbacks last year to 5th of 35 this year.  So, he is slinging it! The only loss was a 20-13 grinder in Denver, and two of the three wins were by 20-plus points.

The defense is where you have to starts asking questions. "Do ya feel lucky, punk?  Well, do ya?" The run defense is elite, allowing 64 rushing yards a game (2nd). The pass defense is not. It gives up 8.5 yards per attempt (30th) and 292 passing yards a game (31st). What's holding it together is a league-leading six interceptions.  Strip out the turnover plays and the defense drops from 7th in EPA to 21st.  A shaky foundation for Super Bowl contender.  

Sunday brings the Eagles, 2-2 but 31st in net EPA, and the market expects Jacksonville by 7. Philly has four sacks all season (28th), so Lawrence should have all day to throw. Jalen Hurts ranks 28th of 35 in CPOE, and his offense has just ten completions of 20-plus yards (22nd) all season, so this isn't the passing attack that exposes a leaky secondary.

Contender? The offense says yes. The defense says ask again when the interceptions stop falling from the sky.

## The Return of the King

Atlanta opened 0-2, capped by that 34-3 loss to Carolina with five turnovers. Going into Green Bay last Thursday, there were numerous analysts calling them the worst team in the league.  Then they put 35 on Packers and 45 on New Orleans. What changed? The guy under center. Cooper Rush started Weeks 1 and 2 and posted a CPOE of -21 in the opener, and he and Jack Strand combined for five interceptions in those first two games. Michael Penix Jr., still working back from last year's torn ACL, didn't take a snap until Week 3. Offensive EPA per play went -0.32, -0.47, then +0.33 and +0.42. In Week 4, Penix ranked 2nd among 32 qualifying quarterbacks in EPA per dropback.

Bijan Robinson took off right alongside him: 194 rushing yards against the Packers, 145 against the Saints, and 1.49 rush yards over expected per carry, 2nd of 51 qualifying backs. The defense was never the problem. It's 1st in the league in both rushing yards and yards per carry allowed, and still 4th in EPA after you strip out the turnovers.

Now for the turn. Those two wins came against the 26th and 30th ranked teams in net EPA. Baltimore is a real test. The Ravens are 3rd in offensive EPA and Lamar Jackson leads the league at 10.0 yards per attempt, though they've built those numbers against four defenses averaging 28th in EPA. The matchup to watch is Bijan against a Ravens run defense that's 22nd in EPA allowed but 11th in yards per carry. The market expects Atlanta by 3.5, on a short week after Monday night.

## Defense Wins Championships... Right?

Do offensive minds really win more? I sorted every head coach from 2021 to 2025 (skipping the COVID season) by which side of the ball they coached before landing the big chair.

The standings say yes. Offensive-path coaches went .527, defensive-path coaches .470. Offensive-path teams made the playoffs 49% of the time against 35%, and won four straight Super Bowls from 2021 to 2024: McVay, Reid twice, and Sirianni.

Then it falls apart. Measured against what the market expected each week, both groups beat it by the exact same 0.08 points per game. The offensive guys aren't out-coaching anybody, they've just had better rosters. Treat each coach as one data point and the gap isn't statistically meaningful. And defensive-minded head coaches don't even build better defenses: both groups average an identical 16.4 rank in defensive EPA. Last season's Super Bowl was Mike Macdonald's Seahawks over Mike Vrabel's Patriots, two defensive coaches.

Which brings us back to 2026, where the offensive coaches are 23-9 head-to-head and beating the market's expectation by almost two points a game. That's the biggest gap in six seasons, and five years of history say expect it to shrink. Liam Coen came up on the offensive side and his offense is Jacksonville's engine. Raheem Morris came up on defense, and his defense is the one constant in Atlanta.

---

Jacksonville's offense is real, and its defense is living on interceptions. Atlanta found its quarterback and its running game, but hasn't beaten a good team yet. And offensive-minded coaches win more because they tend to have better teams, not because they coach circles around anyone. Offense sells tickets. Defense, it turns out, doesn't care who's wearing the headset.

---

*Method: team efficiency, drive outcomes, turnover-adjusted EPA and opponent strength via nflverse/nflreadr; CPOE (40-attempt minimum) and rush yards over expected via NFL Next Gen Stats; Week 5 spreads are the market's expectations, not ours. Head coaches classified by the side of the ball they coached before their first head coaching job, 2021-2025 regular seasons plus playoffs, with interim coaches assigned game by game; "against the market" is final margin minus the closing spread. Start chances from Boxscore Prophet's pre-kickoff runs. Full writeup at [github.com/merrittocratic/boxscore-prophet](https://github.com/merrittocratic/boxscore-prophet).*
