# Always Watching

This year, the NFL season belongs to the North Men. Both the NFC and AFC North Divisions are making a run at the title. The Divisions are a combined 29-8 against everybody else, and they're the only two divisions in the league without a losing team. The Monsters of the North! But, are they really? A record tells you how many you beat. It doesn't tell you who you played, and nobody has played an easier slate than the Norths. So are the teams from the North more like the scare master "Sulley", or Mike Wazowski.

## I Coulda Been A Contender.

Let's start with the case for the monsters. The NFC North is 15-3 outside the division and the AFC North is 14-5. The only other division above .500 is the NFC West at .556. The real question, who were those wins for the "Norths" against? The average non-division opponent for both Norths ranks about 20th in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed). Every other division average opponent ranks between 14th and 16th. Against teams in the top 12 in net EPA, the two Norths are a combined 1-4. 

Hardest divisions to win? Absolutely, especially the AFC North, where all four teams are within a game of each other: Cleveland and Pittsburgh at 5-2, Cincinnati at 4-2 and Baltimore at 4-3. Best divisions though? Not so much. And once you get inside them, a few of these records start to look even fishier.

## Don't Sell Yourself Short Judge, You're A Tremendous Slouch

Three teams have records that are writing checks their play can't cash.

Minnesota is 6-0, and the defense is the real deal. The Vikings are 1st in EPA per play allowed, 1st in sack rate (9.9%), and opponents score touchdowns on just 23% of their red-zone trips, the lowest rate in the league. The offense, on the other hand, is just along for the ride. It's 30th in EPA per play and 32nd in success rate, and Kyler Murray's 85.8 passer rating ranks 22nd of 32 qualifying QBs.

Here's the part that might sting in Minnesota. The Vikings went with Murray over J.J. McCarthy, and then they effectively gave McCarthy away to the Giants. In Weeks 6 and 7, J.J. averaged 10.1 yards per attempt with a 95.2 passer rating and +10.6 passing EPA. Kyler has only managed 6.5 yards per attempt and -8.4 passing EPA for the season. It's two games, but right now the quarterback Minnesota moved on from is running an offense better than the one it kept.

Minnesota is 4-0 in one-score games, and its strength of schedule ranks 30th: five of its six opponents rank 12th or worse in net EPA. Great defense, soft schedule and a pinch of luck has led the way for their 6-0 start.

Cleveland's record is the fishiest of the bunch. The Browns are 5-2 with a -13 point differential, and they rank 18th in net EPA. They're 5-1 in one-score games, three of them by exactly three points. Deshaun Watson's Completion Percentage Over Expected (CPOE) of -4.5 ranks 31st of 32 qualifying quarterbacks. The Browns defense that ranks 8th in EPA allowed keeps them in games, but close-game luck is doing ALOT of work, and luck has a way of running out.

Detroit is 4-2 but ranks just 21st in net EPA. Last week I said a Lions-Bills Super Bowl would be the best-looking shootout on paper in a generation, and nothing has changed. Jared Goff leads all qualifying QBs with a 112.4 passer rating, and the offense ranks 3rd in EPA per play. The problem: the defense ranks 31st in EPA allowed and gives up a league-worst 4.8 completions of 20-plus yards a game. Three of the Lions' four wins came by one score, so they're the exact opposite of the Vikings. The Lions are an elite offense covering for a bottom-two defense, without much margin to spare.

## I Fear You Are Underestimating The Sneakiness

Then there are the records that may be underselling how the good the team is.

Cincinnati might be the best team in either North, and they're sitting third in their own division. The Bengals are 4-2 but rank 3rd in the NFL in net EPA, up from 28th last year, and their +41 point differential leads the division by 26. Joe Burrow is cooking! He has 15 touchdowns, three interceptions and a 110.0 passer rating, 2nd among qualifiers, with eight touchdowns and no picks in the last three weeks. If you're picking a monster out of this group, pick the one in third place.

Chicago is 5-2 and 8th in net EPA, and both of their losses came inside the division: 9-3 at Minnesota, and 31-7 to Green Bay in a game Caleb Williams missed. Williams ranks 6th in passer rating (106.5) and 3rd in CPOE (+5.8).

The other three are closer to what their records say. Pittsburgh (5-2, 16th in net EPA) wins with the run game and a defense that ranks 6th in EPA allowed, despite Aaron Rodgers' passing. Green Bay (4-3, 12th in net EPA) has the league's worst run game and the most takeaways. And Baltimore (4-3, 10th in net EPA) is two different teams: 3-1 with Lamar Jackson, 1-2 with Tyler Huntley.

## Let's Get Ready To Rumble!

Week 8 brings the first real test for the 6-0 Vikings. Minnesota visits Detroit, the market expects the Lions by 3, and by EPA it's best on best and worst on worst: the league's top defense against its 3rd-best offense, and its 30th-ranked offense against its 31st-ranked defense. Our model is fading Goff hard, with a 29% chance of a startable week, down from 49% in his games so far, while the market has him at QB12. If Murray and the Vikings can't score on a defense this bad, the 6-0 has to have a ceiling.

The Norths are deep, not dominant. Two of the division leaders, Minnesota and Cleveland, are built on soft schedules and close wins, and the best team in either division appears to be a 4-2 Bengals team sitting in third place. Records tell you who won. Check who they played.

*Method: game results, point differentials, EPA, success rate, CPOE, passer rating and strength of schedule via nflverse/nflreadr. Week 8 spreads are the market's expectations, not ours. Start chances from Boxscore Prophet's Monday run, compared against the market's rankings; no injury report was out at run time.
