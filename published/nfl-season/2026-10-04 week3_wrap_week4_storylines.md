# The Good, The Bad, The UGLY

Three weeks in and we're still trying to sort out who is really good and who may just be really bad. Buffalo and Kansas City are both 3-0, so naturally the AFC is a two-team race again. Houston and Dallas have one win between them and meet Sunday in a Texas-sized October "elimination game." And Tampa Bay is 0-3, so something must have gone horribly wrong. Let's check the receipts.

## I Am Not So Inevitable?

Are we destined for another 13 seconds classic?  Before we go ahead and set this matchup now, it should be note that neither the Chiefs nor the Bills are the AFC's best by net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed). Jacksonville is 2nd in the league, Kansas City is 3rd, Las Vegas (yes, they are also 3-0!) is 4th, and Buffalo is 8th. 

KC is the real deal, and the defense is why: they're allowing just 16.7 points and 261 yards per game, both 3rd in the league. The offense has reinvented itself around that defense and a revived running game. Mahomes is throwing just 5.8 yards downfield on average, which is down from 8.1 last year, and he ranks dead last of 37 quarterbacks in throws into tight coverage. Kenneth Walker III is doing some serious heavy lifting, 1st in the league in rush yards over expected.

Buffalo is shakier ground. The offense is legit: 33.7 points per game (1st) and the league's best explosive-play rate. But the defense ranks 25th, gave up 31 to both Houston and Detroit, and the Bills needed to survive five turnovers to beat the Chargers. We gave Allen a 67% chance to clear the fantasy start bar in that one, and he only scored 16.5 points. Honest miss. James Cook has been the hit, clearing his start bar both weeks we liked him.

Week 4 is KC's first real test: at Las Vegas, the league's best pass defense with a +5 turnover margin. One 3-0 record has to give in that matchup. Our model has Mahomes at a 50% start chance against a 44% baseline, and likes Rashee Rice (model rank 8, Expert Consensus Ranking 15) more than Travis Kelce (model rank 9, ECR 3). Buffalo gets New England, whose run defense stuffs runs at the line less than anyone's. Cook sits at a 69% start chance and should continue to eat.

KC looks like a contender built to last. Buffalo looks like a great offense living on a borrowed defense.

## Everything Is Bigger In Texas, Including The Problems

Houston's three losses came by 21 points combined, and since Buffalo shredded it in the opener, the defense has been elite.

The offense is the problem, and it isn't new: 24th in EPA per play after finishing 26th last year. The run game is the worst part, stuffed at or behind the line on 27.3% of carries, the league's worst rate. And here's what surprised me: C.J. Stroud isn't the issue. He's completing passes above expectation with zero interceptions. Without Nico Collins, his receivers just aren't getting open. Xavier Hutchinson has 19.4% of the targets and ranks 93rd of 105 in separation.

Dallas is the exact opposite. Its offense turns 59.3% of drives into points, best in the league, and CeeDee Lamb has cleared his start bar all three weeks. The defense is dead last in EPA per play and gives up 148 rushing yards a game.

So the "season on the line" framing is half right. Since 2002, 0 of 68 teams that started 0-4 made the playoffs. Teams at 1-3 made it 13.5% of the time, roughly 1 in 7. Houston is the only team to make the playoffs from 0-3 in that span, and they did it twice. Nobody has climbed out of 0-4.

This is the get-right spot unless Joey Porter Jr. can come in and help the Cowboy defense. The most-stuffed run game in the league faces the worst run defense. If Houston can't run it against the Cowboys, it's time to panic. Our model has Stroud at a 41% start chance against a 30% baseline (model rank 7, ECR 17), one of its biggest disagreements with the market. Full disclosure: we were higher than ECR on Stroud in Weeks 2 and 3, and he missed both times.

## When You're Ugly And Someone Loves You, You Know They Love You

Tampa Bay's losses were 27-33, 19-23 and 16-23. Three one-score games, 17 points total. The defense is 5th in EPA per play. That side of the ball is not broken.

The offense?  It's every bit as bad as advertised. Four touchdowns on 37 drives, with 27% of those drives ending in a turnover. That's why a defense ranked 6th in yards allowed is 23rd in points allowed: the offense keeps handing opponents short fields. Baker Mayfield is dead last of 27 quarterbacks with at least 60 dropbacks in EPA per dropback, and it isn't one bad game: all three had serious red flags. His accuracy is fine. The sacks and turnovers are killing him.  Not the trend you want for your offense: ranked 5th in EPA per play in 2024, slipped to 19th in 2025, and sits at -0.20 per play now.

It looks like it will be the Jalon Daniels show for a couple of weeks due to Baker's thumb injury. The good news for Tampa: Green Bay can't run either (32nd in yards per carry), which puts the game on Jordan Love, who ranks 35th of 37 QBs in completing passes above expectation. The Battle of the Bays is setting up to be VERY ugly.

---

Three storylines, three half-truths. KC is a contender. Buffalo is an offense looking for a defense. Houston should panic, Dallas shouldn't, and Tampa's offense is plain broken.  The record tells you who's winning, but it doesn't tell you why, and the why is what lasts. Sunday gives us three tests: KC against a real defense in Vegas, Houston against a run defense that can't stop anyone, and Tampa Bay with a backup playing against the other Bay whose quarterback is playing like one.
