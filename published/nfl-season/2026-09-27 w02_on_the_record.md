# On the Record: Week 2

**The grade: the model was too sure of itself, and not by a little.**

Every week the model puts a chance on every player to have a startable week, and every week I grade those chances in public once the games are over. Week 2 is the first week that grading really stings, so let's get into it.

## Great Expectations

Start with the whole week. Among the 342 players who had a published chance and actually took the field, the model's stated chances added up to about 74 startable weeks. The real number was 42. That's a little more than half of what the model expected. If the chances are honest, those two numbers should land close together. They didn't.

Broken out by how confident the model was:

- When it said "better than a coin flip" (58% on average), it hit 12 of 23, right around half. That was the closest band of the week, and it still came up a bit short.
- When it said "about one in three" (35% on average), it hit about one in four (24%).
- When it said "about one in six" (16% on average), it hit about one in 17 (6%).
- When it called someone a real longshot (8% on average), exactly one of 66 came through.

Every band came in under what was stated. This wasn't one ugly game dragging the numbers down. The model was too generous up and down the board, and the gap only got worse the further down it went. Players who didn't suit up are left out of these numbers entirely. Counting them as misses would make the bottom bands look even worse.

## Running on Empty

The three biggest misses were all running backs the model gave better than even chances.

- **De'Von Achane (MIA vs. SF):** a 70% chance at a startable week, the most confident call the model made all week. He finished with 12.3 points, short of the 15-point bar.
- **Javonte Williams (DAL vs. WAS):** 64%. He finished with 8.0.
- **Bijan Robinson (ATL vs. CAR):** 62%. He finished with 11.1.

The same top tier also produced startable weeks from Jonathan Taylor, Jahmyr Gibbs, James Cook and Derrick Henry, which is why the top band landed near half instead of falling apart. But "near half" is still under the seven-in-ten and six-in-ten chances the model put on those names, and I'm not going to dress that up as a win.

## Out of Nowhere

Two players the model had written off came through anyway:

- **Cody White (LV vs. LAC):** an 8% chance, roughly one in 12. He finished with 16.4 points.
- **Drew Lock (SEA vs. ARI):** 11%, about one in nine. He finished with 21.4, clearing the 20-point bar for quarterbacks.

Longshots are supposed to land sometimes. This week, though, they landed less often than stated, not more. So White and Lock are the exceptions, not evidence that the bottom of the board is well tuned.

## Versus the Room

One call that did hold up against consensus: Dalton Schultz. With Nico Collins out, the model ranked him the third-best tight end of the week while FantasyPros' expert consensus had him 15th (Data: FantasyPros ECR). The model gave him a 45% chance at a startable week, a little less than a coin flip. He blew past that with 26.0 points, a boom week.

## Still On the Board

Monday night's Giants-Rams game didn't make it into this week's grading. Those chances were locked before kickoff and will be graded as-is in next week's receipts.
