# Round Up The Usual Suspects

I'll own my bias up front. I'm a Buckeye fan, so the idea of not making the CFB this year hurts.  As rabid a fan as I am though, there is no universe where I can say that Ohio State should be ranked above Oregon as long as we are in the same conference, with the same record, and a head-to-head loss.  Apparently, this same logic doesn't hold for the committee.  

Every Tuesday night in November the playoff committee hands out a new ranking, and every Wednesday I find out what I'm mad about. It isn't Georgia at No. 1 or unbeaten Utah at No. 2, it's what comes next. Alabama is 3rd and Tennessee is 9th, both at 8-2, with Tennessee having beaten Alabama by 16 just over a month ago. For reference, the Buckeyes loss to the Ducks was only by 13. Kentucky, which has lost by 28 points twice, is still 16th. And four 6-4 SEC teams are ranked, when the previous record for ranked 6-4 teams at the third release of the cfb ranking was 1. This prompted me to start looking for cases where the committee is playing favorites. Some of it holds up, and some of it doesn't.

## Frankly, My Dear, I Don't Give A Damn

Start with Alabama at 3 and Tennessee at 9. On Oct. 17 the Volunteers beat the Crimson Tide 26-10 in Knoxville, and the box score undersells it. Tennessee outgained Alabama 347-201, while Alabama went 2-of-13 on third down, ran for 54 yards at 2.1 a carry, turned it over four times and allowed five sacks. Its success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule) was 31% to Tennessee's 46%. The score even flatters the Tide: its only touchdown was a one-play, 14-yard drive after an interception, and Tennessee kicked four field goals. It wasn't a rout until late, though. Tennessee led 19-10 until a touchdown with 3:17 left, and its own offense was below zero in EPA per play (points added versus an average play in that situation).

The committee's case for Alabama is the schedule and the ratings. The Tide are ahead of the Vols in SP+ (6th vs. 11th), in FPI (4th vs. 11th, ESPN's version of SP+) and in net EPA (14th vs. 19th, offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed), and its opponents averaged +10.3 in SP+ to Tennessee's +6.9, which included Furman, Kennesaw State and 3-7 Georgia Tech. But the SP+ gap is only 1.4 points, and neither team has done much with its schedule. Against SP+ top-40 opponents, Alabama is 2-2 and Tennessee is 1-2, and their net EPA in those games ranks 15th and 14th among the committee's top 16. A sliver of a rating edge against a 16-point loss on the field is a wash, so a six-spot gap isn't defensible in either direction. The AP has them 7th and 9th, and I think the AP has it right. 

The bigger problem is that Alabama sits ahead of Notre Dame and Miami. Miami is better than the Tide in SP+ (3rd), FPI (3rd), strength of record (3rd, how hard a record was to earn) and net EPA (9th). Notre Dame is better in SP+ (2nd), FPI (2nd) and net EPA (3rd). Both are 9-1, and both are behind Alabama. A quick explanation about strength of record. This metric asks how hard it was to earn a record, not how hard the schedule was. Alabama's schedule is harder (6th in ESPN's schedule strength to Miami's 27th), but it has one more loss, and a second loss costs more than that schedule gap gives back.

## The greatest trick the devil ever pulled was convincing the world he didn't exist.

The best argument for the SEC is its non-conference record. The SEC is 38-2 and the Big Ten is 43-11. Against Power 4 opponents (the other power conferences plus Notre Dame), the SEC is 10-2 and the Big Ten is 4-10, and the Big Ten had 8 of those 14 games at home, compared to the SEC's 5.

The Big Ten does have a point about the schedule. Five of its 14 Power 4 opponents were SP+ top-10 teams, compared to only two of the SEC's 12.  Three of those five Big Ten opponents were Notre Dame, which beat Purdue, Michigan State and Wisconsin by an average of 28. Outside the top 10, the Big Ten went 4-5 and the SEC went 9-1, including home losses by Illinois to Duke, Maryland to Virginia Tech and Minnesota to Mississippi State.

But the SEC's 10-2 is thinner than it looks. It went 4-0 in games decided by four points or fewer: Texas over Ohio State by 1, Auburn over Baylor by 1, Ole Miss over Louisville by 3 and Vanderbilt over NC State by 4. Flip those four and the SEC is 6-6. So the Big Ten played the harder slate and still lost most of the games that mattered, and the SEC's record is a reason to respect it, but a poor reason to move Alabama over teams that beat it in every computer rating.

Does that mean the Big Ten is getting stiffed? Not at 13 through 15. The committee has Oregon 13th, Ohio State 14th and Iowa 15th, which is close to the order the resumes give you. Oregon, 13th in the committee's ranking, is 7-3 and 16th in strength of record, with a wins-over-expected figure of +0.28 (actual wins minus what a generic top-12 team would win against the same schedule, my own calculation) to Ohio State's +0.08 and Iowa's -0.03. Oregon won at Ohio State 41-28, and the Buckeyes are 2-3 against SP+ top-25 teams, and are 4th in SP+, but the head-to-head losses to Texas and Oregon just mean more. 

## Just When I Thought I Was Out, They Pull Me Back In

After Texas lost to Florida, I wrote that one more loss would probably end the Longhorns' playoff hopes. They haven't lost since, and the committee now has them 5th, three spots above the AP's 8th. The case for it is real: Texas is 4-0 against SP+ top-25 teams (Ohio State, Tennessee, Ole Miss and LSU), tied with Oregon for the most among the 16.

The case against it is everything else. Texas is 108th in offensive EPA, 93rd in yards per game and 35th in net EPA, it is 4-2 in one-score games, and it lost to two 6-4 teams, Oklahoma and Florida. Notre Dame at 6th is 2nd in SP+, 2nd in FPI and 3rd in net EPA, against Texas' 8th, 6th and 35th. That's a wins-only resume, and I wouldn't put the Longhorns ahead of the Irish or the Hurricanes.

Earlier this month I called Kentucky college football's version of the Minnesota Vikings: 7-1, 6th in the committee's first ranking and 2nd in the country in strength of record, while every efficiency measure had the Wildcats between 14th and 49th. Two losses later they're 7-3 and somehow still 16th. They're 42nd in SP+ and 63rd in net EPA, and they've lost by 28 to Alabama, by 28 to Tennessee and then to Florida. The AP has them 17th, so the committee isn't alone in being wrong on this one.

It matters because a ranked Kentucky gives Alabama and Tennessee a "ranked win" apiece, and so do the four 6-4 SEC teams the committee ranks (South Carolina, Oklahoma, Florida and Ole Miss, 24th to 47th in SP+). LSU is also 6-4 and 9th in SP+, and it's unranked, as is Nebraska (6-4, 18th in SP+, with losses to Indiana and Oregon by 3 each). I won't call it SEC loyalty, because LSU is an SEC team sitting at home. What I can see is that Oklahoma and Florida each beat Texas and South Carolina beat Tennessee. If Alabama gets so much credit for barely losing to Georgia, shouldn't Nebraska get a similar boost?

---

Georgia is fine, Utah is unbeaten and 1st in strength of record, and the Big Ten has no real complaint. The problem is spots 3 through 9: two 8-2 SEC teams placed above Notre Dame and Miami, the teams the computers like better, with a ranked-win ledger padded by Kentucky and four 6-4 SEC teams. Respect the SEC, but the committee really needs to show its work.
