# I'm Not Bad. I'm Just Drawn That Way.

Every time Iowa comes up in a playoff conversation, someone brings up the schedule. And on paper they have a point: the Hawkeyes' opponents have a combined win percentage of .500, which ranks 99th of 139 FBS teams. But one or two games on either end can drag a schedule around, so I took seven teams sitting between 3rd and 14th in the CFP rankings (Iowa, Alabama, Texas, Tennessee, Texas Tech, Oregon and Indiana), ranked each one's opponents by record, dropped the two best and the two worst, and graded the middle.

I went in with two theories: Iowa's schedule isn't as soft as its reputation, and once you trim the outliers, these seven teams all look about the same. One of those holds up.

## Is This Heaven? No, It's Iowa.

Trim the ends and Iowa's schedule jumps from 99th to 50th in FBS in opponent win percentage, at .557 for the middle seven. The FBS median is .543, so the middle of Iowa's schedule is ordinary, not weak. It's level with Texas Tech (.561) and ahead of Tennessee (.543). The Vols drop the most, from 51st to 63rd in opponent win percentage, because the trim removes Texas and Alabama.

The Hawkeyes did their part in that middle, too. They went 6-1 with four wins over teams with winning records, which matches Texas Tech and beats Tennessee's three. So the first theory holds: the bottom of Iowa's schedule made the whole schedule look worse.

It just doesn't hold all the way. One of those seven middle games is a 55-0 win over Northern Iowa, an FCS team. Take it out and Iowa is 5-1 against the other six with an average margin of +10.3 points, not +16.7. Two of the wins were closer than the scores say. Iowa was outplayed per snap in its 20-19 win at Michigan (-0.164 in net EPA, offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed) and played Iowa State about even in a 16-13 win.

The defense tells the same story. Iowa allows 12.2 points per game, the fewest in FBS, but it's 21st of 138 in defensive EPA per play (points added versus an average play in that situation). SP+ has the defense 4th, so it is very good. The "best in the country" number just had help from the offenses it faced.

What's still missing is the top of the schedule. Iowa's two best opponents are both 7-4, it hasn't played a team with eight or more wins, and it's 0-1 against SP+ top-25 teams. Every rating puts the Hawkeyes about where the committee does: 16th in SP+, 16th in ESPN's FPI, 13th in SRS (point margin adjusted for schedule) and 13th in the CFP rankings. They get there with the 44th-ranked roster in the recruiting talent composite, the lowest of the seven. That's impressive, but it's a lower-group resume.

## Seat's Taken

If the middle of Iowa's schedule is better than advertised, does that make all seven of these schedules about the same? No. By middle-game opponent win percentage, they split into three groups. Texas (.657) and Oregon (.643) are 5th and 8th in FBS in middle opponent win percentage. Alabama and Indiana are tied at .606, 23rd. Then Texas Tech, Iowa and Tennessee sit between .543 and .561, 49th to 63rd. The gap from top to bottom is about 1.6 standard deviations of the FBS spread. Trimming pulled Iowa up, but it didn't squeeze the groups together.

The records are close, though: everyone is between 5-2 and 7-0 in the middle. The records look alike, and the schedules behind them don't.

Here's the part I didn't expect. The middle records run the opposite way from the difficulty. Texas and Oregon have the two hardest middles, and they also have the worst middle records (both 5-2) and the smallest middle margins of the seven.

Texas' full resume is the best of the group. Its seven wins over winning teams lead FBS, it's the only one of the seven without an FCS opponent, and it held its two best opponents to 2.95 yards per play. The middle is where the Longhorns wobble. Both losses are there, to Oklahoma and Florida by three points each, and so is a 22-21 win at LSU in which Texas was outplayed per snap (-0.186 in net EPA). It's 36th in FBS in scoring at 31.5 points a game but 96th of 138 in offensive EPA per play, and the defense (11th in defensive EPA) is carrying it. Tennessee has the same shape: 41st in FBS in scoring, 99th in offensive EPA per play and 4th in defensive EPA.

Oregon's middle has the highest average opponent SP+ of the seven (13.2), and the Ducks won those games by only 6.1 points a game, with losses to Oklahoma State and Illinois. Their 34.8 points per game overall drops to 29.3 in the middle once the 84-0 win over Portland State is gone. Oregon has the third-most talented roster in FBS by the recruiting composite, and three losses.

Alabama is the opposite case: a middle-tier schedule, and results that don't look like anyone's. The Crimson Tide are 7-0 in their middle games, winning by 29.9 points a game, with a net EPA of .398 per play, nearly double anyone else's. The catch is the ends. They're 0-2 against their two best opponents, Georgia and Tennessee, and Tennessee's 26-10 win over Alabama in Knoxville is the best single win any of these teams outside Texas has.

Texas Tech and Indiana don't have Alabama's margin to hide behind, and both have a hole that the record covers up.

Texas Tech is 11-1, but the best teams it has played are 8-3 Arizona and 8-3 Houston, and it beat them by eight and two. It scores 38.2 points a game (10th in FBS) and allows 23.4 (58th), gave up 48 to 5-6 West Virginia and lost by 13 at 6-5 Cincinnati. SRS has the Red Raiders 15th and FPI has them 14th, both behind their No. 9 AP ranking.

Indiana is the one the ratings can't agree on. SP+ has the Hoosiers 5th overall with the No. 2 defense, and they allow 13.8 points a game (5th in FBS). SRS has them 18th, the biggest gap between ratings for any of the seven. Their middle includes Howard, an FCS team, and against the FBS teams in it they're 5-1 with an average margin of +9.3.

## After All, Tomorrow Is Another Day

So, half right. Trim the ends and Iowa's schedule is ordinary rather than weak, right there with Texas Tech and Tennessee. But "they all look alike" only holds for the records. The schedules split into three groups, Texas and Oregon took their losses against the hardest middles of the seven, and Alabama is in a class by itself until it plays somebody at the top.

This weekend helps settle it. Everyone gets a true middle eight once they play their 12th game, and several of these teams' 6-5 opponents can fall to 6-6 and change the math. If Iowa wants to move past the lower group, it needs a win the trim can't take away.
