# They Are Who We Thought They Were

On Friday night Darian Mensah had himself a game, and by Monday morning the Heisman board had reshuffled around him. I guess it pays to show out in a stand alone Friday night game.  Sunday night, Trinidad Chambliss put together nearly the same night, but his game had Notre Dame vs. Wisconsin to contend with, and it didn't move the needle nearly as much.  The number of eyes that see your performance matters for Heisman hype.  

## Anything You Can Do

Mensah went 27 of 30 for 401 yards and five touchdowns in Miami's 45-6 win over Stanford. That's a legitimately great final line. It also came against a defense that ranked at the bottom of the country against the pass in 2025, 136th out of 136 FBS teams, giving up 288.9 yards a game through the air. Chambliss went 21 of 36 for 336 yards and three touchdowns, plus he added 60 yards and a score on the ground.  He beat No. 24 Louisville 41-38 on a 40-yard, fourth-quarter drive in the final 90 seconds for the winning field goal. Louisville's pass defense ranked 24th nationally last year, comfortably top third in the sport. Same weekend, nearly identical total production, and one quarterback was picking apart a defense that couldn't cover air while the other beat a ranked team that actually could. Chambliss had a Heisman moment against a top-25 Louisville team, and still most of the conversation centered on Mensah.   

## The Other Guys

The rest of the field we flagged in the CFB preview mostly did what you'd expect, score a lot on somebody who didn't belong on the same field. Julian Sayin went 21 of 25 for 320 yards and three touchdowns and rushing score before Ryan Day pulled him early in the third quarter during a 56-3 win against Ball State. Arch Manning threw for 305 yards and four touchdowns in a 59-7 blowout of Texas State, which tells you nothing about how that offensive line holds up against anybody real. John Mateer was the outlier in the wrong direction: 11 of 17 for 225 yards and three touchdowns in a 51-0 win over UTEP, a clean box score built on a night where reports out of Norman say he fought his accuracy the whole night.

Dante Moore is the one guy in this group who actually got tested. Oregon trailed by double digits in the second quarter before Moore's 388 yards and three touchdowns carried the Ducks past Boise State 34-27. CJ Carr was efficient, but certainly not explosive against Wisconsin, 19 of 29 for 239 yards and two scores in a 41-13 Notre Dame win.  That game was 13-10 at the half before the Irish pulled away. 

## To Be the Man, You Gotta Beat the Man, WOOOO!

No one has ever won the Heisman in September. One week of box scores against a buffet of overmatched opponents is a highlight reel, not a résumé. What we do know is that Chambliss's numbers belong in the same sentence as Mensah's, not a footnote underneath them. The real sorting will begin this weekend. Ohio State goes to Austin to play Texas, and Oklahoma heads north for Michigan. Beat a real opponent and the stat line means something. Pad it against a defense that finished in the bottom half of the country last year and it's still just noise.
