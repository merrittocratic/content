# Is This The Real Life?

Six weeks in, and every contender is still asking the same question: is this real, or is this just fantasy? Texas was #1 in the AP poll on Saturday morning and by Saturday night they had lost to Oklahoma and questions about their offense are starting to swirl.  Georgia hasn't looked dominant on the scoreboard, but its numbers say it's as far ahead of the field as any recent #1. And Indiana, the defending champion, is riding one of the least respected 22-game win streaks in recent college football memory.  

## Will The Real Texas Longhorns Please Stand Up, Please Stand Up, Please Stand Up?

Oklahoma 27, Texas 24. Texas led 17-7 at halftime and lost on a 47-yard field goal with three seconds left. On the surface, it looks like a similar collapse that Ohio State suffered at the hands of Longhorns in Week 2, when it blew a 23-3 lead. The two games have one thing in common, a big lead that disappeared, but the reason each lead disappeared is completely different.

In Week 2, Ohio State's offense went from +0.198 EPA per play in the first half to -0.406 in the second. Their offensive efficiency fell off a cliff.  Yes, that's what a collapse means.  On Saturday, Texas went from -0.015 in the first half to -0.280 in the second. Both halves below zero.  Kind of hard to collapse when you're never really playing all that well in the first place.  Oklahoma's only first-half touchdown came on a blocked punt, which resulted from a sack on third and 12 deep in Longhorn territory.

The three-touchdown game was the worse passing day, because two throws erased everything else. Peyton Bowen's 42-yard pick-six cost Texas 6.9 expected points on a single play and gave Oklahoma a 24-17 lead. After Texas tied it, Courtland Guillory picked off Manning at the Oklahoma 39 with 24 seconds left, and Tate Sandell kicked the game-winner. Season-long, Manning ranks 118th of 223 quarterbacks in EPA per dropback, essentially average at +0.002.

The Longhorns' offensive struggles is not a one-game problem. Since the Ohio State win, Texas's offensive efficiency has been below zero in three straight FBS games: -0.130 against UTSA, -0.181 against Tennessee and -0.149 against Oklahoma. The raw numbers agree, at 379 yards per game (78th in FBS) and 31.4 points per game (59th), with offensive EPA ranked 92nd. The defense is the reason Texas is in the playoff conversation at all, allowing 16.0 points per game (21st) and ranking 15th in defensive EPA. In fairness, FPI rates Texas's schedule the 4th-hardest in the country so far.  But three straight games point to the same conclusion: the comeback against Ohio State seems to be the outlier, and the team that trailed 23-3 might just be the real Texas.

## Where's The Beef?

If Texas isn't a juggernaut, what about the Big Ten? 



Seven teams in the top 25 in net EPA is the same depth as the last two seasons. What's changed is that the top tier is sliding: Ohio State has slid from 1st to 5th to 9th in net EPA, and the conference's 4-10 record against other Power 4 teams is the worst of the three years.  The other team in the top 10 in net EPA besides Ohio State and Indiana is Nebraska, who is not in the AP top-25.

Oregon is the clearest example of raw stats lying. The Ducks throw for 317 yards per game, 7th in FBS, but rank 55th in passing EPA and 73rd in offensive EPA.  Against UCLA, Oregon used three quarterbacks, none of whom were Dante Moore. Dylan Raiola had 6 completions for 43 yards with two interceptions, and the Oregon offense finished at -0.577 EPA per play.

One team moving in the right direction is the team that just beat the Ducks. UCLA's net EPA rank the last three years: 128th, 129th, and now 12th. The defense is 5th in defensive EPA, and the run game is real by any measure: 253 rushing yards per game (6th in FBS) and 4th in rushing EPA. The passing game is real in the other direction: 205 passing yards per game (93rd in FBS), 120th in passing EPA, and Nico Iamaleava ranks 170th of 223 quarterbacks in EPA per dropback. He had 10 completions for 162 yards and two interceptions against Oregon, so UCLA won that game with its defense and run game.  If UCLA wants to crash the playoff party, they have to win out for the remainder of the their schedule.  Their best remaining opponent in net EPA is Wisconsin (25th) and by the AP USC (19th). 

## Put Some Respect On Their Name!

So if the Big Ten doesn't have a juggernaut, does anyone? Georgia does, and the numbers say it more clearly than the scoreboard has. Georgia is 1st in net EPA, and it's as far ahead of the field as recent #1s were. The fair way to compare #1 teams across seasons is to measure how far each one sits above the FBS average in standard deviations, which accounts for how spread out the whole field is that year. Through Week 6, 2024 Ohio State was 2.90 above average and 2025 Texas Tech was 2.96. Georgia is 2.76, in the same neighborhood. Georgia is also the most balanced team in the net EPA top five, 4th in both offensive and defensive EPA.

That's why the 20-17 win over Alabama is worth a closer look. As a team performance it wasn't convincing: Alabama had the better success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule), 44% to 41%, and ran for +0.66 EPA per carry. Georgia won it through the air, and Gunner Stockton was better than his 249 yards suggest. He averaged 11.3 yards per attempt and +0.554 EPA per dropback, ranking 14th of 199 quarterbacks that week, and he ranks 2nd of 223 in EPA per dropback for the season. On the other side, Keelon Russell finished at -0.301. Alabama found a crack in a run defense that ranks 16th in EPA on the season, but Georgia's quarterback is a big reason it's the best team in the country.

Notre Dame (2nd in net EPA) and Miami (3rd in net EPA) are the next two, and they share the same asterisk: neither has beaten anybody. Notre Dame's best opponent is Wisconsin, 25th in net EPA, and everyone else it has played ranks 89th or worse. Miami leads the country in total yards (555 per game) and points (49.6 per game), and Darian Mensah is 1st of 223 quarterbacks in EPA per dropback. But that total includes a 77-7 win over FCS Florida A&M, and outside Wake Forest (16th in net EPA), every Miami opponent ranks 113th or worse. We've been saying for weeks that Mensah's numbers come against defenses that can't cover anyone, and that's still true. Miami's pass defense, 79th in EPA, is the weak spot to watch when a real opponent that can throw the ball finally shows up.

Which brings us to the team with the most top-25 wins of any contender. Indiana has won 22 straight: 16-0 last year, including the national title, and 6-0 this year. It sits 7th in the AP poll.  Since 2000, every other power-conference team on a 20-plus game streak has remained in the AP top 5 as the wins kept piling up. The only teams on streaks that long to fall outside the top 5 were Group of 5 programs, UCF and Boise State. Do we need to remind the AP voters that Indiana is the defending National Champions.

The numbers actually back the complaint. Indiana is 4th in net EPA and 5th in offensive EPA, scoring 40.2 points per game (14th) and allowing just 11.8 (5th). Josh Hoover is 3rd of 223 quarterbacks in EPA per dropback, and he was at his best against the best team Indiana has played: 263 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions against Nebraska at 12.0 yards per attempt. Nebraska ranks 6th in net EPA, and Indiana has also beaten Northwestern (17th in net EPA), which makes it the only contender with two wins over top-25 teams by net EPA. Its average opponent ranks 61st in net EPA, the toughest schedule of the five contenders (Georgia, Notre Dame, Miami, Ohio State and Indiana); Georgia's and Ohio State's average opponents rank 86th, and Notre Dame's and Miami's rank 95th.

This doesn't knock Georgia off its perch. Georgia's 20-17 win over Alabama (11th in net EPA) and Indiana's 20-17 win over Nebraska are nearly the same game on paper: Georgia outplayed Alabama by +0.125 EPA per play, and Indiana outplayed Nebraska by +0.121. Georgia's offense was better that day, and Indiana's defense was. Where Indiana really has a case is against the team ranked two spots above it.
---

Texas's offense is what we saw on Saturday, not what we saw in September. The Big Ten is as deep as ever, but it doesn't have a team like 2024 Ohio State or 2025 Oregon at the top. Georgia is the closest thing to an elite team this year, even after a shaky win over Alabama. And Indiana is the most underrated team in the country: 4th in net EPA, with the 3rd-best quarterback by EPA per dropback and the most top-25 wins of any contender, sitting 7th in the AP poll on 22 game win streak as the defending national champion.
