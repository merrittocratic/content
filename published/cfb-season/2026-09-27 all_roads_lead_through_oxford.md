
# All Roads Lead Through Oxford

How is it that Oxford, MS has become the center of the College Football universe?  Trinidad Chambliss is making his case for the Heisman.  Lincoln Kienholz's lone loss against a ranked team happened to be against Ole Miss. The coach who built the current Rebel roster abandoned them for Baton Rouge, and is now the biggest villain in the sport.

Ole Miss is no longer just the House of Manning.

## Finkle is Einhorn

We flagged the developing Chambliss-Mensah storyline a few weeks back.  Three games in, and the production is still basically a wash. Darian Mensah ranks 6th nationally in our production index (79 attempts, 873 yards, 11 touchdowns, zero picks); Trinidad Chambliss ranks 7th (110 attempts, 924 yards, 7 touchdowns, 2 picks). In a vacuum, Mensah looks cleaner, but in the immortal words of Lee Corso, **not so fast, my friend**.

Saturday night, Chambliss beat then-#7 LSU 32-24 and previously knocked off a ranked Louisville team 41-38. Mensah's three wins: Stanford (45-6), Florida A&M (77-7, SWAC), Wake Forest (33-20) aren't exactly against murderer's row. So, two quarterbacks producing nearly at the same rate.  One is effectively playing against air, while the other already has two wins against ranked opponents. Any pundit that is arguing Mensah over Chambliss really has not been paying attention.   

## Not Again!!

Lincoln Kienholz backed up Julian Sayin at Ohio State as recently as 2025.  He had only 11 pass attempts all season, before he transferred to Louisville for the 2026 season.  Kienholz now ranks 5th nationally in our production index: 816 passing yards, 6 touchdowns, zero interceptions, plus 153 rushing yards and 4 rushing scores.  A legitimate dual-threat line. Sayin ranks 34th: 839 passing yards, 6 touchdowns, 1 interception, and negative net rushing once you count sack yardage.  Both quarterbacks carry exactly one loss to a ranked team: Sayin to #1 Texas, Kienholz to #8 Ole Miss.

Even in the loss to the Rebels, Louisville had the better per-play EPA (how many points a play was worth relative to an average play in that situation) at 0.161 to Ole Miss's 0.056.  When you start to look at the numbers, the comparisons will probably start to give Buckeye fans some Joe Burrow vibes.  

He's only three games into his Cardinals tenure, so he doesn't need to be the next Burrow to be the better story than Sayin right now. He's out-producing him with zero turnovers, and that is enough to make Buckeye fans curious, or maybe even furious.  Kienholz's win against #16 SMU only makes the storyline more interesting.

## All Aboard, The Lane Train

Which brings us to the guy who walked off the visitors bus in Oxford.  Kiffin didn't just leave for a bigger job. He left two days after winning the Egg Bowl and clinching Ole Miss's first-ever CFP berth.  Runaway bride meets College Football.  In the Grove, fans hung him in effigy. Then Golding's team, the one Kiffin built and walked out on, won the program's first-ever playoff game over Tulane, beat Georgia in the Sugar Bowl, and finally ran out of gas against Miami in the CFB semifinal Fiesta Bowl. Thirteen and two. Best season in school history. With and Without him.

So, when LSU rolled into Oxford Saturday night and lost 32-24, it wasn't just Chambliss beating his old coach. It was the Grove getting to settle a score nine months in the making.  On the raw box score, the win over LSU wasn't a turnover-luck special or a fluke, Ole Miss ran the more efficient offense, 0.219 EPA per play to LSU's 0.121.  They ran up a commanding lead in the first half, survived a surge in the third quarter, and hung on to win in the fourth.  Their second against a ranked team.  Heisman voters, are you paying attention?
