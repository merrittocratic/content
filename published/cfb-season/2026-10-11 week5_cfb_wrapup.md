# I Did Not See That Coming!  And Two Other Stories

The AP poll is really a story about records. The play-by-play is the actual receipt that shows whether those poll rankings are earned. This week I checked three stories against them. Florida's fell apart in Columbia. Miami's hasn't been tested. The Big 12's is irrelevant.

## Ahhh, Remember When...

Remember when I said that Florida wasn't good enough to win every Saturday.  When I said that, I thought it would last longer than a week.  In Columbia, the game was tied 10-10 in the first half, and then Missouri outscored Florida 35-7 the rest of the way for a 45-17 win. Missouri's Jamal Roberts ran for three touchdowns, one of them an 80-yarder, after he appeared to be gobbled up for no gain.

On competitive snaps, the Florida offense managed -0.176 EPA per play and a 33% success rate. When you read competitive here, that just means we're excluding garbage time from the stats.  Missouri posted 0.438 and a 53% success rate. Florida's rushing offense was the worst offender managing just -0.69 EPA per carry and their runs got stuffed for at or behind the line of scrimmage 44% of the time. Remind me again, which team had the Heisman contender at running back coming into this game?    

Florida's defense didn't lose the splash-play battle either and it forced havoc (tackles for loss, forced fumbles, interceptions, pass breakups) on 29.8% of Missouri's snaps compared to Missouri's 17.9% against Florida's offense.  Big plays were even too, so this wasn't a few long gains skewing the data. Florida lost on ordinary down and distance plays.  Whenever it didn't make a play behind the line of scrimmage, Missouri just moved the ball by succeeding on 53% of its competitive plays. That's harder to fix than a few busted coverages, because it means Florida kept getting beat on routine downs.

This is not a new phenomenon in Gainesville.  They've gotten worse every week, and now sit at 128th of 138 in defensive EPA on the season. Obviously, the opponents have gotten better as the season has progressed, so some of the increase is schedule related, but 128 is still 128, so this is not just a schedule problem.

The offense is another story. It ranks 17th in EPA overall, 4th in passing and 79th in rushing. It put up 0.340 against FAU (defense 106th in SP+) and 0.343 against Ole Miss (75th).  But against tought opponents, 0.082 against Auburn (16th) and -0.176 against Missouri (14th). The passing game is real, but the run game disappears the moment the other front can play. The AP dropped Florida from 8th to 16th, which is probably still generous.  Another reminder, Baugh was a darling Heisman contender just last week.

## If You Wanna Crown 'Em, Go Ahead And Crown 'Em

Miami hosts Pitt on Oct. 24, and while most pundits have already crowned the Hurricanes as champions, Pitt is proving that it just might be a worthy spoiler. Miami is 3-0 in conference and 4th in the AP. Pitt is 2-0 and 25th.  Neither team has played an SP+ top-25 opponent, so we really have no idea how good either team is. 

Miami's best win came this weekend in a 41-13 rout over Clemson who currently sits at 54th in SP+. Pitt's best win was also this weekend in a 35-33 nail biter over Virginia Tech who sits at 28th.  In that game, Virginia Tech was better per play, 40% success to 30%, and lost mainly because Pitt won the turnover battle 4-0 and Tech missed a 36-yard field goal with two seconds left. Pitt's offense has been below zero EPA in three of its four FBS games.

There are two very bad matchups in this game that both heavily favor Miami.  Miami's 7th ranked pass offense meets Pitt's 56th ranked pass defense, and Pitt's anemic run game that ranks 95th will have to try to grind out some yards against Miami's 5th ranked run defense. Miami's soft spot is its pass defense ranked at 104th.  For an upset, Pitt needs to beat a bad secondary with a passing game that is above average, while slowing Miami's elite passing attack.

## If a Tree Fell In The Forest, Would Anyone Care Who Wins the Big 12?

The Big 12 has five ranked teams and is 5-7 against the other power conferences. The SEC is 10-2 in those games and the Big Ten is 4-10. Oregon (SP+ 8th) is the only SP+ top-20 team a Big 12 school has beaten.

Who beat Oregon?  Oklahoma State!  The same Oklahoma State team that opened their season by losing 24-10 at Tulsa. Against the Ducks, the Cowboys were able to sustain drives better than Oregon by staying in manageable down and distance situations (success rate 43% vs. 30%).  The Cowboy defense held Oregon to -0.248 EPA per play and stopped three fourth downs.  Cowboy Up!  

BYU is 4-0 and 3rd in net EPA with the 5th-best defense. The adjusted ratings are skeptical and the Cougar schedule explains why. FPI rates it 93rd, and their best win is over Arizona. In their recent 17-10 win over TCU, BYU's passing game was at -0.37 EPA per dropback, so they're not exactly appointment viewing right now.

Texas Tech is 5-0 and 11th in the AP, but ranks 64th in net EPA, with the offense sitting at 93rd overall and 111th in rushing. The Red Raiders were **betting** on a better offense this season when they paid $6.5 million for a quarterback who is no longer on the roster. In their last game at Colorado, the offense managed an anemic -0.379 EPA per play and the defense won it with four interceptions, including quite possibly the easiest pick 6 in college football history when the Colorado quarterback **gambled** and threw the ball right to a Texas Tech defender in the end zone. 



The Big 12 doesn't lack contenders. It lacks evidence and an audience.

*Data: cfbfastR play-by-play (garbage time excluded), CFBD advanced box scores, SP+ through Week 4, FPI as pulled.*
