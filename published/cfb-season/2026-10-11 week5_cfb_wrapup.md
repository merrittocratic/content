# Records Are Cheap

Every week the polls hand us a story and the play-by-play hands us a different one. This week the gap was widest in Gainesville, Coral Gables, and the entire Big 12.

## Florida Didn't Get Unlucky. It Got Run Over.

Missouri 45, Florida 17 looks like a game that got away. It was tied 10-10 at 4:26 of the second quarter, and Missouri outscored Florida 35-7 the rest of the way. J. Roberts ran for three touchdowns, one of them an 80-yarder.

On competitive snaps, Florida managed -0.176 EPA per play and a 33% success rate. Missouri posted 0.438 and 53%. Florida's rushing offense was at -0.69 EPA per carry, line yards were 14 to 52, and Florida's runs got stuffed 44% of the time. Missouri converted every power situation. Florida converted 20%.

Florida's defense was actually the more disruptive one, with a 29.8% havoc rate to Missouri's 17.9% and a wash on explosiveness. It lost down after down, which is the worse way to lose because it doesn't go away.

It also isn't new. Florida's defense, game by game against FBS opponents:

| Opponent | EPA/play allowed |
|---|---|
| Florida Atlantic | 0.042 |
| Auburn | 0.224 |
| Ole Miss | 0.283 |
| Missouri | 0.438 |

Worse every week, and 128th of 138 in defensive EPA on the season. Fair caveat: the opponents got better down that list, so some of the slope is schedule. A 128th-ranked defense is not a schedule problem.

The offense is the real story. It ranks 17th in EPA, 4th passing and 79th rushing. It put up 0.340 against FAU (defense 106th in SP+) and 0.343 against Ole Miss (75th), then 0.082 against Auburn (16th) and -0.176 against Missouri (14th). The passing game is real. The run game disappears the moment the other front can play. The AP dropped Florida from 8th to 16th, which is probably still generous.

## Pitt Has the Per-Play Numbers. Miami Has the Passing Game.

Miami hosts Pitt on Oct. 24, and the ACC is holding a coronation. Miami is 3-0 in conference and 4th in the AP. Pitt is 2-0 and 25th. Per play, Pitt is slightly ahead. Opponent-adjusted, Miami is well ahead.

| | Miami | Pitt |
|---|---|---|
| Net EPA rank | 13th | 10th |
| Offense EPA rank | 6th | 38th |
| Defense EPA rank | 70th | 16th |
| SP+ overall | 5th | 20th |
| FPI strength of schedule | 70th | 100th |

Neither team has played an SP+ top-25 opponent. Miami's best is Clemson at 54th, a 41-13 win. Pitt's best is Virginia Tech at 28th, and the 35-33 win is the one to doubt. Virginia Tech was better per play, 40% success to 30%, and lost because Pitt won the turnover battle 4-0 and Tech missed a 36-yard field goal with two seconds left. Pitt's offense has been below zero EPA in three of its four FBS games.

The matchup comes down to two arrows. Miami's pass offense (7th) meets Pitt's pass defense (56th), the clearest mismatch on the board. Pitt's run game (95th) meets Miami's run defense (5th), a closed door. Miami's soft spot is its pass defense at 104th, and Pitt's passing offense at 25th is the only realistic way through it. For an upset, Pitt needs to beat a bad secondary and slow a passing attack at the same time.

## Oklahoma State Has the Win. Everyone Else Has a Schedule.

The Big 12 has five ranked teams and is 5-7 against the other power conferences. The SEC is 10-2 in those games and the Big Ten is 4-10. Oregon (SP+ 8th) is the only SP+ top-20 team a Big 12 school has beaten.

That's Oklahoma State, which opened by losing 24-10 at Tulsa. Against Oregon, its success rate was 43% to 30%, and its defense held Oregon to -0.248 EPA per play and stopped three fourth downs. The offense was negative too, dragged down by a pick-six. The defense won that game. The ratings are catching up, from 121st in SP+ last year to 34th now. Elo still says 102nd.

BYU is 4-0 and 3rd in net EPA with the 5th-best defense. The adjusted ratings are skeptical (SP+ 17th, FPI 16th, Elo 28th), and the schedule explains it. FPI rates it 93rd, and the best win is Arizona. In the 17-10 win over TCU, BYU's passing game was at -0.37 per dropback. The defense won that one too.

Texas Tech is 5-0 and 11th in the AP, but ranks 64th in net EPA, with the offense 93rd overall and 111th running the ball. SP+ has it 11th, likely propped up by last year's finish, since SP+ leans on preseason expectations this early. At Colorado the offense managed -0.379 EPA per play and the defense won it with four interceptions. The best win is 28-26 over Houston.

The best team on paper is Utah, 4-0 and 9th in SP+, 6th in net EPA and 8th in Elo. Its best win is Iowa State at 58th.

The Big 12 doesn't lack contenders. It lacks evidence.

*Data: cfbfastR play-by-play (garbage time excluded), CFBD advanced box scores, SP+ through Week 4, FPI as pulled.*
