# Hold Onto Your Butts

Five of the top eight teams in the AP poll lost on Saturday, including #1 Georgia and #2 Notre Dame. For two of the losing teams, Notre Dame and Texas, the question now is simple: can they survive one more loss and still make the playoff? Some people in Austin and South Bend may be changing their minds about the 24-team playoff. Probably the same in Tuscaloosa, but the 24-team playoff topic will be for another day.

## I Can't Believe What I Just Saw!

Georgia lost to unranked Auburn, 27-24. Notre Dame lost to #8 BYU, 28-24. Ohio State beat #5 Indiana 19-15, ending the Hoosiers' 22-game win streak. #6 Alabama lost to #14 Tennessee, 26-10, and #7 Texas lost to unranked Florida, 27-24. Only Miami, Ohio State and BYU made it through unscathed.

In college football history, which reliably goes back to 1936, the AP top eight has lost five games in the same week only twice, this week and Oct. 4, 2014. This is the first time #1 and #2 were included in a five-loss week. It could be argued that 2014 was the wilder week. Two of this week's losses were guaranteed because of two top-eight matchups: Notre Dame played BYU and Indiana played Ohio State. In 2014, all five top-eight losses were to lower-ranked teams.

## Unlucky Charms

Notre Dame and Texas have a lot more riding on their next loss than the other losers. Both missed the playoff last year: Notre Dame finished 11th at 10-2 in the final College Football Playoff rankings, and Texas finished 13th at 9-3.

Notre Dame's math changed over the summer. Starting this year, Notre Dame gets an automatic bid if it finishes in the committee's top 12. Under that rule, last year's 10-2 team would have been in. So a second loss, which will most likely come at the hands of the Hurricanes, isn't fatal by itself. Notre Dame is still playing like a playoff team: 4th in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed) and 8th in defensive EPA. C.J. Carr was better than his 261 yards against BYU suggest: his +0.170 EPA per dropback ranked 70th of 216 quarterbacks this week.

The real problem is the resume. Notre Dame hasn't beaten a team that was ranked when they played. Its six wins came against Wisconsin, North Carolina, Purdue, Stanford, Michigan State and Rice, and none of them was in the AP poll at the time. BYU was the only ranked team on the schedule, and that was a loss.

Two ranked teams remain: #3 Miami and #24 SMU. If Notre Dame loses to Miami and SMU falls out of the rankings, Notre Dame finishes 10-2 with zero ranked wins. The committee uses its own rankings, which come out in early November. A 10-2 team with no ranked wins is exactly the profile that slides from 11th to 13th, and that's the difference between an automatic bid and staying home. So the SMU game in Week 12 may end up mattering more to Notre Dame than the Miami game does.

## I Really Hate That Bob Barker

Unlike the Irish, the Longhorns don't have a rule to save them. Once again, Florida may be Texas' undoing. Florida was unranked entering the week, and this week's inexplicable loss now gives Texas two losses to unranked teams in consecutive weeks. And for the second straight week, Texas blew a double-digit lead: 17-7 at halftime against Oklahoma, and 17-3 in the second quarter against Florida.

Back in September I said Florida was good enough to beat anybody on any given Saturday, but not good enough to win every Saturday. Well, Saturday was one of the Saturdays. Texas had the better success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule), 42% to 35%, but it ran only 50 plays to Florida's 81. The Longhorns' run game lost 0.31 expected points per carry, Florida returned a punt for a touchdown, and the game ended on a strip-sack of Arch Manning at 1:58 with Texas down three. Manning's line looked relatively efficient, 173 yards at 8.7 yards per attempt, but his EPA per dropback was -0.033, 116th of 216 quarterbacks this week.

The season numbers say these losses aren't flukes. The offense gains 361 yards per game (93rd in FBS) and ranks 100th in offensive EPA. The defense is 22nd in defensive EPA, and overall Texas is 48th in net EPA.

This is why one more loss probably ends it. At 9-3, Texas would match the record that left it 13th and out of the playoff last year. No team in the 12-team era has made the field as an at-large team with three regular-season losses. The remaining schedule doesn't help either: FPI rates it the 14th-hardest, with road trips to Missouri (29th in net EPA), Texas A&M (55th) and LSU, which is now 1st in net EPA. Short of winning the SEC, the only way to avoid missing the playoff is winning out.

---

Five top-eight teams lost, and it tied a record. Notre Dame can afford one more loss, but it needs to beat a ranked team somewhere along the way. SMU may be its best chance. Texas' losses to two unranked teams mean it's in playoff elimination games for the rest of the season.
