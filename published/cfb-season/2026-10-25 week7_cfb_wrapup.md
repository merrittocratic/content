# Hold Onto Your Butts

Five of the top eight teams in the AP poll lost on Saturday, including #1 Georgia and #2 Notre Dame. That has happened once before. And for two of the losers, Notre Dame and Texas, the question now is simple: can they survive one more loss and still make the playoff?

## Did That Just Happen?

Georgia lost to unranked Auburn, 27-24. Notre Dame lost to #8 BYU, 28-24. Ohio State beat #5 Indiana 19-15 and ended its 22-game win streak. #6 Alabama lost to #14 Tennessee, 26-10, and #7 Texas lost to unranked Florida, 27-24. Only Miami, Ohio State and BYU made it through.

In College Football Data's records, which go back to 1936, the AP top eight has lost five games in the same week only twice, this week and Oct. 4, 2014. This is the first time a five-loss week has included #1 and #2. But 2014 was the wilder week. Two of this week's losses were guaranteed, because Notre Dame played BYU and Indiana played Ohio State, two top-eight matchups. In 2014, all five top-eight teams lost to lower-ranked teams.

## The Luck Of The Irish

Two of those losers have a lot more riding on the next loss than the others. Notre Dame and Texas both missed the playoff last year, Notre Dame at 10-2 and 11th in the final College Football Playoff rankings, and Texas at 9-3 and 13th.

Notre Dame's math changed over the summer. Starting this year, Notre Dame gets an automatic bid if it finishes in the committee's top 12. Under that rule, last year's 10-2 team would have been in. So a second loss, most likely to Miami, isn't fatal by itself, and Notre Dame is still playing like a playoff team: 4th in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed) and 8th in defensive EPA. And C.J. Carr was better than his 261 yards against BYU suggest: his +0.170 EPA per dropback ranked 70th of 216 quarterbacks this week.

The real problem is the resume. Notre Dame hasn't beaten a team that was ranked when they played. Its six wins came against Wisconsin, North Carolina, Purdue, Stanford, Michigan State and Rice, and none of them was in the AP poll at the time. BYU was the only ranked team on the schedule, and that was the loss.

Two ranked teams are left: #3 Miami and #24 SMU. If Notre Dame loses to Miami and SMU falls out of the rankings, Notre Dame finishes 10-2 with zero ranked wins. The committee uses its own rankings, which come out in early November, not the AP poll. But a 10-2 team with no ranked wins is exactly the profile that slides from 11th to 13th, and that's the difference between an automatic bid and staying home. So the SMU game in Week 12 may matter more to Notre Dame than Miami does.

## Groundhog Day

Texas doesn't have a rule to lean on. Florida was unranked entering the week, so Texas now has two losses to unranked teams. And for the second straight game, Texas blew a double-digit lead: 17-7 at halftime against Oklahoma, then 17-3 in the second quarter against Florida.

Back in September I said Florida was good enough to beat anybody on any given Saturday, but not good enough to win every Saturday. This was one of the Saturdays. Texas had the better success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule), 42% to 35%, but it ran only 50 plays to Florida's 81. Its run game lost 0.31 expected points per carry, Florida returned a punt for a touchdown, and the game ended on a strip-sack of Arch Manning at 1:58 with Texas down three. Manning's line looked efficient, 173 yards at 8.7 yards per attempt, but his EPA per dropback was -0.033, 116th of 216 quarterbacks this week.

The season numbers say these losses aren't flukes. The offense gains 361 yards per game (93rd in FBS) and ranks 100th in offensive EPA. The defense is 22nd in defensive EPA, and overall Texas is 48th in net EPA.

That's why one more loss probably ends it. At 9-3, Texas would match the record that left it 13th and out last year, and no team in the 12-team era has made the field as an at-large with three regular-season losses. The remaining schedule doesn't help: FPI rates it the 14th-hardest, with road trips to Missouri (29th in net EPA), Texas A&M (55th) and LSU, which is now 1st in net EPA. The only way around a third loss is winning the SEC.

---

Five top-eight teams lost, and it tied a record. Notre Dame can afford one more loss, but it needs to beat a ranked team somewhere along the way, and SMU may be its best chance. Texas can't afford one more.
