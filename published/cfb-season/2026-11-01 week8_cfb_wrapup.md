# Inconceivable!

Last week BYU knocked off #2 Notre Dame at home. This week the Cougars found out who #2 works for in 2026. Ranked that high for the first time since their 1984 national title team, BYU went to Orlando and lost in double overtime to a 3-3 UCF team that had already lost to Pitt, Houston and Oklahoma State. That's three straight weeks a #1 or #2 team has lost to an unranked opponent. Upsets in general aren't up this year, but upsets at the very top sure are, and it isn't particularly close.

## It's A Trap!

BYU came into Orlando ranked 15th in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed), and UCF came in ranked 63rd.  For a half, it looked like the Cougars would handle the pressure of being #2. They led 7-0, then 14-10 late in the second quarter, and were driving again when Bear Bachmeier threw an interception at the UCF 21 with 1:22 left. The Knights turned the interception into a touchdown with four seconds left in the half to lead 17-14. From there the lead changed hands: 21-17 BYU, 24-21 UCF, then 24-24 on a 47-yard BYU field goal with 12:09 to play.

Then the game got a little crazy.  BYU's defense saved them. Twice.  The first came on a goal-line stand. UCF had 4 plays inside the 5 that ended with BYU's Anisi Purcell stuffing Keyone Jenkins for no gain with 1:42 left. In most normal games that would have been UCF's last chance. This was not a normal game. BYU went three-and-out from its own 1 and punted 61 yards to escape the shadow of their end zone, but a BYU unsportsmanlike conduct penalty handed the Knights 15 free yards and moved them to the BYU 28. With 10 seconds left, you guessed it, BYU blocked UCF's 46-yard field goal attempt and the game went to overtime.

Overtime is where BYU ran out of saves. In the first overtime, a false start left the Cougars facing 3rd-and-13, and Bachmeier found Legend Glasker for a 28-yard touchdown anyway. Jenkins needed one snap to answer, a 25-yard touchdown to Josh Derry: 31-31. In the second overtime, BYU had forced a UCF field goal attempt, but a BYU penalty gave the Knights a fresh set of downs. Jenkins made it count with a touchdown to Grayson Brousseau, and the ensuing two-point conversion made it 39-31. During the next possession, BYU managed to get into a 1st-and-goal from the 8.  A Bachmeier run to the 4 on first down made a third overtime look inevitable. Three straight incompletions later, and the #2 team in the country was defeated in the Bounce House.

The win is UCF's first over an AP top-5 team.  A goal-line stand, a blocked kick and a double-overtime finish sounds like a coin flip. The numbers say UCF was the better team.  UCF's success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule) was 53% to BYU's 42%. Jenkins was also the better quarterback, and for once the box score agrees with the efficiency numbers. He finished with 25 completions for 293 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions, and his +0.287 EPA per dropback in regulation beat Bachmeier's +0.223.  The Knights converted 10 of 17 third downs and ran 74 plays to BYU's 60. The gap was widest on longer yardage passing downs: UCF succeeded 45.5% of the time there, compared to BYU 26.3%.

In regulation, both teams scored 24 points on six trips inside the 40. BYU's came in bigger chunks. UCF's came from doing the boring things right, snap after snap. This wasn't a fluke. The Knights were the better team for four quarters, and BYU's defense is the only reason it took six periods to win it.

## Uneasy Lies The Head That Wears The Crown

BYU had plenty of company on Saturday. Cincinnati beat #8 Texas Tech 37-24. South Carolina beat #11 Tennessee 27-24, one week after the Volunteers handled #6 Alabama. Wisconsin beat #16 USC 24-7, and Minnesota beat #15 Iowa 13-10, the most Iowa score imaginable.

While it sounds like unprecedented chaos, it isn't. Through Week 8 this season, unranked teams have beaten AP-ranked teams 17 times, which right in line with the average of 18 since 1989, when the AP poll went to 25 teams. So the upsets aren't coming fast and furious, they're more like the Air Force, they're aiming high.  

Unranked teams have beaten AP top-10 teams eight times this season, which is double the normal rate and 4th-most through Week 8 in the top-25 era. Now for the crazy stat: a #1 or #2 team has lost to an unranked opponent three weeks running: #1 Texas to Oklahoma, #1 Georgia to Auburn and #2 BYU to UCF. Three such losses through Week 8 ties for the 2nd-most since the AP started keeping records back in 1936, and only one other season had a three-week run like this: 2007, the only year a two-loss team won the BCS version of the national title.

---

This isn't the year of the unranked team. The middle of the poll is losing to unranked teams at a normal rate, or a little less. It's the year nobody can stay at the top. UCF beat the #2 team in the country by being better at the boring stuff, and it took two game-saving plays by BYU's defense to get the Cougars to overtime at all. Whoever moves into the top two on Sunday should check the schedule for a 3-3 team.
