# Inconceivable!

Last week BYU was the #8 team that knocked off #2 Notre Dame. This week the Cougars found out what being #2 feels like in 2026. Ranked that high for the first time since the 1984 national title team, BYU went to Orlando and lost in double overtime to a 3-3 UCF team that had already lost to Pitt, Houston and Oklahoma State. That's three straight weeks a #1 or #2 team has lost to an unranked opponent. Upsets in general aren't up this year. Upsets at the very top are, and it isn't close.

## Mostly Dead Is Slightly Alive

On paper this one wasn't close either. BYU came in 15th in net EPA (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency allowed), and UCF came in 63rd. The win is UCF's first over an AP top-5 team in the College Football Data record.

For a half, it looked like the Cougars would handle it. They led 7-0, then 14-10 late in the second quarter, and were driving again when Bear Bachmeier threw an interception at the UCF 21 with 1:22 left. The Knights answered with a touchdown with four seconds left in the half to lead 17-14. From there the lead changed hands: 21-17 BYU, 24-21 UCF, then 24-24 on a 47-yard BYU field goal with 12:09 to play.

Then BYU's defense saved the game. Twice.

First came the goal-line stand. UCF had 1st-and-goal at the BYU 3, got to the 1, then backed itself up with a false start. On 4th-and-1 from the 1, Anisi Purcell stuffed Keyone Jenkins for no gain with 1:42 left.

That should have been UCF's last chance. It wasn't. BYU went three-and-out from its own 1 and punted 61 yards to escape, and then a BYU unsportsmanlike conduct penalty handed the Knights 15 free yards and moved them to the BYU 28. With 10 seconds left, Faletau Satuala blocked UCF's 46-yard field goal attempt.

Overtime is where BYU ran out of saves. In the first overtime, a false start left the Cougars facing 3rd-and-13, and Bachmeier found Legend Glasker for a 28-yard touchdown anyway. Jenkins needed one snap to answer, a 25-yard touchdown to Josh Derry: 31-31. In the second overtime, UCF's 26-yard field goal came off the board on a BYU penalty that gave the Knights a fresh set of downs. Jenkins made it count with a touchdown to Grayson Brousseau, and the two-point conversion made it 39-31. BYU got a 17-yard completion to Kyler Kasper for 1st-and-goal at the 8 and a Bachmeier run to the 4. Then three straight incompletions, and the #2 team in the country was done.

## Just Keep Swimming

A goal-line stand, a blocked kick and a double-overtime finish sound like a coin flip. The numbers say UCF was the better team.

Per play, the two offenses were nearly even in regulation: UCF at 0.149 EPA per play, BYU at 0.160. The difference was how often each team stayed on schedule. UCF's success rate (the share of plays that keep an offense on schedule) was 53% to BYU's 42%. The Knights converted 10 of 17 third downs and ran 74 plays to BYU's 60. The gap was widest on passing downs (the long-yardage situations where an offense is supposed to be in trouble): UCF succeeded 45.5% of the time there, and BYU 26.3%.

UCF also won up front. Its offensive line was credited with 124 line yards to BYU's 90, the Knights had a 60% rushing success rate to BYU's 41%, and they were stuffed at or behind the line on 12.5% of their runs to BYU's 20%. Duke Watson carried 15 times for 94 yards.

Jenkins was the better quarterback, and for once the box score agrees with the efficiency numbers. He finished with 25 completions for 293 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions, and his +0.287 EPA per dropback in regulation beat Bachmeier's +0.223. Tyren Hornes caught six passes for 137 yards. Bachmeier averaged more yards per attempt, 8.0 to Jenkins' 7.1, but he completed just 16 passes and threw the interception that turned a BYU drive into UCF's halftime lead.

In regulation, both teams scored 24 points on six trips inside the 40. BYU's came in bigger chunks. UCF's came from doing the boring things right, snap after snap. This wasn't a fluke. The Knights were the better team for four quarters, and BYU's defense is the only reason it took six.

## Same As It Ever Was

BYU had plenty of company on Saturday. Five unranked teams beat ranked ones:

- UCF 39, #2 BYU 31 (2OT)
- Cincinnati 37, #8 Texas Tech 24
- South Carolina 27, #11 Tennessee 24, one week after the Volunteers beat #6 Alabama 26-10. It's the Gamecocks' second win over a ranked team this season, after beating #16 Florida.
- Wisconsin 24, #16 USC 7, the Badgers' second as well, after beating #13 Penn State.
- Minnesota 13, #15 Iowa 10, the most Iowa score imaginable.

That sounds like chaos. Across the whole season, it isn't. Through Week 8, unranked teams have beaten AP-ranked teams 17 times. The median season since 1989, when the AP poll went to 25 teams, has 18 by this point, and the record is 33, in 2007. Ranked teams are losing 12.8% of their games against unranked opponents, below the 14.7% median. Of the 38 seasons in the top-25 era, 2026 ranks 21st in unranked wins over ranked teams and 29th in the rate of those wins. If anything, the typical ranked team is having an easier time than usual.

So the upsets aren't coming more often. They're aimed higher.

## Uneasy Lies The Head

Unranked teams have beaten AP top-10 teams eight times this season. That's double the median of four and tied for the 4th-most unranked wins over top-10 teams through Week 8 in the top-25 era. The record is 13, also in 2007.

At the very top, it's historic. A #1 or #2 team has now lost to an unranked opponent in three straight weeks:

- Week 6: #1 Texas, to Oklahoma
- Week 7: #1 Georgia, to Auburn
- Week 8: #2 BYU, to UCF

Three such losses through Week 8 ties for the 2nd-most in CFBD's AP records going back to 1936, behind four in 1981. Only one other season in that span had a run of three straight weeks: 2007, and it did it twice, in Weeks 6-8 and again in Weeks 10-14. That was the year two-loss LSU won the national title.

---

This isn't the year of the unranked team. The middle of the poll is losing to unranked teams at a normal rate, or a little less. It's the year nobody can stay at the top. UCF beat the #2 team in the country by being better at the boring stuff, and it took two game-saving plays by BYU's defense to get the Cougars to overtime at all. Whoever moves into the top two on Sunday should check the schedule for a 3-3 team.
