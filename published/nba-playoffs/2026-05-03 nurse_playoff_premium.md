## The Hook (~100 words)

Set the scene. Eighteen months of Philly media writing the obituary on the Nurse era. 24-58 last year. Played 30 different players in 2024-25 and used a league-record 54 different starting lineups. The "hire was a mistake" narrative was everywhere by Christmas.

Then April happened. A 7-seed. Lost Game 1 by 32. Lost Game 4 by 32. Down 3-1 with Embiid 17 days post-appendectomy.

Won Game 5 in Boston by 16. Won Game 6 at home wire-to-wire.

What if we've been measuring the wrong thing?

## Section 1: The Consensus Take (~150 words)

The case the Philly columnists have been making for 18 months:

- **91-108 regular-season record** as Sixers coach across three seasons
- **24-58 last year** — first time the Sixers missed the playoffs since 2017
- **Lost in 6 to the Knicks** in his only previous Sixers playoff appearance (2024)
- The "mad scientist" branding feels like cope when the team can't crack .500 in November

Frame the consensus fairly. This isn't a strawman piece. The regular-season number IS bad, and Philly fans have legitimate grievances. The injuries are a real qualifier, but they're a qualifier every Sixers coach gets to claim.

The question this article answers: **does the regular-season record actually measure what we think it measures for this coach?**

## Section 2: The Data the Consensus Is Missing (~250 words)

Where the playoff signal lives.

- **Career playoff record: 27-20 (.574) entering this series**, with Game 6 in hand it's 29-21 minimum, possibly 30-21 by Saturday night
- **2019 NBA Champion** (Toronto's first ever)
- **2020 NBA Coach of the Year**
- **Toronto regular-season win % under Nurse: .582** — best in franchise history
- The famous **Curry box-and-1** in the 2019 Finals — arguably the most consequential single-series adjustment of the modern era
- The Embiid game-plan inversion: Sixers hired Nurse *because of how he'd successfully schemed against Embiid* from the Toronto sideline. That's a front office saying "we want this guy to coach our star the way he used to beat our star."
- Toronto teams under Nurse: top of the league in turnover percentage every year; stayed in the top half of defensive rating *even in down years.* A coach with an identity that travels.

The pattern: when the games matter most, his teams either win or push the favorite to the absolute wall.

**Possible chart: career regular-season win pct vs. playoff win pct, all current head coaches with at least 30 playoff games.** Nurse's gap is one of the biggest in basketball. Gap-positive (better in playoffs than regular season) is rare and valuable.

## Section 3: This Series Specifically (~250 words)

The receipts, game by game.

- **Game 1:** Blown out by 32 in Boston without Embiid. Rookie VJ Edgecombe playing his first playoff minutes. The "this is going to be a sweep" coverage starts.
- **Game 2:** Nurse unleashes Edgecombe — 30 points, six 3-pointers. Sixers steal one in Boston. *The Inquirer reporting describes Nurse and his staff "consuming film like a blue whale devours krill" between games.* (Cite Wiss Now / Bob Cooney piece.)
- **Game 4:** Blown out by 32 *again.* Different game, same result. Series tilts to 3-1.
- **Game 5:** Embiid returns 17 days after an appendectomy. Sixers win by 16 *in Boston.* Nurse changes the screen action — Embiid stops setting high picks for Maxey and goes to a different spot in the offense to manage minutes. Rotation shrinks. It works.
- **Game 6:** Sixers never trailed. 106-93. Maxey 30, George 23, Embiid 19. Edgecombe a thunderous dunk on a Maxey-to-George-to-Edgecombe transition break that became the play of the series.

**The historical context line that does all the work:** *Only one team in NBA history has ever won a playoff series after losing two games by 30+ — the 1959 Minneapolis Lakers, against the St. Louis Hawks.* Whether or not the Sixers become the second, the fact that they're in the conversation is itself the story.

## Section 4: The Merrittocracy Thesis (~150 words)

This is the section that earns the brand voice.

The regular season and the playoffs are not the same sample space. They look like the same sport. They are not.

- Regular season: 82 games, rotation depth matters, you live with what your roster gives you most nights, the schedule does most of the talking.
- Playoffs: same 7-game series for two weeks straight. Adjustment over adjustment over adjustment. The coach who can change his offense between Tuesday and Thursday and have his rookie center execute it cleanly *wins games his roster shouldn't.*

Nurse's identity IS adjustment. He literally wrote a book called *Rapture: Fifteen Teams, Four Countries, One NBA Championship, and How to Find a Way to Win — Damn Near Anywhere*. That's not a marketing line. That's a thesis.

A coach optimized for the playoff sample space is going to look ordinary in the regular-season sample space. That's not a bug. That's the design.

## Section 5: The Saturday Branch

### IF SIXERS WIN GAME 7

> *He just authored the second instance in NBA history of a team coming back from two 30-point losses to win a playoff series. The first one was 67 years ago. He did it with the MVP center 19 days removed from emergency surgery, a rookie playing his first month of playoff basketball, and a 7-seed roster he was supposed to fire-sale in November.*
>
> *Houston paid Ime Udoka something like $14M a year. Boston paid Joe Mazzulla a number that's eye-popping for a young coach. Nurse just made both look like reasonable deals — and made his own deal look like one of the best in basketball.*
>
> *The next time someone in Philly media says "fire Nurse" because the Sixers are 14-15 in December, save this article and send it back.*

### IF SIXERS LOSE GAME 7

> *Push a 56-win team to a Game 7 you should never have pushed past Game 4. With your MVP center recovering from emergency surgery and a rookie playing his first playoff minutes. The data point isn't the loss. It's that the series existed.*
>
> *If this is the floor of what Nurse can do with a 7-seed under those conditions, what's the ceiling with a healthy roster, a full offseason, and the same coaching staff?*
>
> *The Sixers' decision tree this offseason just got simpler: keep the coach. The roster is the question. Nurse is the answer.*

## Closing (~75 words, after the branch)

This team — Embiid (when healthy), Maxey, George, Edgecombe — is good enough to win games next year. The question was always whether the coach could pull a championship-level performance out of them when it mattered.

Saturday's result aside, that question got a much better answer this week than it had any right to.

Regular-season Nurse and playoff Nurse are not the same coach. Anybody still betting against the second one isn't reading the data.
