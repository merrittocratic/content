# I'm a MJ Guy, but...

Let me get this out of the way up front: in my mind, Michael Jordan is the greatest basketball player who ever lived. My first basketball memory is MJ hitting the elbow jumper over Georgetown to win the national championship for UNC in '82. I was hooked. Been a Jordan guy and a UNC guy ever since.

So, when I tell you that what LeBron James is doing right now is genuinely insane, I need you to understand that's not a hot take from a LeBron stan. That's a confession from someone who has been on the other side of the argument since the "Kid from Akron" entered the league.

The "kid" is now 41 years old, and averaging 25.3 points, 9.7 rebounds, and 8.7 assists in the 2026 playoffs. The Lakers are up 3-0 on Houston, one game from the second round. And he's doing it without Luka Dončić, the reigning scoring champion and the guy this team is actually built around.  Oh yeah, and Austin Reaves is out too.

LeBron is not the centerpiece of a championship roster, but what he's doing right now might be even more impressive.

---

## Take Two and Call Me in the Morning

Here's what I keep coming back to. Pull the top-two stars from every legitimate contender and ask yourself honestly whether they would make it ouf the first-round.

Take Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Chet Holmgren off Oklahoma City. Take Victor Wembanyama and De'Aaron Fox off San Antonio.  Take Jayson Tatum and Jaylen Brown off Boston.  I feel pretty confident saying none of those teams would be up 3-0 in their first-round matchups.  

And here's the one that really lands: take Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray off Denver.  I love Jokic!  Many consider him the most skilled offensive player alive. Without Jokic, the Nuggets aren't a first-round threat. And with him? They're currently *losing* to Minnesota in their first-round series.

No Luka. No Reaves. No problem.  The Lakers are one win away from the second round.  LeGM deserves some love.

## A List of One

I want to give you some historical context on 41-year-olds averaging 25 points per game in the NBA playoffs.  That list has one name on it.

The oldest player to even *score* in a playoff game was Robert Parish, who dropped 2 points for the Chicago Bulls at age 43. Not 2 points per game, 2 points **total**, as a ceremonial cap piece at the end of a career. That's the pre-LeBron data point on NBA elders in the postseason.

There is no historical comparison to make, because history didn't think to make room for this.

## Put some respect on his name...

I'm not here to relitigate the GOAT debate. MJ is still the GOAT to me and I don't think that changes. Six rings, six Finals MVPs, never lost in the Finals, that's a case that doesn't break.

But "who's the greatest" and "give this man his credit" are two different conversations, and we've spent so much energy on the first one that we've been stingy with the second.

LeBron James is doing something in 2026 that nobody has ever done, at an age when most players are either retired, doing analyst work on ESPN, or logging 8 ceremonial minutes a night. He is *the guy* on a playoff team, winning games in April, and right now he's doing it with his two best teammates watching from the sideline in street clothes.

That deserves some flowers.
