# There Can Be Only One...  Ball

The "last decision" is in and LeBron has chosen the city of brotherly "conditional" love.  Two years, $8 million to join a team that is going to spend an entire season figuring out their roles.  The front office didn't do anything wrong with this signing. They did almost everything right and that's the problem.

The fingerprints of this deal belong to Bob Myers. The same executive who built championship rosters in Golden State now has a strong voice in the roster construction of the 76ers. The 76ers first big swing sent Paul George, two first-round picks, and two second-rounders to Boston for Jaylen Brown.  It was the most shocking trade of the summer, which according to Rich Paul, "changed everything" for Philadelphia as a landing spot. That's not spin, that's the blueprint working as drawn up: trade for one Finals MVP, use him as the lure for a bigger one. 

The problem: nobody can trade for possession touches. The Sixers now roll out Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown, VJ Edgecombe, and James, four former All-Stars and a rookie already flashing star-level tools. Every one of them has, at some point, been the guy who the offense was run through. A basketball only needs one shooter at a time, and there are 24 seconds and just one rim to split five ways. This math doesn't care if the roster looks like an Eastern Conference All-Star poster.

## It Used to be Mad Love 

Miami just gave up four rotation players and a stack of future first-round picks to land Giannis. Pairing him with James would have closed a loop and ended a silent feud from over a decade ago when Lebron left South beach after Pat Riley built a roster that gave "the King" his first two rings.  In this iteration, James would have been the wise co-star orchestrating the offense and offering secondary shot creation, an ideal role for the 41-year-old.  

James signed for nearly the minimum-salary for someone with his service time. Miami is hard-capped at the $209 million first apron after using an expanded trade exception on Giannis, but the Heat still had roughly $18 million of room under that line right after the trade closed. So, Lebron would certainly have fit inside the cap with space to spare. The more honest explanation is priorities, not room:  Miami spent July locking up shooting depth around Giannis and Bam Adebayo rather than holding a roster spot open on a maybe. Cap space was a storyline, but certainty and roster construction were THE REAL stories.  

## I Didn't Get Dressed Up For Nothing

Maybe the Warriors did. Draymond Green declined a $27.7 million player option specifically to clear the room Golden State needed to chase James in free agency and land Anthony Davis in a trade. A clear attempt at building a Big Four around Steph before that era comes to a close. A decade removed from their Finals wars, the rivalry-turned-reunion story would have written itself.  If it all could have come together, a fifth ring for both Lebron and Steph would have been a reasonable expectation. Golden State did the work. James picked a different landing spot.  The cynic in me wonders if there was a little ring counting in that equation.  You can't pull ahead of Steph if you win one with him.

## You Can't Go Home Again... Again.

No math and no origin-story exaplanation neede, as James already delivered the franchise its only championship.  The current roster made the Eastern Conference Finals last spring without him, so he would have walked into a team already built, just needing an accelerant rather than a rescue. The shot-share tension would be smaller because Mobley doesn't need the ball to matter.  So, that's just two ball-dominant guards and a two-way big, not four or five stars all reaching for the same 30 shots a night.

## Not Always Sunny in Philadelphia
Philadelphia's front office built a roster that will win every press conference and highlight reel this fall. Whether it wins in April depends on something the front office can't trade for: Embiid, Maxey, Brown, and James regularly agreeing whose turn it is. Miami had a better positional fit. Golden State made the bigger sacrifice. Cleveland offered the cleanest math. Philadelphia offered the best press conference. Somebody's shot total is going to take real hit this season, and that's gonna play out live on the court, not in the front office war room.
