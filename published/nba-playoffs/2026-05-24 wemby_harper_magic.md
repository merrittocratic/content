# The Magic Was Already in the Building

---

A few weeks ago, we asked whether Victor Wembanyama had his Oscar. The conclusion was straightforward: De'Aaron Fox is a good player, Fox is not Oscar Robertson, and without that co-star, the Kareem precedent was going to be an uncomfortable ceiling on what this Spurs team could accomplish.

Then Game 1 of the Western Conference Finals happened without Fox.

Wembanyama played 49 minutes, scored 41 points, grabbed 24 rebounds, blocked 3 shots, hit a logo three to tie the game with 26.3 seconds left in the first overtime, and carried the San Antonio Spurs to a 122-115 double-overtime road win over the Oklahoma City Thunder. He joined Wilt Chamberlain as the only players to record 40 points and 20 rebounds in a Conference Finals debut. He is 22 years old.

The question we asked in April was: who is Wemby's co-star? The answer Game 1 offered was unexpected, and it came from a player we maybe should have been watching more carefully all along.

## His Name is Not Oscar

Dylan Harper is a rookie. A 19-year-old rookie, specifically, playing in his first Conference Finals in his first NBA season. Against the West's top seed, on the road, without the team's second-best player, he posted 24 points, 11 rebounds, 6 assists, and 7 steals.

That line is not just good. It's historically unusual. Harper became the first rookie since Magic Johnson in 1980 to record 20 points, 10 rebounds, 5 assists, and 5 steals in a playoff game.

Read that again. The last rookie to do that won the Finals MVP the same night he did it, starting at center as a point guard because Kareem's ankle gave out in Game 5.

We spent an entire article saying Fox is not Oscar and Fox is definitely not Magic. Nobody thought to check whether Magic was already on the roster.

## One Game Is Not a Series

The honest version of this is simple: one game proves nothing, and the Kareem precedent does not evaporate because a 19-year-old had a remarkable night.

Wemby still needs more than one Fox-less win to rewrite the history that says dominant young big men require a Hall of Fame co-star. The Spurs still have to win a series against a Thunder team that won the title last year and has home court. Harper still has 48 more minutes of Conference Finals basketball under his belt than he did a week ago, which is a small sample even by playoff standards.

What Game 1 did is sharpen the question. The original piece asked whether Wemby would ever find his Oscar or his Magic. The more interesting possibility now is that both are already in the building, except they're 22 and 19, and we're watching them become those players in real time rather than Wemby waiting for one to arrive.

That's a different story than the Kareem arc. Kareem got Oscar at 22 and Magic at 33. Wemby might be building with his co-stars from the start.

What we're watching now is not just whether the Spurs can beat Oklahoma City. It's whether the framework for understanding dominant young bigs needs to be updated entirely. Kareem's path required acquiring established greatness. Wemby might be on a path where the greatness is being drafted alongside him, and the only thing we were missing was patience.

Fox will be back. When he is, the Spurs will have their version of what we said they needed. But Game 1 raised the possibility that the most important co-star in San Antonio might not be the 28-year-old All-Star.

It might be the kid who just became the first rookie since Magic Johnson to put up that line in a playoff game.
