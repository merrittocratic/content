# I don't do sidekicks .... I am a solo act

---

A few weeks ago we asked whether Victor Wembanyama had a good enough running mate to contend for a championship in his first playoff run. The conclusion was blunt: De'Aaron Fox is good, Fox is not Oscar Robertson, and without the right co-star, the Kareem precedent was going to be an uncomfortable ceiling on what this Spurs team could become.

Two games into the Western Conference Finals, the answer has gotten more complicated and more interesting.

Game 1 without Fox: Wembanyama 41 points, 24 rebounds, a logo three to tie in overtime, Spurs win on the road in double overtime. Game 2 without Fox: OKC bounces back 122-113, SGA goes for 30, Stephon Castle has 9 turnovers, series tied 1-1 heading to San Antonio.

Same missing co-star. Two completely different outcomes. 

The real problem, who is going to step up to be a consistent Robin to Wemby's Batman?

## If you want something done right....

Can Wemby just go it alone, shoulder a bigger load, and carry the Spurs to a title?  While his numbers might seem elevated in the playoffs, The per-36 data doesn't actually support it.  Per-36 is where we normalize a players stats to a standard 36 minutes of playing time, which is a good barometer for an elite player.

His 2025-26 regular season per-36: 30.9 points, 14.2 rebounds, 3.8 blocks. 
His playoff per-36 across 12 games: 25.6 points, 14.2 rebounds, 4.6 blocks.

That's right, his scoring per 36 minutes is *down* five points in the playoffs.  While, his rebounding is identical, he theoretically has stepped it up on the defensive end by effectively averaging 1 more block per 36 minutes.  That stat is also a bit misleading because it includes a monster 12 block outlier in Game 1 of the T-Wolves series.  Underneath the counting stats is a player who is scoring less per minute in the playoffs and defending at the same elite level, possibly even better. 

But, the numbers also highlight the need for a reliable running mate, because someone needs to fill in the scoring deficit.

## Every Great Batman Had a Defined Robin

When Kareem was winning championships, he never had to wonder about his running mate. In Milwaukee, it was Oscar. In Los Angeles, it was Magic. The roster changed; the hierarchy didn't. Michael Jordan had Scottie Pippen for every ring. Shaq had Kobe, then D-Wade. The co-star role didn't rotate, it was settled, defined, and reliable before any of their playoff series ever started.

Wembanyama is doing his part. The problem is no other Spur is consistently staking their claim to be Wemby's Robin.  

Dylan Harper, 19 years old, first Conference Finals, starred in Game 1 vs. the Thunder, posting 24 points, 11 rebounds, 6 assists, and 7 steals. He became the first rookie since Magic Johnson in 1980 to record 20 points, 10 rebounds, 5 assists, and 5 steals in a playoff game. We've found our Robin!

Hold up, wait a minute....  Game 2 looked like a correction. 12 points, 2 rebounds, 3 assists and an injury that threatens his game 3 availability.  

Enter the Dragon, I mean Stephon Castle. Somehow he simultaneously made the case for and against himself in the same 40 minutes. 25 points on 59% shooting from the field and 8 assists.  Sure looks like a Robin to me.  But those pesky turnovers.  His 9 turnovers, the most by any Spur in a playoff game this postseason, resulted in nearly half of Oklahoma City's 27 points off turnovers in Game 2.

Between Harper's Game 1 and Castle's Game 2, the Spurs have now shown you two potential Robins in two games, and neither one could hold the role for more than 48 minutes.

## What does the Fox say...

Here's the question nobody can answer cleanly: when De'Aaron Fox returns, does that settle Wemby's Robin problem or add a third candidate to a rotation that already can't decide?

Fox is the veteran. When healthy, he's the unambiguous second option, the guy who takes the ball in the fourth quarter and makes something happen. But Fox hasn't played in this series. Harper and Castle have been auditioning for a role that was supposed to be Fox's, and one of them had a historically great game doing it. When Fox comes back, does he reclaim the co-star spot, does he slot in beside them, or does his return create more questions than it answers about who Wembanyama is actually supposed to be playing with?

Kareem never had that question. His Robin was always well-defined.

## What the Series Comes Down To

Wembanyama is 22 years old, playing in his first Conference Finals and trying to break a precedent of players making their first playoff run and coming short of a title. He is, by any honest measure, doing his part, but the series probably turns on a simple question: can one Robin claim the role and hold it long enough to give him the support he needs?

Game 3 is Friday in San Antonio. The Spurs have home court. If Harper plays like Game 1, the Spurs go up 2-1 and the Championship momentum becomes very real. If Castle plays like Game 2, both the 25-point version *and* the nine-turnover version, the Thunder probably like their chances of repeating. If Fox comes back, then none of us know what that will mean for the hierarchy.

Batman is locked in, but we're still waiting to find out who puts on the yellow cape.
