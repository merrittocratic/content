# The Form Rocket Problem

---

The PGA Championship preview conversation starts in one place: Scottie Scheffler vs. Rory McIlroy. The defending champion vs. the two-time defending Masters champion. That's the narrative on offer and it's not wrong exactly. They're the two best players in the world and one of them wins the simulation more often than not.

But there's a different conversation happening in my model right now. Two players whose form numbers are running so hot they're almost uncomfortable to look at. And both of them are peaking *this week.* At a different tournament.

## Wire to wire, and then some

Cameron Young just won the Cadillac Championship at Doral in wire-to-wire fashion. You saw that. Here's what the model adds: his recent form residual over the last eight events is running nearly two standard deviations above expected, the highest mark in this entire field. His trend line is positive, meaning it's not one hot week dragging up the average. He's been building toward this for a while, and Doral was the payoff.

The market has him around +1300 heading into the PGA. That's not longshot territory. It's also not "best form in the field" territory. Young is 26 with four Tour wins, so he's not some unknown quantity the odds-makers missed entirely. But the gap between where he's playing right now and where the number is feels real.

## The one who keeps almost winning

Ludvig Aberg doesn't have the marquee win Young just put up, which is why he's getting less noise. But his form trajectory might actually be the more interesting number. His trend slope is the steepest positive value in this entire field. He's not just playing well; he's still *accelerating.*

Four top-five finishes in five events is the track record. His form residual sits around 1.3 standard deviations above expected, second in the field behind Young. The market is offering him somewhere in the +1800 to +2000 range, which is where you land when the wins haven't materialized yet even though the ball-striking clearly has. At some point the outcomes start matching the performances. That window might be now.

## About that timing...

Here is the actual problem.

Both of these players are peak-form right now, this week, at Quail Hollow for the Truist Championship. The PGA Championship starts Thursday at Aronimink Golf Club in Newtown Square, Pennsylvania. Different course, different demands, one week from now.

Form is sticky in short windows. My model rolls on the last eight events, so it's not going to swing dramatically in a week. If Young and Aberg play well at Truist through Sunday and carry good vibes into Monday, the form signal holds going into Aronimink. That's the bull case.

The honest caveat: Aronimink is a different test than Quail Hollow. Long iron precision matters more there than raw distance profile. Course fit is a variable this model doesn't capture, and that's a real limitation worth naming rather than hiding. What I can say is that both players are carrying genuine form into the second major of the year. What I can't say is that it maps perfectly to a venue neither of them has a deep history at.

## Watch for Monday

If either Young or Aberg contends Sunday at Quail Hollow, they're walking into Aronimink with live momentum and a model that's already behind them. The market window to get ahead of that may not stay open long.

The Scheffler vs. Rory conversation will still be there. It always is.
