# You had one job, Just the one!

San Antonio is one game away from the NBA Finals, a place where they haven't been since 2014.

Two years ago they were 22-60. Any pre-season preview that had them as a Finals team probably did not originate outside of the borders of Texas.  And yet here we are on the precipice, and the Knicks are about to have a problem nobody was fully prepared to write about.

One thing to establish before the data: both teams playing in June are meaningfully different from the teams that squared off in December. Karl-Anthony Towns averaged 3.1 assists per game in the regular season. He is averaging 6.1 in these playoffs, almost double.  Since asking Mike Brown for a new role mid-first-round, KAT's playmaking has turned the offense into a juggernaut. For the Spurs, Dylan Harper wasn't a consistent contributing factor in December. He has been in the Conference Finals. The December games are just a foundation, not a final answer.

With that caveat noted, here is what we can build upon.

## When the KAT's away...

The Knicks and Spurs played twice this season, with New York winning the NBA Cup Final 124-113 in December. San Antonio took the regular season rematch 134-132 on New Year's Eve. In both games, KAT fouled out. The playmaker transformation does not change that fact, because the foul pattern predates it. Towns fouled out before he became the offensive hub. He fouled out before the Knicks were 7-and-0 every time he records five or more assists. He fouled out because San Antonio runs a screening-heavy offense designed to generate exactly the contact situations that accumulate for a big man who gambles on switches.

Towns is averaging 3.8 fouls per game this postseason. The Knicks are a different team when he sits. The half-court spacing that makes their offense functional compresses, and Brunson becomes far more ball-dominant at the end of a deep playoff run. San Antonio's coaching staff has a documented playbook that has worked twice. Whether they can execute it in a Finals series against a KAT who is now a more disciplined and deliberate player is the first variable to watch.

KAT has to stay on court in order to handle more playmaking duties and pull the "Alien" out of the paint with his three point shooting.  If he gets in foul trouble, and the Spurs can collapse the paint and close off Brunson's ability to score from the Mid-range and in, the inevitable result will be a short series.

## Defense is the Stronger form of waging war...

If you're a Knicks fan, and you think Brunson is just a slightly smaller version of SGA, then the words from von Clausewitz should make you a little nervous.

Castles are engineered primarily for defense, and Stephon Castle has been the primary defender on Shai Gilgeous-Alexander for all six games of the WCF. That assignment held regardless of score, quarter, or game situation. Over six games, Castle has held SGA to 39.2% shooting from the field -- genuinely elite point-of-attack defense against a two-time MVP. When you look at the game-by-game breakdown, a pattern emerges that goes beyond one player guarding another.

[TABLE IMAGE: wcf_castle_sga_pattern.png]

When Castle scored between 13 and 17 points, SGA scored between 15 and 24, shooting at or under 40% from the field. The Spurs won all three of those games. When Castle scored 24 or 25, SGA scored 30 or 32. The Spurs lost both of those games. The story here is not necessarily about Castle's scoring.  It's about how much energy he can conserve for playing elite defense on the opposing team's best player.  The third game of the series was confounded by lots of factors, Harper was limited to 17 minutes, and Fox was in his unsteady series debut.  

Game 5 is the number that explains the pattern most cleanly. SGA had 32 points on seven made field goals. He went to the line 17 times and made 16. Castle had scored 24 points on 64% shooting while also trying to guard the best foul-drawing guard in basketball. When Castle has to excel at both ends, SGA stops needing to beat him off the dribble. He just waits for the contact.

The pattern holds: when the Spurs have two reliable offensive contributors alongside Castle, the formula works. Game 1 was Wembanyama at 41 and Harper at 24. Game 4 was Wembanyama at 33 with Fox returning for a 12-point double-double. Game 6 was Wembanyama at 28 and Harper at 18 off the bench. In all three, Castle had one job and the Spurs won. When only Wembanyama was producing and Castle had to fill the gap on offense, SGA found a way. Oklahoma City won.

Harper's health is the variable that determines Castle's job count. Fox has been unreliable since his return. Wembanyama will not produce 33 points every night. What keeps Castle at one job is a functional Harper. Which makes the next question the most important one in a potential series with Knicks.

Can Harper stay healthy and productive enough to allow Castle to be what he's engineered to be? 

## Two players, each with one job

While there is more at play than these two factors, KAT staying on the court and out of foul trouble and Castle being allowed to lock in on defense are the two most highly correlated with winning for their respective teams.  

The Knicks are 7-and-0 when KAT records five or more assists this postseason. The Spurs are 3-and-0 in the WCF when Castle scores 17 or fewer. Both of those numbers are opponent-specific, both are fragile and both were not present for either of their previous matchups. 

What happened in December was a different sport, played under different rules. In June, KAT and Castle each have one job. Let's see who keeps theirs.
