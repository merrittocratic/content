# Dream The Impossible Dream

---

If the Oklahoma City Thunder get past the San Antonio Spurs, the NBA Finals will feature a matchup we've twice this season.  Both meetings were unique and highlighted what the series could look like. The regular season receipts tell a more complicated story than
"the defending champs will handle the team that hasn't been here since 1999."

OKC went 2-0 against New York. Both games were close and both ended with the Knicks on the wrong side of the scoreboard. Eerily Jalen Williams and Ajay Mitchell missed the first regular season matchup, and that is a real possibility again if this is the NBA Finals matchup.  The second regular season game was good-on-good with both teams at near full strength providing a slightly different data point.

## Do Two Days in March Matter in June

**March 4, MSG. OKC 103, NYK 100.**

They can if you consider some context.  Chet Holmgren walked into Madison Square Garden and dropped 28 points and eight rebounds, including a career-high-tying six 3-pointers. He had 14 points in the first quarter alone. The Knicks had no answer, and Oklahoma City built a 44-31 lead. 

Then OKC went cold for five minutes, but Holmgren bailed them out with two 3-pointers in the final 46 seconds of the first half to push it to 50-40 at the break.  So the man getting dominated by Wemby right now had 14 in the first quarter and two clutch threes to end the half.  He was everywhere.  SGA added another 26 points.  Karl-Anthony Towns (KAT) had 17 and 17, but fouled out in the fourth quarter. Brunson had 15 assists and 5-for-18 shooting. OKC held on for the three point road win without their second best player and Ajay Mitchell.

**March 29, OKC. Thunder 111, NYK 100.**

Brunson bounced back with 32 points on 13-for-22 shooting. KAT added 15 points and 18 rebounds. New York cut the deficit to one in the fourth quarter, but could never take the lead. SGA was having an off shooting night in the first three quarters, but then went 3-for-3 in the fourth with 10 points, icing a game the Thunder really had no business winning. SGA made 13 of 16 free throws and his streak of 20-point games reached 135. 

Two games, both won by OKC. Here's what that means for June.

## My Name is Chet

While it's not quite a greasy pork sandwich served on a dirty ashtray, the conventional pre-Finals read on Chet has been pretty grim.  Before his matchup with the "*Alien*", he averaged 19.0 points and 9.1 rebounds in the first seven playoff games. Since Wembanyama entered Chet's orbit, his scoring is down to 12.2 points on 21-for-41 shooting.  

But the big KAT and the "*Alien*" are two very different humans.  Against the Knicks, Holmgren averaged 22 points and 8.5 rebounds across two games, and he did it in two different roles: dominant stretch big against the zone (Game 1) and complementary piece when Shai asserted control (Game 2).  KAT is not a rim defender and likes to play out on the perimeter.  Holmgren torches that coverage from the elbow, the dunker spot, and the 3-point line. 

In a viral video, SGA struggled to answer how to get Chet more involved after game 4 against the Spurs, but that hesitation says more about Wemby than Chet. Against the Knicks, that problem doesn't apply the same way. Watch how quickly Holmgren's role expands the moment the defense stops being Wembanyama.

## Inside the Mind of the KAT

There is no doubt that the Knicks have found something by extending the playmaking ability of KAT.  The real question is can a KAT truly change his stripes?  The regular-season matchups keep raising this: KAT fouled out of Game 1 in the fourth quarter with the game on the line. OKC held on. That's not an anomaly; that's Oklahoma City's defense doing something intentional. The Thunder screen
aggressively and hunt mismatches, and Towns' tendency to reach on switches invites contact in ways that accumulate. In the playoffs, New York has been 9-0 when KAT dishes out five or more assists. The flip side is that he's also averaging 3.8 fouls per game this postseason.

A team with SGA and Holmgren has the personnel to run Towns into pick-and-roll situations that force him to choose between gambling for the steal and fouling. If OKC can get Towns into foul trouble in a Finals game, New York's offense fundamentally changes and  Brunson becomes significantly more ball dominant.  The exhaustion this creates is tough to overcome late in games as a small guard.  With the big KAT on the bench, the half-court spacing that's made New York's offense one of the best in playoff basketball suddenly compresses.

While the recent KAT assist narrative is real, so is the narrative about him picking up dumb fouls.

## The Injury Asterisk

This whole preview assumes a healthy OKC roster, and right now that assumption is doing a lot of work. Both Jalen Williams and Ajay Mitchell missed Games 4 and 5 of the Western Conference Finals with injuries. Williams, who scored 22 points on 7-for-11 shooting in the March 29 regular-season matchup, is OKC's pressure release valve.  With him, SGA doesn't have to the primary shot creator and playmaker on every possession.  Without him, Oklahoma City becomes, at best, a two-man show with a deep bench, which is a different proposition against a team that's been 11-0 since hitting its stride.

Mitchell's absence matters in a different way. New York's Mikal Bridges was averaging 15.6 points on 67% shooting and 44% from three coming out of his mid-playoffs slump. Bridges against a healthy, long OKC perimeter rotation is a different proposition than Bridges against a shorthanded one.

If the Thunder match up against the Knicks in the Finals, the injury report for Game 1 on June 3 will tell you more about this series than anything written between now and then.

## If I make it there....

New York is peaking at the right time. Oklahoma City might be banged up at the wrong one.

If everyone's healthy in June, bet the defending champs. If the injury report looks anything like it did in Games 4 and 5 of the West Finals, that calculus shifts. For the first time in 27 years, MSG is going to host an NBA Finals game.

Whatever happens after tip is anyone's guess.
