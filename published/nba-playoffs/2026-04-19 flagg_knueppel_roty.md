# Are We Being Konned?

---

ESPN's final Rookie of the Year straw poll came in 80-20. Cooper Flagg got the 20.

Let that land for a second. The consensus #1 pick. The biggest preseason ROY favorite in the history of the betting market. The first rookie since Michael Jordan to lead his team in points, rebounds, assists, AND steals. Twenty votes out of a hundred.

Everyone else went Kon Knueppel. I get the case, but when the other guy is mentioned with MJ as the only other "recent" player to do something amazing over a season, the conversation should be over.

## The case for Konn

It's real. Knueppel led the the entire NBA, not just rookies, in three-pointers made with 273. Rookie record. First rookie ever to lead the NBA in triples, period. He shot 43% from deep on volume we haven't seen from a first-year player since Steph Curry. He played 81 games. He helped drag Charlotte from 19 to 44 wins.

Durable, efficient, winning. The résumé voters reward.

And yet.

## It looks close, but is it really?



Stare at the total points row for a minute. Knueppel played ELEVEN more games than Flagg and logged 207 more minutes, and ended up with *only* a twenty-nine point edge. One decent scoring night covers his entire volume advantage.

Flagg leads every production category that doesn't coalesce around the 3-point line or require more games played. He became the youngest player in NBA history to score 50. Almost to prove a point, he did it again four days later. That isn't a counting stat. That's an event.

## Konntext matters

The usual pro-Konn defense is contextual. He plays for a real team, Flagg plays for a roster in freefall, team success matters.

Fine. But context cuts both ways. Flagg carried a 26-win Mavericks team that traded Luka last year and then traded his replacement this year.  What was billed as an ideal landing spot becasue Flagg wasn't supposed to carry the team as a rookie, became anything but. 

"Street Clothes" and Kyrie were exactly who we thought they were, and Flagg drew the top defensive assignment every night. Meanwhile, Knueppel was able to lean into his style and play off Ball, literally and figuratively, in an offense that asked him to spot up, move the ball, and take what came to him.

The nearest parallel is Wembanyama vs. Holmgren in 2024. Holmgren played 82 games on a 57-win Thunder team. Wemby played 71 on a 22-win Spurs team. Wemby swept all 99 first-place votes. The per-game alpha on the losing team won unanimously.

Same shape of race. Different answer. What changed?

## This ceiling mignt not be made of glass

Here is the real question, and somehow nobody is asking it: what are these guys going to be next year?

Knueppel is a 20-year-old who just shot 43% from three via a league-leading amount of makes in a real NBA offense. Legitimate calling card. Also roughly what he is.  Would anyone be surprised if Knueppel settles in as an 18 ppg player for the next decade?  A very good complementary piece, the third-best guy on a contender, the kind of sharpshooter who plays fifteen years and makes real money doing it. Great career. Not a franchise-tilter.

Flagg is a 19-year-old who just put up 21/7/5 on a team with no floor spacing and relied on him to be the primary creator. Put shooting around him. Drop in a secondary offensive engine so defenses can't load up on him every possession, and a leap to 25-27 ppg isn't optimistic, it's the expected outcome. The ceiling on a 6-9 forward who defends, passes, and can drop 51 against playoff-caliber defense isn't the ceiling of a player, it's the ceiling of the league itself.

One guy is likely approaching his ceiling, while the other is just starting to find his.

## Who do you want with the ball?

One more question, and it's the one Rookie of the Year should measure. It's late, it's tied, you need a bucket. Flagg or Knueppel, who do you want?

Nobody's honest answer is Knueppel. Not the voters, not the gamblers, and given how the play-in went not Charles Lee.

Tuesday in the play-in against Miami, Knueppel went 2-for-12 from the field, 0-for-6 from three, and watched crunch time from the bench while LaMelo dragged them to an overtime win. Friday in Orlando — 3-for-10, 1-for-6 from deep, 11 points in a 121-90 elimination that ended Charlotte's season and extended the longest active playoff drought in the NBA.

This should concern the voters who went 80-20 for Konn, because it's rare to see a consenus take go this bad, this fast.  The alpha of this year's class was Flagg. The per-game numbers say so. The degree-of-difficulty says so. The ceiling says so. And when the lights came on, the bench said so.

Don't be Konned.
