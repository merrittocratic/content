# La Masia Never Got These Kids

---

Every World Cup, some version of "why can't we compete in soccer" comes up in the media, and no one ever cites a specific reason.  Well, I'm not going to do that either!  At least, not with any data to back it up.  What can I say, I'm getting caught up in the World Cup mania!  I might even start randomly falling down and grabbing my knee if someone brushes up against me. 

The obvious answer to the question is that the athletic pipeline has a drain, and it runs toward a different sport. Every other country sends its fastest, most explosive, most coordinated kids toward a pitch at age eight. We send ours toward a football field instead of a futbol pitch and then spend thirty years wondering why we can't get out of the Group of Death. France isn't stumbling into Mbappés. Brazil isn't producing Neymars by accident. They find them young and run them through a system that takes twelve years to produce a world-class player.

So here's the thought experiment. What if the system found ours first?

What if Saquon Barkley grew up in São Paulo instead of Coplay, Pennsylvania? What if Justin Jefferson's first club was Olympique Lyonnais instead of a Louisiana travel team? What if at age nine, a Barcelona scout saw Jahmyr Gibbs and handed his parents a plane ticket?

The result is an XI that ends the conversation permanently.

---

## Formation: 4-3-3

**In goal: J.J. Watt**

Six-five, 285 pounds, he possesses a wingspan that closes the goal like drawing a curtain. In his prime, Watt batted down 20 passes from the line of scrimmage, which is the goalkeeper's entire job description. Combine that with his elite reactivity, and then run JJ through a German academy from age eight and you get the most terrifying goalkeeper in the world.

The only concern is he will sprint 30 yards from goal to challenge the striker himself. The coaching staff considers this a feature, not a bug.  

I know a thing about goalies.  I'm married to one!  You might have her to thank for this article...

**The back four: Surtain, Hamilton, Gonzalez, Stingley**

Here's the thing almost nobody says out loud: cornerbacks are already doing the soccer defender's job. Read the route, mirror the movement, maintain position, react to the ball, that is 1-on-1 defending at the highest level of any sport. Patrick Surtain II and Derrick Stingley Jr. were doing it against NFL wide receivers running 4.3 forties. Kyle Hamilton is 6'4", reads plays before they develop, and arrives exactly where the ball lands. Christian Gonzalez is the fourth member of this back line. He is, on this squad, a rotation player. Somewhere a European director of football is staring at this list and questioning his life choices.

**Midfield: Gibbs, Barkley, McCaffrey**

Running backs are the midfielders. This is non-negotiable. These are athletes who run full speed while reading a defense, catch passes out of the backfield, and change direction in a phone booth, all on the same play. Jahmyr Gibbs is one touch and gone before the defender processes what happened. Saquon Barkley covers ninety minutes at full sprint and looks annoyed when it's over. McCaffrey is the number 10, sees the gap before it opens, finds the pass nobody else saw coming. Put him through a La Masia academy starting at age seven and you have the most complete creative midfielder in the sport.

**Forwards: JSN, Chase, Jefferson**

This is where it becomes genuinely unfair. Justin Jefferson glides past defenders the way he glides past NFL corners, effortlessly, even with 20 extra pounds of "ice" around his neck. Ja'Marr Chase runs through arm tackles for a living; imagine him running at a 170-pound center back who can't use his hands. Jaxon Smith-Njigba is the technician, precise, intelligent, always in the right spot before the ball arrives. These three athletes spent their developmental years learning how to get open against the best coverage in the world. Twelve years of that same instinct pointed at a soccer pitch is a different kind of problem.

---

The rest of the world has been winning the futbol argument not because they have better athletes. They win it because their pipeline goes toward a pitch. 

I guess to actually make the World Cup interesting, it's a good thing we have a different pipeline.
