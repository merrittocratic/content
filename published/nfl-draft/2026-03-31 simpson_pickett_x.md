**1/8 — HOOK**
Daniel Jeremiah says he likes Ty Simpson more than Kenny Pickett.

Cool. Let's check the receipts.

🧵

**2/8 — THE COMPARISON**
Some low-start or one-year starting QBs drafted in Rd 1 since 2010 side by side (plus Kenny Pickett who started or played in 52 games!):

Newton: 51 total TDs, 1,473 rush yds, Heisman, natty ✓
Haskins: 54 total TDs, 4,831 pass yds, 28 records ✗
Pickett: 47 total TDs, ACC title, 52 career games ✗
Simpson: 30 total TDs, 93 rush yds, no major awards ???

Last in every column.

**3/8 — THE HASKINS PROBLEM**
Dwayne Haskins had the best one-year starting season in recent memory. Led the FBS in passing. 50 touchdowns. Set 28 school records. Heisman finalist.

Released by Washington in 2 years.

If *that* résumé couldn't overcome limited starts, what makes Simpson's lesser version more projectable?

**4/8 — 93 RUSHING YARDS**
The only low-start QB to make it in the NFL was 6-6, 250 lbs, rushed for 1,473 yards and 20 TDs, won a Heisman and a national title, and had a full JUCO championship season before his FBS year.

Simpson is 6-2, 208 and rushed for 93 yards all season.

The archetype isn't close.

**5/8 — THE TAPE GOT WORSE**
Simpson's first 9 games: ~67% completion, 21 TDs, 1 INT.
His last 6: 61%, 7 TDs, 4 INTs.

Defenses adjusted. He didn't adjust back. Haskins didn't have this problem and still busted. Pickett had 52 career games of tape to evaluate. Simpson has 15 starts — and a third of them trended the wrong direction.

**6/8 — THE SMARTER PLAY**
Need a developmental QB? Take one on Day 2–3, not Round 1.

Nussmeier (LSU) comes from the program that just recently produced Burrow and Daniels. Allar (Penn State) is 6-5, 240 with 3 years starting — first-round tools at a Day 3 price.

Both cost a fraction of the capital. Both have more experience. If you're wrong, you're out a 3rd-rounder, not a franchise pick.

**7/8 — FOLLOW THE MONEY**
Worth noting: Simpson's most vocal media advocate shares agency representation with him through CAA. That doesn't make the evaluation wrong — but it's context you should have.

Meanwhile, the "projects better than Pickett" claim has zero statistical support. Zero.

**8/8 — TAKEAWAY**
Nobody's calling Simpson a bust. But drafting him in Round 1 means betting that a 6-2, 208-lb pocket passer with 93 rushing yards and 30 total TDs breaks a pattern that's only been broken once — by a 6-6, 250-lb Heisman-winning national champion with 51 TDs and a JUCO title.

That's not analysis. That's hope.
