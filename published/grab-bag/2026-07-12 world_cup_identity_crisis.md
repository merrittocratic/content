# Am I a Soccer Guy Now?

I've watched four soccer matches in my entire life, all in the last three weeks. No childhood rec league, no fantasy Prem team, no opinion on whether five center-backs is a real formation or a war crime. Just two articles in a row breaking Merrittocracy rules: 1. Never going to write about the WNBA, darn you Caitlin! 2. Writing on vibes and not data.  The whole premise of this brand is that flying by feel is how you crash.  

Identity crisis, table for one.

## Get to the Choppa!

Erling Haaland is the reason. He moves like a predator, slow and nearly invisible for long stretches, then the ball finds him and it's over before the defense finishes processing he moved at all. Seven goals in five games, Norway's first quarterfinal ever and first World Cup since 1998, on a 39% conversion rate nobody's matched since Gary Lineker in 1986. He's averaging a goal every fourteen touches, the fewest touches-per-goal in sixty years of World Cup scoring, and one brace was enough to send five-time champion Brazil home in their earliest exit since 1990. He plays like he's rationing effort on purpose, because he is.

## Where's Our Freak?

Here's what actually nagged at me: this tournament is co-hosted on American soil, in front of the deepest athletic pipeline on the planet, and the U.S. doesn't have anybody playing Haaland's position the way Haaland plays it. Real question, not a hot take, I've got no model here, just a guy who flew helicopters for twenty years wondering why we can't get at least one freak athlete skilled enough to make a difference on the world stage. Somewhere there's an alternate-universe Haaland who grew up in Ohio, who would normally play tight end and marry Taylor Swift, that actually plays soccer for the good 'ol U.S. of A!

## Pulisic..., Pulisic...  Is this thing on?

Somebody made a specific, checkable claim, so let's check it. Christian Pulisic was "effectively a no-show".  One goal in eight career World Cup appearances, the 2022 winner against Iran, scored on a hip injury that knocked him out for the second half. This tournament, on home soil with "Captain America" riding on him, he played four of USA's five games, started three, and didn't find the net.  His ending, forced off with a leg injury as Belgium closed the door 4-1 in the round of 16. "No-show" or "Quiet at the worst possible time", take you pick of labels.  Neither are great and they get worse when you compare them to other stars of the sport.  

## Appointment Viewing

Messi and Mbappé are tied at eight goals apiece for the Golden Boot, and Messi just became the outright leading World Cup scorer in history, 21 goals across six tournaments at 39 years old, on a goal near stoppage-time that dragged Argentina back from two down against Egypt. Ronaldo, his rival for two decades, is already out at 41, Portugal beaten by Spain, in what he'd called his last World Cup.  Ronaldo also accomplished something he had never done, scoring a goal in a Knockout game.  He went out on his sword in 1-0 loss to what might be the eventual World Cup winner in Spain.  Haaland sits one goal behind the leaders at seven, Kane one behind him at six. Three players scoring seven-plus in a single World Cup has never happened before this year, and right now Messi's Argentina and Haaland's Norway are both playing win-or-go-home quarterfinals today, a few hours apart. No simulation needed to know that's good television.  

Again, that paragraph should be brutal for a Pulisic fan to read.

## So, Am I a Soccer Guy Now?

I don't know yet. I know I've picked a favorite predator, I know I'll call out a bad stat on sight no matter the sport, and I know flying blind isn't always spatial disorientation. Sometimes it's just a guy on a couch, four games deep, having a great time. Ask me again after the final.
