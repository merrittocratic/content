# Am I a Soccer Guy Now?

I've watched four soccer matches in my entire life, all in the last three weeks. No childhood rec league, no fantasy Prem team, no opinion on whether five center-backs is a real formation or a war crime. Everything else on this Substack runs on a training set; this one's running on pure vibes -- and the whole premise of this brand is that flying by feel is how you crash. Instruments dark, having the best sports summer of my adult life. Occupational identity crisis, table for one.

## The Predator Thing

Erling Haaland is the reason. He moves like a predator -- slow and nearly invisible for long stretches, then the ball finds him and it's over before the defense finishes processing he moved at all. Seven goals in five games, Norway's first quarterfinal ever and first World Cup since 1998, on a 39% conversion rate nobody's matched since Gary Lineker in 1986. He's averaging a goal every fourteen touches, the fewest touches-per-goal in sixty years of World Cup scoring, and one brace was enough to send five-time champion Brazil home in their earliest exit since 1990. He plays like he's rationing effort on purpose, because he is.

## Where's Our Guy?

Here's what actually nagged at me: this tournament is co-hosted on American soil, in front of the deepest athletic pipeline on the planet, and the U.S. doesn't have anybody playing Haaland's position the way Haaland plays it. Real question, not a hot take -- I've got no model here, just a guy who flew helicopters for twenty years wondering where all our freak athletes went. My honest, unmodeled guess: they went to the NFL and the NBA, because that's where this country built the incentive structure to send them. Somewhere there's an alternate-universe Haaland who grew up in Ohio and plays tight end now. The one take in this piece I won't defend with a regression.

## Let's Check the Receipts

Somebody made a specific, checkable claim, so let's check it. I said Christian Pulisic was "effectively a no-show" and guessed he hadn't scored in two World Cups across nine games. Close, not quite -- the real number is one goal in eight career World Cup appearances, the 2022 winner against Iran, scored on a hip injury that knocked him out for the second half. This tournament, on home soil with "Captain America" riding on him, he played four of USA's five games, started three, and didn't find the net -- then his tournament ended early anyway, forced off with a leg injury as Belgium closed the door 4-1 in the round of 16. "No-show" isn't fair to the guy who's scored the only goal that's ever mattered for this program at a World Cup. "Quiet at the worst possible time" is the more honest label, and it's not a great one either.

## Appointment Television, No Model Required

Messi and Mbappé are tied at eight goals apiece for the Golden Boot, and Messi just became the outright leading World Cup scorer in history -- 21 goals across six tournaments at 39 years old, on a stoppage-time hat trick that dragged Argentina back from two down against Egypt. Ronaldo, his rival for two decades, is already out at 41, Portugal beaten by Spain, in what he'd called his last World Cup. Haaland sits one behind at seven, Kane one behind him at six. Three players scoring seven-plus in a single World Cup has never happened before this year, and right now Messi's Argentina and Haaland's Norway are both playing win-or-go-home quarterfinals today, a few hours apart. No simulation needed to know that's good television.

## So, Am I a Soccer Guy Now?

I don't know yet. I know I've picked a favorite predator, I know I'll correct a bad stat on sight no matter the sport, and I know flying blind isn't always spatial disorientation. Sometimes it's just a guy on a couch, four games deep, having a great time. Ask me again after the final.
