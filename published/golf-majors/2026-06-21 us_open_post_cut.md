# Tale of Two Tee Times

The preview flagged a meaningful Thursday wave advantage for the AM starters based on afternoon winds expected to gust up to 40 mph.  The USGA also saw this, and set up the course accordingly.  Then reality set in, the wind laid down, and the PM wave took advantage.

For a course the USGA treats as a proving ground for playing in wind, the scoring gap between the waves was the conditions story hiding in the leaderboard.

Saturday is setting up to be a similar story. The AM wave faces 18+ mph sustained winds at tee time. Clark, Schauffele, and Fitzpatrick draw 11-13 mph. Scheffler and McIlroy, who both need to make a moving day charge face 15.2 mph. Russell Henley, Cameron Young, and Jordan Spieth are back in the teeth of it, so don't expect many early charges on Saturday.

## Should we call him Wind-am Clark?

Lockers are exhaling a sigh of relief.  Wyndham Clark has caught fire with the putter and Shinnecock is handing him an opening. While the hot putter narrative is not wrong, it's also not the whole story.

Clark leads the field at -7 through two rounds on the strength of two independent vectors: approach play at +4.84 SG (#5 in the field) and putting at +4.67 SG (#7 in the field). Round 1 was all about the putter, +3.98 with the flat stick.  Round 2 looked like the correction that usually ends these kinds of heaters: the putter cooled to +0.69, but fortunately for Clark's locker, the approach game held its form at +2.00, and he still gained 3.25 strokes on the field.   

Going into Thursday, the model had him 19th by predicted SG and 15th in win probability at 1.6%, a legitimate pre-tournament bet, but he's outperforming even that reasonably bullish profile. Win probability after two rounds: 34.2%, more than three times the next closest player. The model's R3 projection has him 21st in the field by expected strokes gained. That's regression to the mean working as designed, but the lead is already banked and Clark just needs to hang on.  Easier said than done at Shinnecock.  Look out lockers...

## The Challengers

Schauffele had the most impressive Friday in the field. His R2 of +6.25 SG moved him to 11.0% win probability, and the model has him fifth in projected R3 SG at +0.928. The pre-tournament case for Schauffele was straightforward: elite talent, elite prior, due for a clean week. He's finally having one.

Fitzpatrick was one of the opening seven in the preview and the model's seventh-ranked player going in at +1.39 predicted SG, based on a strong 3 win year. Through 36 holes he's at +8.56 total SG and T2, but the profile contrasts Clark's. Approach (+4.46, #6) and around the green (+2.79, #4) are carrying him. Off-the-tee is -1.35, 129th in the field. He's not winning across the bag, he's surviving a bad driver on the strength of his irons and short game. A 2022 US Open champion doing it without his driver is a more fragile position than the leaderboard suggests. At Shinnecock, the rough collects eventually. He knows what the last day looks like. The question is whether the driver lets him see it.

Morikawa is the sleeper the preview built a case for:  Shinnecock is an iron-play course and his approach game is the best in this field. Round 1 was modest at +0.31 SG. Round 2 ELITE was +7.25 SG.   

Scheffler remains the model's #1 pick by projected R3 SG at +1.843 and 6.6% to win. He'd need Clark to stall and the best 18 holes he's played all year. Scottie Scheffler doing Scottie Scheffler things are always possible.
