# Donald Ross, Meet the Prior Anchor

---

Maverick McNealy and Alex Smalley are leading the PGA Championship.

Go ahead and sit with that for a second.

The pre-tournament narrative at Aronimink ran roughly as follows: Scottie Scheffler would play Scheffler golf, Rory McIlroy would either seize his Grand Slam moment or flame out dramatically, and the rest of the field would sort itself into the usual collection of elite players and credentialed almost-wases. Friday's pin placements — described by Scheffler himself as the hardest he's ever faced anywhere on Tour — had other ideas. The course gave the conventional wisdom a 73.1 scoring-average reality check and handed the halfway lead to two guys the consensus had largely ignored.

The model had them both in the top 20. That's not a brag. It's a breadcrumb.

---

## What the Model Said

Before Thursday, I flagged two names: **Ludvig Aberg** (form mean +1.34, steepest positive slope in the field) and **Tyrrell Hatton** (form mean +1.47, 129 events of history). The case for both was genuine hot-hand signal going into a major that punishes everyone who doesn't earn it.

One of them is in contention. One of them is watching the weekend from his hotel room.

**Hatton missed the cut.** The preview noted that his career prior was slightly negative (-0.18), meaning the entire model case for him was form-driven — a player running meaningfully above his historical baseline at exactly the right time. When Aronimink got brutal on Friday, the prior reasserted itself. The model knew the form was real. It also told you, in the same breath, that his long-run baseline said otherwise. That tension didn't resolve in his favor.

**Aberg**, on the other hand, has been one of the best ball-strikers in the field through 36 holes. His round-two strokes-gained number (6.61 relative to the field) is among the highest of the week. On a day the field averaged more than three over par, he was deep in the red. The model still has him 17th heading into Moving Day.

That gap — one of the best 36-hole performances in the field, ranked 17th by the model — is not a bug. It's the prior anchor problem I wrote about before Thursday, now visible in real numbers. Aberg has 66 events of history. His career baseline is still developing, and it's slightly negative. The model sees what he's doing this week and applies heavy regression to the mean anyway. On some level it's correct — career baselines don't evaporate in two rounds. On another level it's watching a player shoot himself into contention and filing it under "probably an outlier."

This is exactly what's going into the Shinnecamp roadmap.

---

## The Best Round Nobody's Talking About

**Chris Gotterup** shot a 65 on Friday. That's the lowest round of the tournament. He's tied for third. The model has him 28th.

Gotterup's prior is slightly negative (-0.124), his form is solid but not elite, and the model looked at one of the best rounds of the week and shrugged. If the Aberg situation is the prior anchor problem in its readable form, Gotterup is the same problem with the volume turned up. He's not a player the model is rooting against. It just doesn't know what to make of him yet, and when models don't know what to make of someone, they push them toward the middle.

---

## The Call That's Looking Good

Here's something the model actually got right: **Rory McIlroy** went four over par on Thursday. He was projected to miss the cut. The model kept him at number two in the projected rankings because his career skill prior (1.682, highest in the field) said the Thursday round was an outlier, not a trend.

He shot 67 on Friday.

Regression to the mean is an ugly phrase until it works. McIlroy is now in the field for the weekend. He's six back of the leaders and has some real ground to make up, and this is where the model's honest limitation shows its face: it predicts next-round performance, not tournament position. The deep hole from Thursday doesn't disappear. It compounds. A cumulative projection — one that adds actual rounds to projected rounds — is on the list for Shinnecamp.

---

## The Weekend Acid Test

**Xander Schauffele** had the best Thursday of anyone near the top of the leaderboard. Friday was a different story. He gave shots back on a brutal afternoon and sits several strokes off the pace heading into Moving Day.

The model has him fourth overall — entirely on the strength of a 1.265 skill prior from a player who won here a year ago. It's holding faith in the defending champion's elite baseline through a rough round the same way it held faith in McIlroy's. One of those calls is going to look smart by Sunday evening.

---

## On Reitan, DeChambeau, and Fleetwood

**Kristoffer Reitan** made the cut. The model has him 36th, still weighed down by a career prior of -3.965 despite running the hottest form in the field. He won two weeks ago. The model continues to disagree politely. Two tournaments in, the prior-versus-form tension is now a documented pattern, not a footnote.

**Bryson DeChambeau** missed the cut for the second straight major. The model projected him to bounce back in round two — and he might have, locally. But the model predicts rounds, not tournaments. A catastrophic Thursday followed by a solid Friday is still a missed cut. That's the limitation in its most concrete form.



---

## What Happens Saturday

The cut line settled at three over par. McNealy and Smalley lead at four under, with six players within a stroke. Fifty-eight players are within six of the lead, reportedly the most in PGA Championship history at the halfway point. Scheffler, Young, Thomas, and Kim are two back in a group that includes the world number one. McIlroy is six back but alive.

Aronimink is a par 70 with no bailout holes. Donald Ross didn't believe in par fives, and he really didn't believe in letting anyone off the hook. Whatever Moving Day produces, the course will have a vote.

The model will be watching. So will I.
