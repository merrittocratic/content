# Seven Favorites. Then It Gets Fun.

---

The model has 156 opinions about what happens this week at Shinnecock Hills. Most of them agree with the market, but there are some players that are more interesting than others.

No surprises for Scheffler, McIlroy, Rahm, Schauffele, Fleetwood, Cantlay, Fitzpatrick: top 10 by strokes gained and top 10 by win probability, no dispute. When two independent lenses produce the same names, you take them seriously. The consensus exists because those seven are genuinely better than the rest of this field, and both lists know it.


---

## Living Off Reputation

One thing the completed data makes visible: McIlroy's form mean coming in is +0.217, the lowest of anyone on this favorites list, and it's not close.  Fleetwood, ranked fifth by the model, is at +0.508. The market has McIlroy at 7.4% win probability because he's Rory McIlroy, and his career prior is high enough that +0.217 above his baseline is still a strong number in absolute terms.

But if you're looking for value within the consensus group itself, Fleetwood at 3.0% with hotter recent form and a form mean more than double McIlroy's is worth a second look. Same tier of player. Less than half the win probability. The market is pricing the name on the back of the jersey, not what the last eight events say.

## Putt for show, Irons for Dough?

Morikawa is at 2.5% win probability with a strokes-gained number of +1.111, outside the model's SG top 10 but inside the win probability top 10. The gap asks: what does the win model see that the raw SG rank doesn't?

It's actually two things.

First, he finds fairways at 69.8% against a field average of 60.4%, one of the most accurate drivers in this field. At 292 yards off the tee, he's essentially average, but at Shinnecock where the rough is brutal and the par-4s stretch long, short grass matters more than a few extra yards. The win model likes that.

Second, his approach game is +0.775 SG App over his last 30 rounds, the best in this field. Shinnecock is an iron-play course at heart. That's his entire identity.

The quiet vulnerability: the putter is leaking at −0.086. In a birdie-fest, survivable. In a U.S. Open grind where you're hitting long irons from the fairway all week at even par, that's where it becomes a problem. The model has him at 2.5% to win because the approach game is genuinely elite enough to compensate. Whether Shinnecock agrees with that logic across four rounds is the bet.

## The Other Guys

I'm a peacock, you gotta let me fly!

**Cameron Young** (SG rank 16, 1.3% win, form mean +1.97) is running the hottest form of any player in this group, and the win model is effectively ignoring him. The driving numbers explain the discount: +0.134 SG OTT at 300 yards, 53.6% accuracy. That's the McIlroy profile, length without precision, and at Shinnecock that's a real exposure. The model sees the ball-striking and puts him 16th. The win model sees the accuracy and dials back the ceiling. If he's finding fairways this week, 1.3% is criminally cheap.

**Chris Gotterup** (SG rank 37, 0.9% win, form mean +1.09, slope flat) is the driving number that stops the conversation: +0.697 SG OTT at 313 yards, 60.4% accuracy. That's Scheffler-level production off the tee. He's been consistently above his baseline, the form isn't fading, and 0.9% is the price the market is offering for a player gaining at that rate off the tee on a course that rewards exactly that. The PGA Championship preview flagged him and he was in contention through 36 holes. He knows how to handle a U.S. Open setup.

**Maverick McNealy** (SG rank 26, 0.8% win, form mean +0.976) is the quiet case. Stanford product who's been on the bubble for years and looks like something clicked recently. At +0.054 SG OTT, 295 yards, 59.8% accuracy, accurate but not a bomber, which limits his ceiling but keeps him out of trouble in the rough. 0.8% with a top-30 SG projection and rising form feels overlooked. He led Aronimink through 36 holes. He knows what this looks like.

## Mama, there goes that man again

If the consensus seven are the no-doubters and the three above are the value names, Reitan is where both lists get it wrong.

SG rank: 56. Win probability: 0.4%. Driving strokes gained: +0.967 at 308 yards. That is Scheffler-level production off the tee from a player the model barely notices. Form mean +1.596, slope +0.501, the second-fastest-rising trajectory in the entire field.

We ranked him 97th at Aronimink. He finished in contention. The prior problem is still partially in play: he doesn't have enough elite-event history for the career anchor to trust what his form is screaming. The form is real anyway.

The caveat is real too: 55.6% driving accuracy. His draw is favorable going off with one of the earlier tee times of Thursday.

0.4% is a price, not a pick. On a week where 308 yards and a hot hand can outrun a thin résumé, it might be less wrong than it looks.

We missed him once. Making the same mistake twice is a different kind of problem.
