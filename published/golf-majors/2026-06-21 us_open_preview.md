## The Model Sees It. The Market Doesn't.

On the SG top 10 but not the win probability top 10: **Niemann, Justin Thomas, DeChambeau**. High ball-striking numbers, legitimate Shinnecock profiles on paper. The model sees the raw strokes-gained production and puts them in the company of the consensus core.

The win probability model disagrees. Something — tail risk, course variance, historical distribution — is dragging their championship odds below what the SG rank implies. DeChambeau in particular has the ball-striking history to contend at any major; whatever the model sees in his win distribution is worth watching as the week unfolds.

## The Market Sees It. The Model Doesn't.

On the win probability top 10 but not the SG top 10: **Morikawa, Hatton, Si Woo Kim**. The market is pricing something the raw strokes-gained number hasn't captured.

The most interesting case is Morikawa. He's at 2.5% win probability with a strokes-gained number of +1.111 — that's not a small gap. Something in his distribution is working in his favor, and if you know Morikawa's game, you can guess what it is: Shinnecock rewards precise approach play and elite putting. His entire career is built on those two things. The win probability model isn't wrong to like him. The question is whether you trust a course-fit signal over a raw SG rank when the course is this specific.

Hatton showed up in the PGA Championship preview and missed the cut — the prior reasserted itself when Aronimink got hard. He's back in the win probability list here anyway. Si Woo Kim getting a bump without the corresponding SG rank is the one to watch; that's either a genuine course-fit signal or noise that burns out by Friday afternoon.

## The Draw Is Already a Story

Before any of this matters, the morning/afternoon split matters.

Today's AM wave: misty start, winds climbing to 16mph out of the NNW by 9am — the direction Shinnecock is specifically designed to punish. This afternoon: sunny, 9mph variable. Different golf course, same 18 holes. Classic U.S. Open draw split where the lottery can be worth four strokes before anyone hits a ball.

The model doesn't know which wave each player drew. The scorecard will.

## The Off-the-Wall Pick

If the consensus seven are the no-doubters, and the divergences are where the model and market separate — Reitan is where both of them get it wrong.

SG rank: 56. Win probability: 0.4%. Driving strokes gained: +0.967 at 308 yards. That is Scheffler territory in one specific category. Form mean over the last eight events: +1.596. Slope: +0.501, the second-fastest-rising trajectory in the entire field.

We missed him at Aronimink. He ranked 97th and finished in contention anyway. The prior problem is still partially in play — he doesn't have enough elite-event history for the model to fully trust what his form is signaling, so the career anchor drags him down the list. The form is real. The prior hasn't caught up.

The caveat: 55.6% accuracy off the tee. In morning winds at Shinnecock, that number gets tested immediately. If he draws afternoon, the upside is genuine. If he draws morning, the rough settles the question by the fourth hole.

0.4% is not a recommendation. It's a price. The question is whether it's less wrong than it looks on a week where 308 yards and a hot hand can outrun a thin résumé.

We missed him once. Making the same mistake twice is a different kind of problem.
