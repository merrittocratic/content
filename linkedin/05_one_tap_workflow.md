Every piece of content that leaves my publication passes through three buttons on my phone: Skip, As-is, or Edited.

Behind those buttons is Earnest, the OpenClaw agent I introduced a couple of posts back. During live golf rounds he monitors scoring and fires heater alerts. The rest of the time he watches a tiered map of sports media personalities on X and drafts replies in house voice when something analytically substantive drops.

The tiers are a strategy, not a follower list. Tier 1 gets checked every 30 minutes from 5am to 10pm ET and splits three ways: the destination platforms, the credibility layer where the track record gets built (PFF, The Ringer, No Laying Up), and breaking news. Tier 2 is beat reporters and analytics accounts on a three-hour cycle.

When Shams reported the Austin Reaves max deal, the draft waiting in Telegram wasn't a news summary. It was a take about how finding second-creation guards outside the draft is what keeps expensive contention windows alive. That's the bar: the system surfaces the moment and drafts the argument, then I tap.

The number that matters most is the cap: eight surfaced candidates a day. That's the discipline separating a publishing operation from a reply-guy account.

This matters beyond sports. One-person media operations rarely die because the analysis degrades. They die because the operational load compounds until the posting cadence drops. Earnest handles the present. The model handles the receipts. The human handles the argument.

And the goal from post one still stands, in public: two years, The Herd.
