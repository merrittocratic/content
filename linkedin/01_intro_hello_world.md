Fifteen minutes before Wyndham Clark eagled the par-5 5th at Shinnecock Hills, my phone buzzed.

An automation agent named Earnest had flagged Clark's early strokes-gained rate as a 90th-percentile heater, and routed the alert to my phone with enough context to make a content call in under 30 seconds. Clark eagled the hole. A few days later he won the US Open. The system was in position before the moment instead of narrating it after everyone else already had.

Hello, LinkedIn. I'm Steve Merritt. I flew H-60s in the Navy for 20 years, pivoted to data science, and this spring launched The Merrittocracy: a sports analytics publication built on the premise that consensus narratives should have to survive contact with the data.

What started as one NFL draft model (boom/bust probabilities, roughly 3,750 players across 15 draft classes) has turned into a small operation over the summer. A live win-probability stack for the golf majors with post-mortem calibration audits after every event. Narrative checks through the NBA playoffs. And Earnest, an OpenClaw agent on a Mac Mini who monitors, drafts, and alerts, but is not allowed to publish a single word without human approval.

Over the next few weeks I'll break each piece down here: the draft model call the league played out on cue (Caleb Downs, pick 11), how Earnest is built, what the golf model got wrong this summer (Brier scores included), and the one-tap workflow that keeps a one-person publication present on X without becoming a reply-guy operation.

One goal, stated publicly, because accountability shouldn't stop at the brand strategy: within two years, I want to be a guest on The Herd. Either it happens or it didn't, and the receipts will be public either way.
